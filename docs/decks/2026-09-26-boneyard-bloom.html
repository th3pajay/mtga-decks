Overview

Renew is an activated ability that lives in the graveyard: pay the cost, exile the card, put counters on a creature, “activate only as a sorcery.” The mechanic is a one-shot resource, so the deck is built to (a) put Renew creatures into the graveyard early and (b) convert those buried cards into a single overwhelming attacker.

Two cards turn that conversion into real card flow rather than a one-for-one trade. Kheru Goldkeeper makes a Treasure every time one or more cards leave your graveyard during your turn, and Kishla Skimmer draws a card the same way (once per turn). Every Renew activation, and every Molt Tender mana activation, therefore leaves a Treasure or a card behind. The deck digs its own engine out of the graveyard, then finishes with evasion plus a land that makes the attacker unblockable.

Approach

Game plan phases

Turns 1-3 — stock the boneyard. Molt Tender on turn one mills and later fixes mana by exiling graveyard cards. Alchemist’s Assistant and Rot-Curse Rakshasa put early points of damage on the table (the Rakshasa is a 5/5 trampler for two mana, decayed, so it is a one-shot attacker that leaves a Renew card in the graveyard). Sage of the Fang and Champion of Dusan arrive on turn three as bodies that later become ammunition.

Turns 4-6 — build the pile. Lasyd Prowler mills a chunk of the library on entry, which both fills the graveyard with Renew creatures and loads lands into the graveyard for its own Renew count. Bitter Triumph and Long Goodbye keep the board manageable while Bitter Triumph’s discard cost drops the first Renew creature into the graveyard for free.

The combo turn — pre-combat main phase. Pick one attacker (Kheru Goldkeeper or Qarsi Revenant for natural evasion), then chain Renew activations in the correct order: everything that adds counters first, Sage of the Fang last so its “double the number of +1/+1 counters” multiplies the finished total. Every activation triggers Kheru Goldkeeper (Treasure) and one of them triggers Kishla Skimmer (draw). Then attack.

Average nonland mana value is 2.4, with 25 lands. Green does the heavy lifting, black supplies the removal and the discard engine, and blue is a light splash for Kishla Skimmer and Naga Fleshcrafter. Molt Tender’s “exile a card from your graveyard: add one mana of any color” and Kheru Goldkeeper’s Treasures both smooth out three-color draws.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with Molt Tender when you have it; milling a Renew creature into the graveyard is worth more than the 1/1 body. Rot-Curse Rakshasa is the fastest pressure in the deck and a perfectly acceptable turn-two play, because it is never meant to survive: attack, trade or sacrifice it, and the Renew ability is online in the graveyard. Hold Bitter Triumph until it can also bin a stuck Renew creature as its additional cost.

Mid game

Deploy two or three bodies and stop over-committing. Lasyd Prowler’s entry mill is the biggest single graveyard-filling play in the deck, and everything it mills makes its own later Renew bigger. Kishla Skimmer is the priority land beyond the first few turns: it converts the exile costs you are already paying into cards. Kheru Goldkeeper is the one four-drop worth tapping out for, because the Treasure it makes pays the next Renew activation.

The combo turn

Sequence matters. In your pre-combat main phase:

  1. Confirm the target. Kheru Goldkeeper, Qarsi Revenant or a Rakshasa with a counter already on it are the best homes.
  2. Activate the keyword Renews first: Alchemist’s Assistant ({1}{B}, lifelink counter), Champion of Dusan ({1}{G}, +1/+1 and trample counters), Qarsi Revenant ({2}{B}, flying plus deathtouch plus lifelink counters).
  3. Activate Kheru Goldkeeper’s Renew ({2}{B}{G}{U}) for two +1/+1 counters and a flying counter if you have the mana.
  4. Activate Sage of the Fang’s Renew ({3}{G}) last: one more counter, then double the total.
  5. Activate Rogue’s Passage ({4}, {T}) on the same creature, then attack. Deathtouch plus trample plus unblockable plus a doubled counter total is the kill; lifelink buys the turn back if the attack is not lethal.

Each activation exiles a card from the graveyard, so Kheru Goldkeeper adds a Treasure and Kishla Skimmer adds a card on the way through.

Win condition 2 — the copy turn

Naga Fleshcrafter enters as a copy of any creature on the battlefield, then its Renew ({2}{U}) puts a +1/+1 counter on a target nonlegendary creature you control and makes every other creature you control a copy of that creature until end of turn. Pick a fat printed body — Lasyd Prowler (5/5) or Rot-Curse Rakshasa (5/5 trample) — and attack with the whole board as copies of it. The copies get the printed body, not the counters, so choose the target for its base stats. Copies of the Rakshasa are decayed and will be sacrificed at end of combat, which is irrelevant when the swing is lethal.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with three lands and a two-drop, or two lands that include Molt Tender. Ideal keeps have 3-4 lands, one or two Renew creatures, and one engine card (Kishla Skimmer, Lasyd Prowler or Kheru Goldkeeper). Send back hands that are all four-drops, hands with no black source and no Molt Tender, and one-land hands. Do not keep a hand that needs to reach four mana and has no way to get there.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Sage of the Fang’s doubling is a multiplier, so it must be the last activation of the chain. Doubling before adding the other counters wastes the effect.
  • Renew is sorcery speed only, meaning pre-combat main phase is the whole window. Plan the whole chain before activating the first ability.
  • Every Renew activation exiles a card, so the abilities are finite. Count the graveyard before starting a chain and keep one activation in reserve if the attack might not be lethal.
  • Rot-Curse Rakshasa’s Renew ({X}{B}{B}) puts decayed counters on any number of target creatures. Used on defending creatures it removes their ability to block for the rest of the game, and any that attack are sacrificed at end of combat. It is a legitimate way to clear the way for the alpha strike.
  • Molt Tender’s second ability exiles a card from the graveyard for mana, which also triggers Kheru Goldkeeper and Kishla Skimmer. It is a mana source that advances the engine.
  • Bitter Triumph’s discard cost is upside: discard a Renew creature that is stuck in hand to destroy a creature or planeswalker.
  • Maximum Overdrive grants indestructible, so it doubles as protection for the stacked attacker in response to removal, and it adds a counter that Sage of the Fang can later double.
  • Naga Fleshcrafter’s copy is not a triggered ability you can chain for value: the copies lose the copied creature’s counters, so it is a one-turn finisher rather than a value engine.

Matchups

Aggro. Alchemist’s Assistant and the lifelink counters from the Renew package are the stabilisers, and Bitter Triumph and Long Goodbye trade up. Racing is viable because Rot-Curse Rakshasa attacks for five on turn two.

Control. Long Goodbye cannot be countered, and Kishla Skimmer plus Kheru Goldkeeper grind card advantage out of graveyard exiles. Do not spend the whole graveyard into a sweeper: keep two Renew creatures in reserve and rebuild.

Midrange. Counter stacking wins the creature fight. Sage of the Fang’s doubling outscales anything fair, and Rogue’s Passage walks past the biggest blocker.

Combo. There is no stack interaction, so the plan is speed plus discard: Bitter Triumph to rip a piece and Rot-Curse Rakshasa’s decayed counters to stop the creatures that matter.
