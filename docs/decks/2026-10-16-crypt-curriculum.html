Overview

Surveil is the set’s graveyard mechanic and this deck runs it as an engine, not as card selection. Every surveil does three jobs at once: it grows Denzilore Fatehold’s team, it counts down the seven cards Eye of Jace and threshold spells ask for, and it feeds Dark Matter Manipulator, which gains +2/+0 for every seven cards in your graveyard.

Engine piece Copies What it does
Eye of Jace 4 Upkeep surveil 1 every turn, then cashes itself in for 2 damage to each opponent once the graveyard holds seven or more cards
Proctor of Potential 4 Surveils on every creature entering, including itself; later returns from the graveyard for two mana if you surveilled this turn
Surveillance Phantasm 4 A 2/3 flying blocker that can attack on any turn you surveilled
Yuriko, Hope from the Shadows 4 One-mana flash surveil 2, or a removal spell scaling off the graveyard count
Seasoned Cryomancer 4 Draws two and discards two, filling the graveyard and tapping attackers down with stun counters
Liliana the Repentant 2 Mills two on every creature or planeswalker entering, and rebuys one card from the graveyard

The deck is not a fragile two-card combo. It is a midrange pile where sixteen cards surveil or reward surveilling, and the payoffs are all counting the same resource.

Approach

Plan by phases:

  1. Turns 1 and 2: deploy the engine. Eye of Jace and Yuriko are one mana, Proctor of Potential and Surveillance Phantasm are two. Every creature that enters triggers another surveil, so the graveyard crosses seven cards quickly without spending a card to do it.
  2. Turns 3 and 4: start cashing in. Seasoned Cryomancer refills the hand, Sphinx of False Conclusions lands on defence at instant speed, and Denzilore Fatehold converts every surveil trigger from that point on into a +1/+1 counter on the whole board.
  3. Turns 5 and up: finish with an evasive board, Eye of Jace triggers, and Restless Reef attacking as a 4/4 deathtouch Shark. Uldaros Theorix is the reset button that rebuys the best card of each type out of the graveyard for free.

Twenty-four lands, seventeen blue sources, eleven black and eight white. Twelve of the spells cost one or two mana, so the deck keeps deploying while it mills; the curve tops out at a single six-drop, which means the only card that can rot in hand is Uldaros Theorix.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Open on Eye of Jace and Yuriko. Eye of Jace is the perfect one-drop here: it surveils every upkeep on its own, so the graveyard count climbs whether or not you draw anything else. Yuriko’s surveil 2 mode is almost always the right choice on turn one; the minus-X mode is a real removal spell later, when the graveyard count is what decides a block. Proctor of Potential is the two-drop that makes the engine stick: it surveils on every creature entering, so a hand with two or three cheap bodies turns into six or seven cards in the graveyard by turn four.

Mid game

From turn three the deck stops paying for its graveyard and starts spending it. Seasoned Cryomancer is the turn-three play that keeps the plan moving: draw two, discard two, then tap the opponent’s best attackers and put stun counters on them, which is how this deck survives aggro without removal. Denzilore Fatehold at instant speed is the real payoff, and once it is on the battlefield every surveil trigger is a team-wide pump, so Eye of Jace’s upkeep, Proctor of Potential’s entry trigger and the Restless Reef attack all become combat math the opponent cannot profitably block.

The combo turn

The deck’s best turn is a self-mill loop into a reanimation. Surveil once to satisfy Proctor of Potential, then pay two mana to return it to the battlefield from the graveyard. It enters, which surveils again, which pays for the next Proctor, and each of those entries is another +1/+1 counter on the team with Denzilore Fatehold out. Two Proctors in the graveyard plus five mana is two surveil triggers, two counters on the whole board, and two extra cards filtered into the yard, all in one turn, and the same loop turns on threshold for Proft Sinister Mastermind and the seven-card clause on Eye of Jace. Use the loop before combat; the counters land on the attackers you are about to use.

Win condition two

The second win is a drain, not a combat step. Every copy of Eye of Jace that reaches an upkeep with seven or more cards in the graveyard sacrifices itself for 2 damage to each opponent and 2 life for you, so four copies are eight damage to the face spread over four turns and eight points of life buffer. Dark Matter Manipulator turns the same graveyard count into a body: it is a one-drop that grows by +2/+0 for every seven cards in the graveyard, so a game that goes long turns a one-mana 1/2 into a lethal attacker. When the drain plan is not quite enough, Uldaros Theorix rebuys a creature and a spell out of the graveyard and casts them for free, and Denzilore Fatehold is the usual creature half of that.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands with at least one blue source and one engine card, ideally Eye of Jace or Yuriko. Hands with a surveil engine plus a payoff are keeps even without a plan for turn two, because the deck’s engine pays out on its own triggers. Be careful with hands that have no black or white source at all: Proft Sinister Mastermind, Denzilore Fatehold and Proctor of Potential are all locked behind a second colour. Ship hands of five lands, hands with Uldaros Theorix and no early plays, and hands with Restless Reef as the only blue source, because it always enters tapped.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Eye of Jace sacrifices itself when the trigger resolves, so it is a one-shot. Do not treat it as a permanent; four copies are four separate drains, and they stage over the game.
  • Denzilore Fatehold triggers on each scry or surveil, not each card. A single Surveil 2 that puts two cards into the graveyard is still one +1/+1 counter on each creature.
  • Proctor of Potential’s recursion needs you to have scried or surveilled this turn, and the returned body carries a finality counter, so it is exiled instead of dying. Return it in the turn you are attacking, not as a blocker.
  • Proft Sinister Mastermind cannot be cast at all until the graveyard holds seven or more cards. Treat it as a turn-four-plus card, and note that its discard ability is a real removal spell on any turn the seven-card clause is live.
  • Seasoned Cryomancer’s tap-and-stun clause only triggers if you discard at least one nonland card. When the hand is all lands, the discard is a blank; that is the correct time to mill into a spell with Sphinx’s Approach instead.
  • Sphinx’s Approach can be cast from the graveyard only in the sense that it can be milled into value for threshold. With three copies, the exile-five-copies clause can never turn on; the spell is here as an instant-speed draw-two that a mill-heavy turn makes cheap in card terms.
  • Dark Matter Manipulator counts every seven cards in the graveyard, both players included, and it counts card types only for Tarmogoyf-style effects, not here: it is a flat +2/+0 per seven cards, so it is a 3/2 at seven cards, a 5/2 at fourteen.
  • Uldaros Theorix casts copies of permanents as tokens. Exiling a creature and a sorcery from the graveyard and casting both is legitimate as long as the total mana value is six or less.

Matchups

Against aggro, the plan is to race with Drain and to blank attackers with Seasoned Cryomancer’s stun counters and Surveillance Phantasm’s 2/3 flying body; this is the deck’s tightest matchup, because there is no removal spell in the list. Against midrange it is a favourite: Denzilore Fatehold grows the board faster than most decks can trade, and the Proctor loop converts spare mana into counters. Against control, keep Eye of Jace for the drain and hold Sphinx of False Conclusions for flash blocks, and expect to win slowly through the drain plus the Recursion from Liliana the Repentant and Uldaros Theorix after a wipe. Against combo, the deck has no interaction beyond tapping attackers down, so it must count on Denzilore Fatehold plus fliers to close before the opponent goes off.
