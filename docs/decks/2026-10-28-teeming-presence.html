Teeming Presence

Naya creature-count combo: a token swarm that casts its own spells for free — Reality Fracture

Overview

Reality Fracture’s green half is built to make bodies. Omnipresence ({5}{G}{G}{G} mythic enchantment) is the card that turns those bodies into mana-free spells, and it reads exactly as it sounds: you may cast spells with mana value less than or equal to the number of creatures you control from your hand without paying their mana costs.

That single line changes what the deck wants to do. Every creature is simultaneously a threat and a mana source, so the plan is not to curve out but to reach a critical mass of creatures and then empty your hand for zero mana. Six creatures means every three-drop and four-drop is free. Seven creatures means Verdant Kraken ({4}{G}{G}{G}, 6/6) and Face Yourself ({5}{R}{R}) cost nothing. Ten creatures means Emrakul, the Exigent Doom ({10}, 12/12 flying trample) arrives for free and untaps every land you control on the way in.

The deck’s cheap half is chosen so that the count climbs while the mana develops at the same time:

  • Simulacrum Shaper ({1}{G}{G}, 2/2) fetches a basic land onto the battlefield and draws a card when it dies.
  • Greenhouse Propagator ({2}{G}, 2/3) taps for {G} and gains a life on every creature entry, which is what makes Germinate Recruits ({2}{W} instant) worth a slot: it creates X 2/2 Cadets where X is the life gained this turn.
  • Loot, the Nexus ({2}{G}, legendary 2/1) is the mana engine: {T} adds one mana of any colour for each different power among creatures you control. Two powers is a two-mana land, five powers is a ritual.
  • Jiang Yanggu, Never Alone ({3}{G}) brings Mowu, a legendary 3/3, and untaps every token at end of turn so the swarm can block after attacking.
  • Hexhaven Invigorator ({G}{G}{G}{G}, 6/6 vigilance) fetches a land for every point of damage it takes, so a chump block or a sweeper trigger turns into three or four extra lands.

The payoff spells are deliberately the most expensive cards in the deck, because those are the ones Omnipresence makes free: Face Yourself, Verdant Kraken, Garruk, Curse Breaker, Ghalta, Emrakul.

Approach

Game plan phases

Ramp 1-4 Land, then Simulacrum Shaper or Greenhouse Propagator, then Loot, the Nexus or Pia, Aether Ascetic. Loot is the priority: every extra power on the board is another mana.
Build the count 3-6 Jiang Yanggu for two bodies, Germinate Recruits for a burst of Cadets, Garruk’s -3 for a 4/4 Beast. Six creatures is the number that matters.
Convert 6+ Omnipresence. From that point the hand is free: every card with mana value at or below the creature count costs nothing. Face Yourself copies the board, Verdant Kraken adds a land creature every upkeep, Emrakul ends the game.

Average mana value is 4.74 across 34 non-land cards, which is why the deck runs 26 lands and six pieces of ramp. The coloured weight is overwhelmingly green: 50 green pips against 4 red and 3 white, with 18 green sources, 7 white and 7 red. The red and white cards are not there to be cast off Mountains and Plains in the early game, they are there to be cast for free once Omnipresence is online.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one to three are about mana that also builds a board. Simulacrum Shaper is the best turn-two play: it ramps, it blocks, and it replaces itself. Greenhouse Propagator is the follow-up, because it both taps for {G} and starts the life total that Germinate Recruits converts into Cadets. Loot, the Nexus is the card that decides how fast the deck plays: with a Cadet (power 2) and Mowu (power 3) on the board it already taps for two mana of any colour, and once a 4/4 Beast, a 6/6 Kraken or an 8/8 Ghalta joins, it is a four or five mana land.

Do not trade Simulacrum Shaper for nothing. Its death trigger is the deck’s cheapest card draw, and chumping with it is often better than chumping with a token that Loot counts.

Mid game

The mid game is the creature count. Six creatures is the number to aim at, because six is where every three-drop and four-drop in hand becomes free. Jiang Yanggu puts two bodies on the board at once (and Mowu is a third distinct power for Loot), Germinate Recruits turns a Propagator-heavy board into three or four Cadets at instant speed, and Garruk’s -3 adds a 4/4 Beast that also draws a card off his static ability.

Garruk’s +2 is easy to underrate: untapping two lands with Loot in play is a second Loot activation, and untapping two lands with eight lands in play is half a turn of mana.

The combo turn

Omnipresence is the turn the deck stops paying for spells:

  • Resolve Omnipresence with six or more creatures on the board. Check the count first: the free-cast clause uses the creature count at the moment you cast, not at the moment the enchantment resolved.
  • Cast everything in hand with mana value at or below that count, in the order that grows the board: Germinate Recruits and Jiang Yanggu first (more creatures), then Garruk, then Verdant Kraken.
  • Verdant Kraken makes a 3/3 Forest Tentacle at the beginning of each player’s upkeep, so the count grows on your opponent’s turn as well.
  • At seven or more creatures, Face Yourself is free. Target yourself: you get a hasty token copy of every creature you control, including the copies’ own power, and the whole board attacks.
  • With Garruk on the battlefield the copies are not sacrificed at the end step, because Face Yourself only demands a planeswalker. Without him they still attack once, which is usually enough.

Two Face Yourselfs in one turn is the ceiling: the first copy doubles the board, the second copies the doubled board.

Win condition 2

Emrakul is the second finish and it does not need Omnipresence. The card’s own exile ability costs {3}: exile it from hand, target a land, and that land taps for {C}{C} until Emrakul is cast from exile. On a 26-land manabase that is a two-mana land on turn four, which is the difference between a turn eight Emrakul and a turn eleven one. When it resolves, the cast trigger untaps every land you control, so the second main phase is fully funded.

Ghalta the Unstoppable is the cheaper version of the same plan: at 8 power on the board it costs {G}, and it gives every other creature trample, which is what makes a board of Cadets lethal through blockers.

Hall of Echoes is the third conversion. Five mana and the land becomes a copy of your best creature until end of turn, with the legend rule suspended, so it copies Emrakul next to the real Emrakul, or copies Verdant Kraken to double the token production. It taps for {C}, so it never pays a green pip; treat it as a spell that happens to be a land.

Mulligan guide

Keep: three or four lands with Simulacrum Shaper or Greenhouse Propagator; hands with Loot, the Nexus plus two cheap creatures (Loot is the deck’s mana); hands with Pia, Aether Ascetic and at least one enchantment payoff to find.

Mulligan: two-land hands where one land is Hall of Echoes or Roiling Canopy (both are effectively a land behind); hands with no green source; hands that are all five-drops and above with no ramp.

The deck mulligans more aggressively than a midrange deck because it needs both halves: a ramp creature and something to ramp into. A six-card hand with three lands, a Shaper and two payoffs is better than a seven-card hand of lands and expensive spells.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Omnipresence only covers spells cast from hand. Flashback, adventure and graveyard casts are not free.
  • Casting for free means paying nothing, so an X spell cast off Omnipresence has X equal to zero. Germinate Recruits is therefore the one card in the deck that must be cast for its own {2}{W} before Omnipresence lands, not after.
  • Face Yourself copies are sacrificed at the beginning of the end step if you don’t control a planeswalker. Garruk is the only planeswalker in the deck, and he is worth holding until after the swing for exactly that reason.
  • Loot, the Nexus counts distinct power values, not creatures. Two 2/2 Cadets add one mana, not two; a 2/1 Loot, a 3/3 Mowu, a 4/4 Beast, a 6/6 Kraken and an 8/8 Ghalta add five.
  • Garruk’s static ability draws on creatures with power 4 or greater entering, which includes Face Yourself copies of Kraken, Invigorator, Ghalta and Emrakul.
  • Hexhaven Invigorator’s trigger is damage, not combat damage. A sweeper that deals three to it fetches three lands.
  • Roiling Canopy enters tapped and needs five other Forests to pump, so it is a late-game land, never a keep.
  • Hall of Echoes and Roiling Canopy both produce colourless or green only; the deck’s white and red cards are almost always cast for free.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Fast aggro Block with Hexhaven Invigorator (6/6 vigilance) and Mowu, and use Germinate Recruits at instant speed as a fog. The deck stabilises around turn five and then wins with a single Face Yourself swing.
Control Omnipresence is an enchantment, so most counterspell suites cannot answer it once it lands; Pia tutors for it and the deck has three. Bait a counter with Verdant Kraken, then land the enchantment.
Combo The deck is slow, so race with Ghalta and the Kraken tokens, and use Hall of Echoes to add a second big attacker rather than trying to interact.
Ramp and big mana Both decks go over the top, but this one goes over the top for free: Emrakul plus Face Yourself beats any fair board, and Roiling Canopy’s pump wins the stalled ground.
