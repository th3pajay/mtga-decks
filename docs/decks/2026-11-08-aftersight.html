Overview

Reality Fracture’s surveil package is not a reanimator shell here. It is a trigger engine. Four cards pay you for looking at the top of your library: Proft, Consulting Detective converts each scry or surveil into a counter and a card for {2}; Denzilore Fatehold converts it into a counter on every creature you control; Prudent Fateseer converts it into a team power pump; Diviner of Victory converts it into a permanent +1/+1 on itself.

The enablers are cheap and redundant: Proctor of Potential surveils on its own entry and on every creature that follows, Snarling Gorehound surveils whenever a small creature enters, Faerie Dreamthief and Eye of Jace surveil on entry or upkeep, three of the lands surveil as they enter, and Diresight and Found Footage surveil twice at a time. Because Proctor also returns itself from the graveyard for {W}{U} as long as you have scried or surveilled that turn, the engine rebuilds after a sweeper.

The two prepared creatures are the set’s payload: Prudent Fateseer enters prepared and can cast Peer Review (a 2/2 Cadet plus surveil 1) for free, and Diviner of Victory enters prepared and can cast Unwind History (bounce a cheap creature plus surveil 1) for free. Each free spell is both a removal or board piece and another surveil trigger, which is why they sit above the curve here.

The deck wins by attacking. It does not need a single big spell to close: it needs two or three surveil events in a turn while Denzilore Fatehold and Proft are on the battlefield, and the attack that follows is one turn lethal.

One practical note: Reality Fracture is Standard-legal but the set still carries no arena_id in the card data, so the import list becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there. The non-set support cards in the list are already on Arena.

Approach

Game plan by phase

Turns 1 to 3, build the lookout. Land, one-drop engine, second engine. Faerie Dreamthief and Snarling Gorehound are the preferred openers because they surveil without spending mana. Proctor of Potential is the priority two-drop: it surveils on entry and then on every creature after it. Diresight on turn three is a fine play when you have no engine, but it is better held until a payoff is out.

Turns 4 to 6, stack payoffs. Proft first, then Denzilore Fatehold, then Prudent Fateseer. Do not tap out for Denzilore if the board is empty of creatures: it needs a board to pump. Peer Review from a prepared Prudent Fateseer is the ideal follow-up, because the Cadet enters (Proctor surveils), and the surveil triggers Proft, Denzilore and Fateseer on the same turn.

Turn 6 and later, close or loop. Either attack with a board that grew two counters this turn, or cast Uldaros Theorix and convert a stocked graveyard into free spells. Hall of Echoes turns the last five mana of a stalled board into a second attacker.

Curve and mana

Mana value 1 2 3 4 6
Spells 15 5 11 3 1

Average mana value is 2.17 across 35 spells with 25 lands. The deck is deliberately land-heavy for a low curve: it needs four lands on turn four to hold up two payoffs and a surveil spell in one turn, and the goldfish checks put four lands on turn four at roughly 75 percent. Colour demands are light in pips (11 blue, 10 white, 16 black) but the shocks matter for turn-one black and turn-two white-blue. Never keep a hand that cannot produce black by turn two or white-blue by turn three.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a one-drop that surveils on its own. Faerie Dreamthief into Proctor of Potential is the strongest two-card opening: the Faerie surveils on entry, then Proctor surveils again, so Proft and Denzilore have already paid off by the time they land. Against aggro, Blessed Ghoul is the better one-drop: lifelink plus a {2}{W/B} return from the graveyard buys the turns the engine needs.

Do not crack Found Footage early unless you are short on lands or need to enable a Proctor recurrence in the same turn. Two mana for a surveil 2 and a card is fine on turn three; it is excellent on the turn you deploy Proft.

Mid game and the combo turn

The engine turn looks like this: Proctor of Potential and Proft on the battlefield, Denzilore Fatehold just resolved, then a creature spell. The creature enters, Proctor surveils, Proft takes the {2} and grows while drawing a card, Denzilore puts a counter on every creature, and Prudent Fateseer adds +1/+0 to the team. Casting Peer Review from a prepared Fateseer does all of that and adds a 2/2 body that itself triggers Proctor. That single turn is usually four to six additional power plus a card.

Order of operations matters inside the turn. Resolve the surveil trigger first, decide whether the card goes to the graveyard, and only then decide whether to pay for Proft: Proft draws after the surveil, so you can bin a land and still draw a spell. If you have two payoffs and only two mana, Proft is almost always the right choice over holding mana up, because the deck has no instants worth representing other than the free Unwind History.

Win condition 2

Uldaros Theorix is the long-game out. He wants a graveyard with one creature, one sorcery, one artifact and one land so that his enters trigger has the widest choice. Exiling Diresight, Found Footage, a spent Eye of Jace and a dead Proctor typically lets you cast two or three copies for free in the same turn, and the permanent copies become tokens. Hall of Echoes copying Uldaros the turn he attacks gives you two 5/5 fliers, which is usually the whole combat step once the rest of the board is counted.

Mulligan

Keep hands with two or three lands and at least two surveil sources. A hand of lands, payoffs and no engine is a mulligan even if the payoffs are rares: Proft and Denzilore do nothing without triggers. Six-card hands are fine when they contain Proctor of Potential plus a one-drop that surveils on entry. Seven-card hands with 25 lands should almost never need a land mulligan, so a mulligan usually means the hand is short on engines, not on lands.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Proctor of Potential’s recursion requires that you have scried or surveilled this turn. A land that surveils on entry (Meticulous Archive, Undercity Sewers, Shadowy Backstreet) counts, so a land drop can turn on the {W}{U} activation. The returned Proctor carries a finality counter, so it cannot be recurred twice.
  • Eye of Jace checks the graveyard after its own surveil. With two surveils a turn it reaches seven cards around turn four or five, and the sacrifice deals 2 damage to each opponent and gains 2 life. That is a genuine clock in stalls, not just a loot engine.
  • Snarling Gorehound only triggers on creatures with power 2 or less entering. Cadet tokens, Blessed Ghoul, Diviner of Victory and Faerie Dreamthief all qualify. Proft stops qualifying once he has a counter, so make Gorehound triggers before you grow Proft if you have the choice.
  • Denzilore Fatehold is the card most often stranded in hand: {1}{W}{U}{U} is the strictest cost in the deck. Cast it only when you can also surveil that turn, otherwise a 3/4 flier that does nothing is a bad use of four mana.
  • Uldaros Theorix copies spells, so copies of permanent spells enter as tokens. If your graveyard holds no sorcery, the trigger still works, it just gets fewer cards.
  • Hall of Echoes adds only {C} and does not become a creature itself. It copies a creature you control until end of turn, so it attacks in the same turn, and the legend-rule exemption means copying a legendary creature is safe.
