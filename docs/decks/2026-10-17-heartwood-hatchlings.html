Overview

The set’s prepared mechanic is a free spell stapled to a body: a prepared creature lets you cast a copy of its back-face spell, and doing so unprepares it. The green-red half of that cycle all shares the same back face, Soul Tether, which makes a Heartwood token — a red and green artifact that taps for red or green. Six cards in the deck are Soul Tether creatures, so the deck’s first job is to keep turning bodies into mana rocks.

The second job is what makes it a combo rather than a ramp deck. Draconic Visitor replaces every artifact token you create with a 5/5 red Dragon with flying. Once it is on the battlefield, Soul Tether stops making mana and starts making Dragons, Saheeli’s Thopters arrive as Dragons, and a single upkeep with Woodwork Prodigy prepared is a free 5/5 flier every turn.

Approach

Plan by phases:

  1. Turns 1 and 2: deploy prepared bodies. Heartwood Crafter on one, then Konstrari Improviser or a second Crafter. Do not cash the Soul Tether yet unless the mana is spare; the token is worth more once there is something to convert.
  2. Turns 3 and 4: build the rock count. Woodwork Prodigy and Tenured Tethermage put two to four Heartwoods on the battlefield, which is both ramp and the fuel for the combo turn. Karn, Gilded Guardian refills the hand off the colours among those artifacts.
  3. Turns 5 and up: land Draconic Visitor and convert. From that point every Soul Tether, every Saheeli trigger and every Aerid Konstrari death is a 5/5 flier. Craftwork Crusher and Hall of Echoes close games that stall on the ground.

Twenty-four lands: fourteen green sources, eight blue and six red, plus every Heartwood token, which taps for red or green. Karn, Gilded Guardian is counted at two because its hybrid cost is payable with two generic mana. Twelve cards cost one or two, so the deck is never short of an early play, and the two seven-drops are the only cards that can sit dead in an opening hand.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Heartwood Crafter is the ideal one-drop: it enters prepared, blocks well enough as a 1/1, and its mana ability pays for the Soul Tether it is holding. Turn two is Konstrari Improviser, or a second Crafter plus Artifist Acumen to dig. The important discipline in the first three turns is not to fire Soul Tether into an empty board. A Heartwood token with nothing to convert is a 2/2-for-three rate; the same token on the turn Draconic Visitor lands is a 5/5 flier. Fire the tethers when the mana is otherwise going unused, or when a Tenured Tethermage or Woodwork Prodigy is about to make the board wide enough that the extra artifact count matters.

Mid game

Woodwork Prodigy is the engine’s centre. It re-prepares during every upkeep, so from turn three it offers a Soul Tether each turn without spending a card. With Geist of Saint Thalia out, that copy costs two mana total and can be paid with a Heartwood plus one land. Tenured Tethermage converts a spare land into two tapped Heartwoods, which is the fastest way to reach five mana for Draconic Visitor while keeping the board intact. Karn, Gilded Guardian is the mid-game refill: a Heartwood is two colours, so Karn usually draws two cards on entry and leaves a 5/5 vigilance trampler behind.

The combo turn

The deck’s best turn is the one where Draconic Visitor arrives on a board that already holds prepared bodies and artifacts. Order it like this: cast Draconic Visitor, then cast every Soul Tether you have queued. Each copy now creates a 5/5 red Dragon with flying instead of a Heartwood. Woodwork Prodigy re-prepares at the next upkeep, which is another Dragon. Saheeli, Jewel of Avishkar converts each of those noncreature spells into a Thopter that also arrives as a Dragon, and Puppet Crafting on the Echoverse Fulcrum gives the board a 5/5 Construct that survives a creature-only sweeper. Two prepared bodies plus Saheeli is three Dragons in one turn off six or seven mana.

Win condition two

When the air plan is answered, the deck wins on the ground with artifact bodies. Craftwork Crusher is a 7/5 trampler that removes a creature or planeswalker and draws a card when it enters, Hungering Puppetbeast eats spare Heartwoods for +1/+1 counters and a keyword of choice, and Aerid Konstrari pumps itself by the number of artifacts you control. Hall of Echoes is the land half of this plan: five mana turns it into a copy of the biggest creature on the board, so the Crusher attacks as two 7/5 tramplers and Draconic Visitor can be doubled for a second conversion effect in the same turn.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands with at least one green source, one prepared creature, and one payoff — Draconic Visitor, Karn, Gilded Guardian or Craftwork Crusher. A hand of Heartwood Crafter into Konstrari Improviser with no five-drop is still a keep, because Woodwork Prodigy and Tenured Tethermage are common enough that the deck finds its converter by turn five. Ship hands with no green source, hands whose only lands are Hall of Echoes and Konstrari Annex, and hands with two or more Draconic Visitor and no early creature.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Prepared only re-arms through the card’s own text or an outside effect. Heartwood Crafter and Konstrari Improviser enter prepared once; after their Soul Tether resolves they are vanilla bodies. Woodwork Prodigy is the only card here that re-prepares every turn, which is why it is a four-of.
  • Draconic Visitor replaces the creation, it does not copy the token. Soul Tether under the Visitor makes one 5/5 Dragon and no Heartwood, so the mana rock is gone. Sequence ramp first, conversion second.
  • Saheeli, Jewel of Avishkar triggers on casting, and Soul Tether copies are sorceries, so the Thopter arrives before the Soul Tether resolves. With the Visitor out that Thopter is a 5/5 Dragon.
  • Tenured Tethermage’s land sacrifice is optional and it is a real cost: only take it with four or more lands, or when the two Heartwoods immediately pay for a five-drop.
  • Karn, Gilded Guardian counts colours among other artifacts you control. A Heartwood is both red and green, so one Heartwood is already two cards; the deck has no other multicoloured artifact, so expect two, not five.
  • The Echoverse Fulcrum’s five-mana ability destroys all creatures, Dragons included. It is a reset button for the turn the opponent has committed more than you, not a removal spell.
  • Puppet Crafting can enchant any non-Aura artifact, so a Heartwood token is a legal target and stays a mana rock while it is a 5/5. Enchanting the Echoverse Fulcrum makes the sweeper body-safe.
  • Hall of Echoes taps for colourless only. Two copies are utility, not mana fixing; do not count them as green or red sources when keeping a hand.
  • Craftwork Crusher’s choose-two modes are picked on entry, and the damage mode can hit a planeswalker or clear a blocker before the trample damage is assigned.

Matchups

Against aggro the deck is soft: there is no removal spell in the list, so the plan is to trade Heartwood Crafter and Konstrari Improviser early, land Karn as a 5/5 blocker, and race with Dragons from turn five. Against midrange it is a favourite, because the token engine goes wider than most midrange boards and Craftwork Crusher answers their best creature on entry. Against control, hold Draconic Visitor until you can cast it with a Soul Tether or a Saheeli trigger behind it, and lean on Karn and the Echoverse Fulcrum to keep cards flowing; a single Visitor that resolves usually ends the game in two attacks. Against combo, the deck has no interaction beyond damage, so it must mulligan toward the fastest curve and count on flying Dragons closing before the opponent’s engine comes online.
