Overview

Reality Fracture’s surveil axis is not a card-selection theme, it is a fuel line. Forty-three cards in the set surveil and forty-nine reference the graveyard, and the strongest of them convert library-to-graveyard movement into size. This deck takes that literally: every spell either fills the graveyard, profits from the graveyard, or both. There is no card in the sixty that does neither.

The cheapest engine is Dark Matter Manipulator ({B}, 1/2). It mills three on entering and gains +2/+0 for every seven cards in your graveyard, so once the fuel is flowing it stops being a one-drop and becomes a four- or six-power attacker for one mana. Two more mill effects sit on bodies: Something Worth Saving ({1}{G} instant, mill four, take a permanent from among them, gain 1 life) and Primal Witchstalker ({1}{B}{G}, 2/1 menace, mill four and return a land from the graveyard to the battlefield tapped). Seasoned Cryomancer ({1}{U}{U}, 2/2) filters two cards into the graveyard and taps down the opponent’s board for each nonland card discarded, then exiles itself to draw two later.

The payoff side is where the deck stops being a mill pile. Recursive Recruitment ({2}{U}{B}) makes two 2/2 Cadets, and its flashback halves the cost of the second cast by putting the card in the graveyard first: cast from a graveyard, each Cadet enters with a +1/+1 counter for every three cards in your graveyard. Tarmogoyf ({1}{G}) and Winter, Tormented Loner ({2}{B}, 0/3) scale off the same number. Uldaros Theorix ({3}{U}{B}{B}, 5/5 flier) exiles one nonland card of each type from the graveyard, copies them, and lets you cast up to six mana of those copies for free. Cruel Calculations ({2}{U}) draws a card for every card that hit a graveyard from a library this turn, which in this deck means it draws four off one mill spell.

There is no ramp and no fixing beyond the set’s own duals: the curve tops out at six and the deck wants to spend turns one through four developing, milling and attacking.

Curve and mana

Twenty-four lands, thirteen blue sources, twelve green, eleven black; the only double-colour costs are blue ({1}{U}{U} on Cryomancer, Chandra and Sphinx) and the fixed {3}{U}{B}{B} on Uldaros. The one-drop slot is four Manipulators, the two-drop slot has eleven cards, and everything above four is two Uldaros. Four Islands, four Swamps and four Forests mean the deck functions after a wipe on lands alone, which matters because the duals all want a planeswalker or two other lands first.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead on a Manipulator when you have one: it mills three immediately, which turns on Goyf’s card-type count and starts the graveyard clock. Hold Something Worth Saving until you can use the permanent it returns; milling four on turn two to find nothing is still fine, but the card is at its best when it rebuys a Recursive Recruitment or a Chandra you already used. Primal Witchstalker is the turn-three play that guarantees a fourth land, since it returns a land from the graveyard to the battlefield tapped.

Mid game

Chandra, Chill of Compliance is the engine’s steering wheel. Her first +1 surveils and returns any noncreature, nonland card you bin to your hand, so every Chandra activation is either a free spell back or a land on top; her second +1 pays for the noncreature spell you just rebought. Seasoned Cryomancer is the other half: draw two, discard two, and tap down two attackers in the process. Against creature decks that sequence is often a two-for-one on defence, and it sets up Cruel Calculations for a four-card draw.

The combo turn

The deck’s “combo” is a two-card conversion in one turn:

  1. Get Recursive Recruitment into the graveyard. Cryomancer, Sphinx of False Conclusions and Something Worth Saving all do it incidentally; you can also discard it to Twinned Vision’s flashback cost.
  2. Flash it back for {6}{U}{B} with twenty or more cards in the graveyard. Two Cadets enter with six or more +1/+1 counters each, which is twelve power across two bodies in one turn with no prior board.
  3. Copy the larger Cadet with Hall of Echoes for {5} and attack for the difference.

With Uldaros Theorix in the same turn, the flashback and the free-cast trigger can chain: Theorix exiles the Recruitment from the graveyard as its sorcery, copies it, and casts the copy for free, which puts two more counters-scaled Cadets on the board without paying the flashback cost.

Win condition 2

When the graveyard is smaller, the deck wins with bodies. Tarmogoyf is a two-mana threat that is usually 4/5 or 5/6 by turn four because instants, sorceries, creatures and lands are all in graveyards. Dark Matter Manipulator is a one-mana threat that grows two power per seven cards. Winter, Tormented Loner is a 0/3 that edicts on entry and then attacks as a five- or six-power creature once the graveyard fills. Hall of Echoes converts any of them into a second copy for a turn, which is how the deck closes through a single blocker.

Mulligan guide

Hand Keep?
One-drop Manipulator, two lands, a two-drop Keep. The deck needs the engine online by turn two
Two lands, Cryomancer, Something Worth Saving, Recursive Recruitment Keep. Twelve cards in the graveyard by turn four is the threshold that makes Recruitment lethal
All lands and Uldaros Mulligan. Six mana with no mill is a dead hand
Chandra plus three lands and no mill Keep on the play, mulligan on the draw. Chandra belongs in a hand that already mills

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Cruel Calculations targets a player. Target yourself after milling to draw the full count; targeting the opponent only counts their own library-to-graveyard movement.
  • Tarmogoyf counts all graveyards. Removal spells and fetch-style sacrifice effects on the other side of the table grow it too.
  • Chandra’s first +1 returns only noncreature, nonland cards. Creature cards stay in the graveyard, which is where Winter and Recursive Recruitment want them.
  • Primal Witchstalker needs a land in the graveyard for the second half of its trigger; it will whiff if the four milled cards are all spells.
  • Hall of Echoes produces colourless mana. It is a fifth-land-plus card, not a turn-three land.
  • Darklight Phoenix needs two creatures to have died this turn. It returns at the beginning of combat, so it is a rebuy after a block-heavy turn, not a turn-four play.
  • Sphinx of False Conclusions copies itself when it dies, and the copy is a token, so it does not loop further but it does leave a 4/2 flier behind after a trade.
