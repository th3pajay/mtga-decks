Overview

Reality Fracture’s Prepared mechanic is a one-shot spell stapled to a creature: the creature enters prepared, you may cast a copy of its spell, and doing so unprepares it. That is fine value. The problem is that it is only one spell — unless something re-prepares the creature.

Two things in this deck do that. Hexhaven Dueling Arena ({2}, {T}, sorcery speed) prepares a creature that attacked this turn, which turns every attack step into another free spell. And Pyre Rhymer ({1}{R}{R}, 3/3 Elemental Sorcerer with prowess) holds the best spell in the set for this purpose: Molten Tide ({R} instant, “until end of turn, whenever you tap a Mountain for mana, add an additional {R}”). A free ritual every combat is already a lot of red mana. Ajani’s Anguish ({X}{R} enchantment, enters and deals X damage to any target, your creatures have trample) is where that mana goes, and Stingcaster Mage ({1}{R}, 2/1 haste) gives Molten Tide flashback from the graveyard for {R} so the ritual can happen twice in a turn.

The supporting cast is the Jeskai half of the prepared cycle, chosen because their free spells either pump the team, dig, or answer a permanent — all things a prowess deck wants on the stack. Twenty-two of the twenty-nine creatures are prepared; the rest are prowess and value payoffs that get paid every time one of those free spells is cast.

Approach

Game plan phases

Turns 1–2, commit cheaply. One-mana prepared creatures are the engine: Pompous Battlemage ({R}, 1/1 Goblin Sorcerer, prowess) and Diviner of Victory ({U}, 1/1 Dwarf Wizard). Both arrive prepared. Casting the free copy is optional — Diviner’s Unwind History bounces a cheap creature and surveils 1, which is worth holding for a real target, while Battlemage’s Improvised Act loots and is usually correct on turn 2 if the hand is land-heavy.

Turns 3–4, deploy the board. Variable Chaser ({2}{U}, 2/3 flying prowess), Pyre Rhymer and Prudent Fateseer ({1}{W/U}{W/U}, 1/4) are the three-drop tier. Prudent Fateseer is the quiet engine: every scry or surveil gives the whole team +1/+0 until end of turn, once per turn, and the Peer Review tokens surveil. Traxos, Academy Guardian ({3}{U}) costs {1}{U} on any turn you have already cast a noncreature spell, which the prepared copies provide for free.

Turns 5+, convert. Rhymer attacks, Hexhaven re-prepares it after combat, Molten Tide makes every Mountain two red, and Ajani’s Anguish or a second Anguish ends the game. Vraska, Soul of Stone adds Sculpture Treasure tokens on each noncreature spell, which both ramps the finish and gives vigilance attackers.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a one-drop, and prefer the one that produces blue mana pressure: Diviner of Victory grows on every surveil, so Fatehold Chronologist and Prudent Fateseer later turn it into a 2/2 or 3/3. Do not fire Unwind History at the first legal target — hold it for the creature that would otherwise block a flier. Improvised Act is a free loot; use it to dig for the third land or the Molten Tide.

Mid game

The three-drop slot decides games. Resolve Pyre Rhymer when you have four or more lands, because the Molten Tide it carries is only worth casting with Mountains behind it. Prudent Fateseer plus any Peer Review creature is a soft combo: the Cadet token’s surveil triggers the Fateseer, which pumps the team, which makes the 1/4 body into a real blocker while the fliers attack. Fulminous Forte is the answer to a token board — one damage to each creature and planeswalker your opponents control, your side untouched.

The combo turn

The sequence that ends games:

  1. Attack with Pyre Rhymer (and any other prepared creature). Prowess pumps it; combat damage is incidental.
  2. After combat, activate Hexhaven Dueling Arena for {2} and {T}: Rhymer becomes prepared again.
  3. Cast the free copy of Molten Tide. From now until end of turn, each Mountain taps for {R}{R}.
  4. Tap out. With four Mountains and two Steam Vents that is twelve red.
  5. Cast Ajani’s Anguish for {X}{R} with X at nine or more, pointing the damage at the opponent. A second Anguish, or a Wrath of the Bloodmane on the last blocker, finishes.

Stingcaster Mage is the redundancy: its enters trigger gives Molten Tide flashback for {R}, so a turn that already spent the prepared copy can still double its mana from the graveyard. Two Molten Tides on eight Mountains make every Mountain tap for three red instead of one: twenty-four mana, more than enough for both Anguish copies and a Wrath.

Win condition two

Without the ritual, the deck wins the ordinary prowess way. Every prepared copy is a noncreature spell, so a board of Variable Chaser and Traxos grows by one or two power per free spell while attacking in the air, and Tetsuko Umezawa, Pursuer double strikes for two prowess triggers each. Ajani’s Anguish granting trample converts a wide board of 3/3s into a one-turn kill through chump blockers. Tetsuko’s second ability also punishes small blockers: any creature with power or toughness 1 or less that blocks costs its controller a life.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands that include a blue source and a red source, one one-mana prepared creature, and either a three-drop from the Value tier or Ajani’s Anguish. Ship hands with no red source (the burn plan disappears), one-land hands, and hands made entirely of four-drops. A two-land hand with Pompous Battlemage, Diviner of Victory and Hexhaven Dueling Arena is a keep: the Arena turns the second creature into a repeated free spell.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Prepared is one-shot. Casting the copy unprepares the creature, and nothing re-prepares it automatically. Hexhaven Dueling Arena and its {4}, {T} mode (any creature, not just attackers) are the only ways back, so save the Arena activation for a turn where the free spell actually matters.
  • Hexhaven produces {C}, not coloured mana. It pays generic costs only; do not count it as a blue source.
  • Steam Vents is typed “Land — Island Mountain”, so it triggers Molten Tide’s doubling and counts toward the ritual. Eight of the twenty-four lands are Mountains, which is why the ritual is reliable without over-loading the manabase.
  • Prudent Fateseer triggers once per turn. Surveilling twice in a turn is still only one pump, so sequence the Peer Review copy after combat rather than before it when the pump matters for damage.
  • Twinned Vision reads “if this spell wasn’t cast from your hand, draw two”. Flashback is the intended mode: cast it from the graveyard for two cards, discarding a spare land to the flashback cost.
  • Vraska, Soul of Stone’s tokens are artifact creatures with vigilance, so they attack and stay home on defence, and they sacrifice for any colour of mana on the ritual turn.
  • Wrath of the Bloodmane costs {1} less with a legendary creature in play. Tetsuko, Traxos and Vraska all qualify, so it is usually a two-mana four-damage instant.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Fulminous Forte is a one-sided sweeper against one-toughness boards. Trade prepared one-drops freely, then stabilise behind Prudent Fateseer’s 1/4 and Traxos’s 1/5.
Control Do not over-commit into sweepers. Hold Molten Tide and both Ajani’s Anguish copies for a single turn where the ritual produces ten or more red. Twinned Vision’s flashback keeps the hand full.
Midrange Attack in the air with Variable Chaser and Traxos, keep Wrath of the Bloodmane for their largest threat, and let Tetsuko Umezawa double strike race.
Combo Fulminous Forte answers small utility creatures and planeswalkers at instant speed. The deck’s clock is turn five or six with a ritual, so race rather than react.
