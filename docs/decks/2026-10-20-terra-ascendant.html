Overview

Empower is the set’s flagship mechanic and it is secretly a landfall mechanic in this shell. Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes reads “Landfall — whenever a land you control enters, empower Jace 2”: every land drop either creates a Jace planeswalker token (with “[-1]: Surveil 1” and “[-3]: Draw a card”) or adds two loyalty to the one you already have. Two mana on turn two, a land a turn, and the token is climbing three or four loyalty a turn without you spending a single card on it.

That loyalty is currency. Way of the Wildspeaker gives every planeswalker you control “[-4]: Create a 4/4 green Beast creature token with trample” and Kiora of Salt and Sand gives them “[-8]: Create an 8/8 blue Leviathan creature token with hexproof”. Because the counters arrive on landfall rather than on a fixed schedule, a Jace token routinely sits at seven to nine loyalty and can spend four on a Beast, then grow back past the threshold on the very next land.

The second floor of the deck is lifegain. Way of the Paradox gains a life every time you activate a loyalty ability and lets you play an additional land that turn, which is the only card in the deck that both pays the lifegain triggers and re-arms the landfall engine in the same turn. Koth of the Homestead gains a life on every land drop and grows a creature on every Plains. Those points of life are the fuel for Titanbones, Towering Heart (two +1/+1 counters per gain), Bloombrute (a card per turn), Kwia Vigorbloom (a Lotus token per turn, three mana of any one colour) and Unflinching Hortimancer.

Nothing here is a fair card played fairly: lands are the engine, loyalty is the resource, life is the second resource, and the win is whatever the two resources buy this turn.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Establish the tap-out engine (turns 1 to 3). Land, then two mana: Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes, Tam's Resistance (empower Jace 4, plus a +1/+1 counter) or Solarium Sentry. Transformative Commons enters untapped from the moment a planeswalker is on the battlefield, so the mana fixes itself as the engine starts. Koth of the Homestead on turn three begins the lifegain drip and turns every Plains into a counter.
  2. Turn loyalty into bodies (turns 4 to 6). Way of the Paradox is the priority: from then on every loyalty activation gains a life and buys an extra land drop, so the same turn both triggers the lifegain payoffs and re-triggers Avatar. Way of the Wildspeaker enters and empowers Jace 7 by itself, immediately arming the Beast ability. Titanbones and Bloombrute convert the life into a growing board and cards.
  3. Convert (turn six onwards). Spend loyalty on Beasts and Leviathans, land a Kwia Vigorbloom for the flying lifelink clock, and attack with a board the opponent cannot out-size. Kiora of Salt and Sand makes the biggest attacker unblockable on any turn you activated a loyalty ability, which is every turn this deck functions.

Curve and mana

Sixteen cards cost two, ten cost three, six cost four and four cost five or more, on twenty-four lands with thirteen green sources, eleven white and twelve blue. The deck wants four lands by turn four and is happy to see six, because Way of the Paradox converts extra lands into loyalty and lifegain rather than flooding out.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead on a green source and a two-drop. Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes is the best turn-two play because it makes the following land drops into loyalty; Tam's Resistance is the best turn-two play when you already have an Avatar or a Way in hand, since it puts a Jace token straight to four loyalty. Do not trade Solarium Sentry or Unflinching Hortimancer for a card; they are the bodies that make the lifegain plan lethal later.

Mid game and the combo turn

The combo turn is any turn that starts with Way of the Paradox on the battlefield and a Jace token at five or more loyalty:

  1. Activate the token’s “[-3]: Draw a card” (or “[-1]: Surveil 1” when the top card is known to be bad) — this gains a life from Way of the Paradox and triggers Titanbones, Bloombrute, Kwia and Hortimancer.
  2. Play the extra land Way of the Paradox grants — Avatar empowers Jace 2, Koth gains another life, and a Plains adds a +1/+1 counter.
  3. Spend four loyalty on the Beast ability from Way of the Wildspeaker, or eight on the Leviathan from Kiora of Salt and Sand, and pass with a token still above zero loyalty.

Because the token only dies at zero, the sequencing rule is simple: never activate an ability that would leave the token at zero unless the body it buys is worth more than the engine. With Theorist's Sanctum untapped, two mana buys two loyalty back at instant speed if the opponent tries to attack it down.

Win condition 2

Titanbones, Towering Heart plus any repeatable lifegain is the deck’s honest clock: Paradox activations, Koth land drops and Solarium Sentry triggers each add two counters, so a Titanbones that survives two turns is usually a 10/9 or larger. Kiora of Salt and Sand makes it unblockable on any turn a loyalty ability was activated, and Bloombrute’s activated ability gives it trample and lifelink for the finish. Kwia Vigorbloom is the alternative: a 6/6 flying lifelink that also makes Lotus tokens, which then pay for a second spell in the same turn.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two to four lands, at least one green source, and either Avatar of Burgeoning Echoes or Way of the Paradox. A hand of Transformative Commons, Hallowed Fountain, Avatar, Tam’s Resistance is a snap keep. Ship hands with no green source, hands with only five-plus drops, and hands whose only engine is Way of the Wildspeaker without a loyalty source. Six-card hands are fine in this deck; the engine is redundant.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Way of the Paradox grants the additional land drop on the turn you activate a loyalty ability, so hold a land in hand rather than playing it out when the Way is about to land.
  • Empower creates the Jace token only if you do not already control one, and then puts the counters on it. Multiple empower effects in one turn stack onto the same token, which is why Tam's Resistance into a land drop can put a fresh token to six loyalty.
  • Countersculpt can behold the Jace token, so the two-blue counter is effectively unconditional once the engine is running.
  • Theorist's Sanctum can also behold the Jace token to enter untapped — behold is a choice, not a sacrifice.
  • Vigorbloom Charm’s fight mode is the answer to a large blocker that Kiora cannot make unblockable; the hexproof and indestructible mode protects the token from removal in response to an activation.
  • Kwia Vigorbloom’s Lotus token is an artifact, not a land, so it does not trigger landfall — it pays for spells instead.

Matchups

  • Aggro: the deck’s early bodies are 3/3 and 2/1, and Solarium Sentry gains 2 on every cheap opposing spell, so the first four turns are survivable. Vigorbloom Charm and Countersculpt cover the one big threat, and once Titanbones or Kwia lands the life totals swing hard.
  • Midrange: the best matchup. The opponent cannot out-size a board that adds loyalty on every land and counters on every life gain; save Vigorbloom Charm for their removal spell on the token.
  • Control: the engine sits on enchantments, lands and a planeswalker token, so sweepers only reset the creatures. Keep a land in hand to re-arm landfall immediately after a wipe, and use Way of the Cryomancer’s copy ability to double a Charm or a removal spell in the counter war.
  • Combo: the deck has no stack interaction beyond Countersculpt, so the plan is to race with Beasts and Leviathans and to use Kiora’s unblockable rider for exact damage. Countersculpt is for the one spell that actually ends the game.
  • Graveyard decks: nothing here interacts with a graveyard, so the plan is speed — a turn-four Beast plus a turn-five Titanbones is usually faster than a reanimation setup that needs its own setup turn.
