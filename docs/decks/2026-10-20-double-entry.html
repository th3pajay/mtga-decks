Double Entry

Five-color card-draw combo — Vnwxt doubles every draw and Homunculus Horde compounds it

Overview

Double Entry is built on one arithmetic idea: a single card draw should be worth two cards, and every extra card drawn should be worth a creature. Vnwxt, Verbose Host supplies the first half once you reach max speed — “If you would draw a card, draw two cards instead” — and Homunculus Horde supplies the second, creating a token copy of itself every time you draw your second card in a turn.

That combination collapses the usual cost of a draw-payoff deck. Normally you have to spend two cards to get one payoff trigger. Here, one one-mana Might of the Meek, one Quick Study, or a single Fountainport activation is already two draws, so a lone Vnwxt turns your cheapest cantrip into a full trigger for every payoff on the board. Each Horde on the battlefield triggers separately, so the copies compound: one Horde becomes two, two become four, and the tokens arrive with Warleader's Call draining the opponent for each body.

The deck plays five colors because the pieces live in different places: blue owns the engine, white owns the anthem and the guaranteed draw sink, black owns the drain and the recursion, red owns the one-mana cantrip and the looting glyph, green owns the mana. No dual lands are used, so the mana is deliberately cheap on colored pips (almost everything costs a single colored mana) and green one-drops plus Uncharted Haven carry the fixing.

Curve and mana

The curve tops out at four. Twenty-two of thirty-six spells cost two or less, so the deck functions on three lands and starts drawing two cards a turn by turn four. Colored requirements are intentionally flat: the only double pip in the deck is The Water Crystal at {2}{U}{U}, and it is a four-drop you cast after your blue sources are online. Green sources come first (Llanowar Elves, Bloom Tender, Forest, Uncharted Haven naming green), then blue, and the red and white splash cards are all single-pip.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with fixing over action when you have both: a turn-one Llanowar Elves into a turn-two Vnwxt, Verbose Host is the best opening in the deck, because Vnwxt needs time to climb to max speed. Speed rises once on each of your turns when an opponent loses life, so the first point of damage matters more than it looks — a Raven Eagle attack, a Mischievous Mystic in the air, or a Warleader's Call trigger all advance the clock that doubles your draws.

If you do not have Vnwxt, Mischievous Mystic is the correct two-drop: it is a 2/1 flier that starts making Faeries as soon as any second draw happens, and it carries Warleader's Call well.

The combo turn

The typical engine turn is three mana and no cards spent on payoffs: cast Quick Study (two draws, doubled to four cards by Vnwxt), and every payoff on the battlefield triggers once. With one Horde you get a copy; with two Hordes you get two copies; with a Mystic and a Fabricator as well you add a Faerie and a Thopter for each of those. Warleader's Call turns each of those bodies into a point of damage on entry, so a “draw two” spell commonly ends up as four to six cards, three to five creatures, and three to five damage.

Fountainport is the repeatable version: {2}, {T}, sacrifice a token, draw a card. Under Vnwxt that is two draws and a trigger, which means spare Faeries and Thopters convert directly into more Hordes.

Win condition 2

The Water Crystal is the alternate axis and it punishes anyone who tries to grind you out. Vnwxt already removes your maximum hand size, and the deck draws in bursts, so holding twelve to sixteen cards is normal. Activating the Crystal for {4}{U}{U} mills cards equal to your hand size, and its own replacement effect adds four on top: a fourteen-card hand mills eighteen. Two activations across two turns is a kill, and the Crystal also discounts every blue spell by {1} while it is out.

The same artifact makes every future mill effect four cards larger, and Raven Eagle’s Clue tokens keep the hand stocked so the mill number keeps climbing.

Mulligan guide

Keep when Ship when
Two or three lands including a blue source and a green source, plus a two-drop engine piece One land, or no blue source
Any hand with Vnwxt plus a one-mana draw spell All four-drops with no early interaction
Hands with two Hordes and three lands on the play Hands that need white and red early but only have Islands

Six-card hands are fine in this deck because the engine is redundant; seven-card hands with no two-drop are not.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • “Draw your second card each turn” triggers exactly once per turn. Extra draws after the second do nothing for the payoffs, so sequence the cheapest draw last only if you need the cards, not the triggers.
  • Vnwxt only doubles draws at max speed (4). Before that it is a 0/4 that removes your maximum hand size, which is still relevant for the mill plan.
  • Volatile Wanderglyph triggers when it becomes tapped, so attacking with it loots; crewing Thopter Fabricator with it also taps it and loots.
  • Wolfbat returns only if you can pay {B}, and it comes back with a finality counter — it dies permanently the second time.
  • Gleaming Splendor’s {2}{W} ability targets two players, and you may target yourself and the opponent, which guarantees your second draw on a turn where you otherwise have no draw spell.
  • Token copies made by Homunculus Horde are 2/2 creatures, not Hordes in the deck sense, but they do trigger Warleader's Call and they can be sacrificed to Fountainport.
  • Might of the Meek gives trample regardless; the +1/+0 clause needs a Mouse you do not have, so treat it as a one-mana cantrip.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Trade early with Mystic and Wolfbat, land Warleader's Call to make blocks profitable, then out-draw them; the drain triggers buy the extra turn you need
Control Do not commit three Hordes into a sweeper; hold Fountainport and one draw spell, then rebuild in a single turn. Switch to the Crystal plan if the board is contested
Midrange Go wide and use Rogue's Passage on the largest token; their removal is one-for-one against a deck that makes two bodies a turn
Other draw decks Your engine is cheaper; race with fliers and keep Raven Eagle draining every turn
