Overview

Cloaked Column is a Mardu deck built on one loop: attack with a Mobilize creature, and every token that arrives is a creature entering the battlefield. Alliance payoffs and enter-trigger enchantments are watching that zone constantly, so a single combat step turns into four to eight separate damage triggers.

The numbers are what make the deck work. Slash, Reptile Rampager deals 2 damage to each opponent for every creature that enters, Raphael, Tough Turtle adds 1 more, and Warleader's Call plus Impact Tremors add 1 each. The tokens are free — Mobilize creates them tapped and attacking, already swinging — so a Zurgo attack with two payoffs up is 6 damage to each opponent before blockers are even declared, and the tokens are still attacking.

Zurgo, Thunder's Decree is the engine’s anchor. Its second ability gives your Warrior tokens “This token can’t be sacrificed” during your end step, which is exactly when Mobilize’s sacrifice trigger tries to fire. The tokens simply stay. Every Zurgo attack permanently adds two 1/1 Warriors, and those Warriors attack again next turn while Zurgo makes two more.

Disguise is the deck’s other half, and it is not decoration. A disguise creature is cast face down for {3} as a 2/2 with ward {2} — a real body that dodges most early removal and costs the opponent a card to answer. Turning it face up later is where the value sits: Pyrotechnic Performer converts any flip into damage equal to that creature’s power to each opponent, Dog Walker makes two Dog tokens on the flip (two more Alliance triggers), and Unyielding Gatekeeper exiles a nonland permanent. Expose the Culprit is the instant-speed flip, and it can also re-cloak your own disguise creatures to re-arm those flip triggers later.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1–2: establish the watchers. Land a payoff first — Raphael or a face-down disguise body — so the tokens you make later are worth more. Torch the Tower and Get Lost cover the early exchange.
  2. Turns 3–4: start the column. Zurgo or Dalkovan Packbeasts arrive and attack. From here, every combat creates two to three tokens; with Slash or Warleader’s Call down, that is 4 to 12 damage per combat spread across both opponents.
  3. Turns 4–6: convert to lethal. Slash lands as a 7/5 that keeps paying out, EPF Point Squad has grown into a threat, and Rogue’s Passage clears a blocker for the final swing. Pyrotechnic Performer flips close games that stall on the ground.

Curve and mana

Twenty-five lands: twelve shock lands (four each of Blood Crypt, Godless Shrine, Sacred Foundry), five fastlands, two Rogue’s Passage and six basics (3 Plains, 2 Mountain, 1 Swamp). White is the deepest color at sixteen sources, red sits at thirteen, and black is a deliberate splash at eleven — only Zurgo and Bone-Cairn Butcher ask for it, and both are happy to arrive a turn late. Lead on Sacred Foundry or Inspiring Vantage, hold Godless Shrine for the turn you cast Zurgo, and pay the 2 life on a shock only when the tempo matters.

The nonland curve is 2 ones, 16 twos, 12 threes, 2 fours and 3 fives; average mana value is about 2.7. Nothing in the deck costs more than five, and Mobilize means you never have to tap out to attack — the tokens arrive for free.

Gameplay strategy

Early game (turns 1–3)

  • Turn 1: Torch the Tower on their mana creature, or hold it for a real threat.
  • Turn 2: Raphael, Tough Turtle, Impact Tremors or a face-down disguise creature. A face-down 2/2 with ward {2} is often the best play — it is a body now and a payoff later.
  • Turn 3: Zurgo, Thunder’s Decree or Dalkovan Packbeasts, and attack immediately. Even a single attack with two payoffs on board is 4 to 6 damage to each opponent.
  • Do not trade tokens for nothing. Mobilize tokens die at the end of the turn on their own unless Zurgo is on the battlefield; with him, they are permanent.

Mid game (turns 4–6)

  • Slash is the card that ends games: it is a 7/5 that pays 2 damage per entering creature, and it makes its own 2/2 Mutant every time it attacks. Cast it before attacking, never after.
  • Bone-Cairn Butcher makes your attacking tokens deathtouch, so a swarm of 1/1s suddenly blocks and kills anything.
  • EPF Point Squad grows by one counter per entering creature. Watch it become the biggest creature in play, then clear a blocker with Rogue’s Passage.
  • Expose the Culprit is at its best on their turn: flip a face-down Pyrotechnic Performer mid-combat and the 3 damage to each opponent can end the game before damage is dealt.

The combo turn

  1. Board state needed: one Mobilize attacker (Zurgo, Packbeasts or Butcher) plus at least two payoffs (Slash, Raphael, Warleader’s Call, Impact Tremors, EPF Point Squad).
  2. Attack with every Mobilize creature. The tokens are created tapped and attacking, so they enter and are already in combat.
  3. Resolve the enter triggers — each token triggers each payoff separately. Slash deals 2, Raphael 1, Warleader’s Call 1 and Impact Tremors 1 per token, per opponent.
  4. Mana up, then flip disguise creatures with the leftover: each flip is another Pyrotechnic Performer trigger, and Dog Walker’s two Dogs are two more Alliance triggers after the flip.
  5. If a blocker would stop lethal, Rogue’s Passage the biggest attacker for {4}. Ten damage from triggers plus an unblockable 7/5 usually closes both opponents in one turn.

Win condition 2: the swarm

Not every game is a burn game. Warleader’s Call turns every 1/1 Warrior into a 2/2, EPF Point Squad carries counters out of every combat, and Zurgo keeps adding two Warriors per attack permanently. That is a board that wins through combat damage alone, with Rogue’s Passage guaranteeing the last hit and Bone-Cairn Butcher’s deathtouch tokens making blocks unprofitable.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: two or three lands with white and red, one two-drop payoff (Raphael, EPF Point Squad, Pyrotechnic Performer), and one Mobilize creature or a card that rewards going wide.
  • Trim: hands with no creature at all, hands of only five-drops, or one-land hands without a one-drop. Five-land hands with a payoff are keepable — the deck uses its mana.
  • Best single card to see: Zurgo. It upgrades every later combat from temporary tokens to a permanent army.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Zurgo’s rule is the whole trick. Warrior tokens cannot be sacrificed during your end step, which is exactly when Mobilize wants to sacrifice them. They stay on the battlefield and attack again next turn.
  • Mobilize tokens enter tapped and attacking — they never block, and they cannot be declared as attackers next turn unless they survive (Zurgo) or the turn is over. Their enter triggers happen before blockers, so killing your attacker does not stop the damage.
  • Turning a creature face up does not trigger “enters the battlefield” — it only triggers flip abilities like Pyrotechnic Performer’s. Dog Walker’s Dogs are the exception: those tokens really do enter.
  • Expose the Culprit’s second mode cloaks your own face-up disguise creatures. A cloaked card is turned face up for its mana cost, not its disguise cost, so re-arming Pyrotechnic Performer costs {1}{R} the second time.
  • Ward {2} on face-down bodies matters. Opponents must pay 2 to target them, which is often the difference between your engine surviving and not.
  • EPF Point Squad costs {1}{R/W}{R/W} — white or red mana both work, so it is never stuck in hand.
  • Get Lost gives the opponent two Map tokens. Use it on the threat that actually races you, not on the first creature you see.

Matchups

  • Aggro: your tokens trade up with Bone-Cairn Butcher on the board, Torch the Tower and Get Lost answer the early curve, and Dalkovan Packbeasts blocks as a 0/4 vigilance. Race only when Slash is out.
  • Control: disguise is your best card against them — a face-down 2/2 with ward {2} blanks sorcery-speed removal, and Expose the Culprit lets you flip in response. Do not over-commit into a sweeper; Zurgo rebuilds two Warriors per attack.
  • Midrange: you go wider faster. Slash plus two Mobilize attackers outpaces most four-drop creatures, and Rogue’s Passage beats a stalled board.
  • Combo: you have no stack interaction, so the plan is speed — burn them out with Slash and Warleader’s Call before their engine comes online, and hold Get Lost for the key creature.
