Overview

Heartwood is Reality Fracture’s artifact economy. A Heartwood token is a red and green artifact with “{T}: Add {R} or {G}” — mana and an artifact, never a creature. The deck turns that last detail into an advantage: the tokens ramp, they block nothing, and they pile up as a resource that four different cards convert into damage or size.

The supply is the Soul Tether cycle. Heartwood Crafter, Konstrari Improviser and Woodwork Prodigy all enter prepared, and while prepared you may cast a copy of Soul Tether ({2}{R/G}) to create a Heartwood and unprepare the creature. Woodwork Prodigy is the engine: it becomes prepared again at every upkeep, so it hands you a Heartwood every turn for the rest of the game. Tenured Tethermage and Aerid Konstrari add tokens on entry.

The payoffs all scale. Craterclaw Colossus gives the team trample and +X/+0 where X is your artifact count, Pia, Determined Rebuilder does the same to one creature every turn, Aerid Konstrari grows by the same measure when it activates, and Secluded Starforge taps artifacts for power without spending a card. Because Heartwoods regenerate every turn, the same six artifacts get counted again and again.

The blue splash is thin and deliberate: The Mechanist, Aerial Artisan turns a Heartwood into a 3/1 flier and pays you a Clue for every prepared spell, and Saheeli, the Sun's Brilliance copies the biggest artifact or creature on the board for a turn.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-3, lay the engine. Lead on Heartwood Crafter or Konstrari Improviser. Cast the Soul Tether copy as soon as the mana exists — a 1/1 plus a mana rock on turn two is exactly the curve this deck wants. Sandstorm Salvager and Pia, Determined Rebuilder add creature tokens that can actually attack, and Konstrari Charm clears a blocker or fixes a colour.
  2. Turns 3-6, multiply the count. Woodwork Prodigy is the priority three-drop: every upkeep it re-arms, so every turn it converts three mana into a Heartwood. Tenured Tethermage turns a spare land into two tokens, Aerid Konstrari is a 5/4 flier that pays a Heartwood on entry and again on death, and The Mechanist starts turning spare Heartwoods into 3/1 fliers while each Soul Tether copy draws a Clue.
  3. Turns 5-7, convert. Deploy Craterclaw Colossus (haste, team trample, +X/+0 by artifact count), then attack with everything. If the ground is stalled, Pia or Secluded Starforge stacks the same bonus on one flier and finishes through the air. Saheeli doubles the biggest enters trigger on the turn you need it.

Curve and mana

Six cards cost one mana, twenty-two cost two, three cost three, two cost four, two cost five and one costs seven. The deck is deliberately top-light: it wants three lands, a Heartwood engine and a scaling payoff. Twenty-four lands is correct because Secluded Starforge produces only colourless mana and Temur Monument plus Melded Moxite smooth the draws. Eight of the duals arrive tapped unless you control two other lands, so the first two lands of a hand should be basics.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Open on a one-drop or a two-drop that makes an artifact, never on a tapland. Heartwood Crafter on turn one into Konstrari Improviser plus its Soul Tether on turn two is the ideal sequence: two bodies and two mana rocks by turn three, with Burst Lightning or Konstrari Charm held for the first blocker. Do not trade the prepared creatures in combat if the trade costs you the Soul Tether body — the tokens matter more than the 1/1s.

Mid game and the combo turn

The engine turn is a re-armed Woodwork Prodigy with three mana up: cast Soul Tether, make a Heartwood, keep the 3/3 back to block. Every extra token is worth roughly one point of power on the combo turn, which is why Tenured Tethermage (two tokens from one land) and Aerid Konstrari (one on entry, one on death) are worth their cost.

The combo turn itself is Craterclaw Colossus with five or more artifacts in play. It enters, your whole team gains trample and +5/+0 or better, and the Heartwoods that paid for it are still untapped for Secluded Starforge. Sequence it as: cast Colossus, activate Pia or Secluded Starforge on the largest evasive body, then attack. Saheeli, the Sun's Brilliance copying the Colossus doubles the enters trigger, which is usually lethal on its own.

Win condition 2 — the copy engine

Saheeli, the Sun's Brilliance is not a fair card in this shell. Copying Craterclaw Colossus gives a hasty 5/5 that triggers the team pump a second time; copying Hungering Puppetbeast gives a hasty 5/5 and a Heartwood; copying a 3/3 Golem or a Heartwood keeps the artifact count climbing for Secluded Starforge. Against control, copy whichever artifact is largest and attack once — the copy is sacrificed at end of turn, so it is pure profit.

The Mechanist, Aerial Artisan is the other half of the grind: each prepared spell is a noncreature spell, so every Soul Tether copy makes a Clue as well as a Heartwood, and {T} turns a spare Heartwood into a 3/1 flier that counts as an artifact for every payoff.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with a Heartwood supplier (Heartwood Crafter, Konstrari Improviser) plus two or three lands, and keep Woodwork Prodigy with three lands. Ship one-land hands without Konstrari Charm or Burst Lightning, ship hands with three or more colourless-only lands, and ship hands that need both a fourth land and a second colour. Temur Monument and Melded Moxite make five-land hands keepable.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Preparation exists only on creatures with prepare spells: Heartwood Crafter, Konstrari Improviser and Woodwork Prodigy. Nothing else in the deck can become prepared.
  • Casting a copy unprepares the creature. Woodwork Prodigy is the only one that re-arms itself; the other two pay out once per entry, so copy their spell before they die if the mana is free.
  • Heartwood Crafter’s {T}: Add {C} can only pay for spells not cast from your hand — which is exactly the Soul Tether copy, so tap it before casting.
  • Heartwood tokens are artifacts, not creatures. They cannot attack, they do not trigger Sandstorm Salvager, and they die to artifact removal; The Mechanist is the way to turn one into a body.
  • The legend rule applies immediately, so a Saheeli copy of Aerid Konstrari dies on the spot: two Heartwoods for one activation, not a second attacker. Copy the non-legendary payoffs instead.
  • Tenured Tethermage sacrifices a land on entry — only use it on a spare land or when you are flooding, and remember the two Heartwoods arrive tapped.
  • Konstrari Charm’s third mode adds {C}{C}{C}, which pays generic costs only; it is the fixer for double-green cards like Hungering Puppetbeast.
  • Secluded Starforge taps the artifacts as part of the cost, so activate it after the Heartwoods have produced mana and before combat.
