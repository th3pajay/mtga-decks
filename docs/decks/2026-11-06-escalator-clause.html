Naya power-up and tiered combo — pay more, get more

Overview

Escalator Clause is built on two mechanics that do the same thing from opposite directions. Power-up abilities are activated abilities on your creatures with a printed cost, and that cost drops by the creature’s own mana cost on the turn it entered — so a five-mana Hulk can pay three mana for its power-up on the turn you cast it. Tiered spells are cheap instants that offer a menu of additional costs: you pay the base cost plus exactly one of the listed modes, so the same one-mana card is a cantrip-sized trick early and a game-ending effect late.

The deck fuses them into a single curve of escalating payments. Power-up creatures grow themselves with +1/+1 counters, and Tifa’s Limit Break converts those counters into damage by doubling or tripling the creature’s power and toughness. Because Tifa’s costs {G} plus a chosen mode, the deck never needs to tap out for a sorcery-speed haymaker: the finisher is an instant that turns a 5/5 trampler into a 15/15 trampler after blocks.

Two things make the counters count. Knight of Wundagore grows every time any other creature gets a counter, and Graduation Day puts a counter on your board every time you point an instant at a creature — which every tiered spell in the deck does, including the protection spell. Inspiring Call then turns a board of counter creatures into a fistful of cards plus indestructible, which is how the deck reloads after a sweeper.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1–3, stack the board cheaply. Quicksilver, Brash Blur is free from your opening hand, Serpent Specialist trades up with deathtouch, and Knight of Wundagore starts growing off any counter you place. Hold Tifa’s Limit Break as a combat trick rather than a pump.
  2. Turns 3–5, convert mana into counters. Cast a power-up creature and immediately activate its ability in the same turn to claim the mana-cost discount. Volcanic Villain’s {5}{R} becomes {3} on the turn it lands; She-Hulk’s {4}{G}{G} becomes {1}{G}; Hulk’s {6}{R}{G} becomes {3}.
  3. Turns 5+, escalate to lethal. Point Tifa’s Limit Break at the biggest trampler, or animate Restless Prairie and triple it. Rogue’s Passage clears the last blocker. Inspiring Call refuels when the opponent stabilises.

Curve and mana

Nineteen one-drops is not a typo: nine are creatures and ten are tiered instants or cheap enchantments, so the deck functions on two mana. Fourteen green sources, eleven red and twelve white carry the three-colour shell, and the surveil duals filter into one of four Tifa’s Limit Breaks rather than merely fixing.

Gameplay strategy

Early game (turns 1–3)

Lead with Quicksilver on the battlefield (it is free from your opening hand) and Serpent Specialist. Do not spend Tifa’s Limit Break for +2/+2 unless the trade is essential. Against creature decks, hold Fire Magic as a one-sided sweeper — your deathtouch blockers survive it at the {2} tier more often than their board does.

Mid game (turns 4–6)

This is where the discount rule wins games. Sequence a five-drop and its power-up in the same turn: Hulk plus its own ability costs {3}{R}{G} plus {3} for eleven power of trample, and every other power-up you control drops by {3} while Hulk is out. She-Hulk plus her ability costs four mana plus {1}{G} for a 5/5 that keeps growing every turn you have spare mana.

The combo turn

  1. Have a trampler with counters — She-Hulk at 5/5, Hulk at 11/10, or Abomination at 5/5.
  2. Cast Tifa’s Limit Break paying {G} and the Meteor Strikes mode {2}: the creature’s power and toughness double. That is three mana for a doubling, at instant speed, after blocks are declared.
  3. With eight mana available, pay {G} plus Final Heaven {6}{G} instead: triple power and toughness. An 11/10 Hulk becomes 33/30 trample.
  4. If a chump blocker would still stop it, activate Rogue’s Passage ({4}, {T}) before combat.

Win condition 2: the Prairie

Restless Prairie is a real threat, not a mana sink. For {2}{G}{W} it becomes a 3/3 Llama, and its attack trigger pumps every other creature +1/+1. Animate it, then resolve Tifa’s Limit Break on it — it is a creature at that point, so Meteor Strikes makes it a 6/6 and Final Heaven a 9/9. Rogue’s Passage makes it unblockable. This line wins through board stalls, through creature removal aimed at your power-up creatures, and through graveyard-free games where nothing else connects.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: two or three lands including green, at least one one-drop creature, and either Tifa’s Limit Break or a three-drop power-up creature.
  • Keep on six: any hand with two lands, a one-drop and a tiered spell; the deck’s curve forgives a missing land far more than a missing play.
  • Ship: one-land hands, hands with no green source, and hands of only four-plus-drops. This deck wins by turning cheap mana into counters, not by top-decking.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • The discount only applies on the entry turn. “Reduce the cost by its mana cost if it entered this turn” means a Volcanic Villain cast on turn three powers up for {3} that turn, then costs the full {5}{R} forever after. Activate it the turn you cast it or accept the full price.
  • One power-up per creature, ever. Each power-up ability can be activated only once, so the counters are a one-shot investment per body; that is what makes Hulk’s {3} discount so valuable, since it lets a second creature power up in the same turn.
  • Tiered gives you exactly one additional mode. You cannot pay for Fira and Firaga on the same Fire Magic. Choose the mode that matches the mana you have.
  • Tifa’s Limit Break doubles the creature’s current power and toughness, so counters and Restless Prairie’s attack pump are multiplied, and a double strike counter doubles the damage again: Quicksilver at 2/2 with a double strike counter becomes 4/4 double strike for eight damage.
  • Graduation Day triggers on the cast, not the resolution. The counter goes on a creature you control even if the tiered spell is countered, and it triggers off Restoration Magic’s protection mode too.
  • Inspiring Call counts creatures with counters at resolution — power up first, then draw.
  • Restoration Magic’s Cure mode is one mana and protects a creature from removal; the Curaga mode covers your whole board for {3}{W} and is the answer to a sweeper.

Matchups

  • Aggro: you are the faster deck when you curve out, but your bodies are small. Serpent Specialist’s deathtouch and Fire Magic’s first tier are the equalisers; trade early and let Knight of Wundagore grow off the counters.
  • Midrange: the power-up discount lets you go over the top of their four-drops. Save Tifa’s Limit Break for a post-blocks blowout and keep Inspiring Call for their removal turn.
  • Control: your tiered spells are cheap enough to bait counters, and Restless Prairie plus Rogue’s Passage is a win condition that does not need a creature spell to resolve. Do not over-commit into a sweeper; hold Inspiring Call as the reload.
  • Combo: Thunder Magic’s eight-damage mode kills most combo creatures, and Fire Magic’s top tier clears token engines. Race with haste creatures while holding Restoration Magic for their removal.
