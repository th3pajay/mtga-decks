Anvil Chorus

Red / White / Blue (three colors) — Storied Dwarf equipment swarm with a token-into-counters payoff, built on The Hobbit set

Overview

Anvil Chorus is the Storied Dwarves build of The Hobbit set, and it is built on one loop: Equipment are artifacts, artifacts are also legendaries and Sagas fuel for Storied, and the same Equipment that turn on Storied are what Dáin Ironfoot, Dwarven Shortsword and The Lonely Mountain are counting.

The deck attacks from a base of eleven Equipment sources across its cheap bodies: Iron Hills Blacksmith and Dáin Ironfoot each create a colorless Axe equipment token on entry, Dwarven Shortsword arrives with a 2/2 red Dwarf token already attached to it, and Glamdring, Foe-hammer is a two-mana Equipment whose equip cost is payable by the same creatures that want the pump. Nothing in the deck costs more than four, and twenty-two of the thirty-seven nonlands cost two or less.

Storied is the engine switch. Controlling three or more artifacts, legendaries or Sagas gives an enduring story for the rest of the game, and the deck almost always gets there by turn three because two Axe tokens plus any of the six legendary Dwarves — or Belladonna Took, or Glamdring itself — already counts. Thorin Oakenshield turns that on into ward {1} for the entire board, saving the team from the first removal spell pointed at it. Óin the Brave becomes a hasty 2/3 that loots. Fíli the Pathfinder gives the whole team +1/+1 and makes a 2/2 red Dwarf token whenever a nontoken Dwarf enters. Kíli the Resourceful makes the first equip of each turn free.

Belladonna Took is the card that ties the two halves together. Every token the deck makes — Axe tokens, Dwarf tokens, the Shortsword’s 2/2 — triggers her, and the third trigger of a turn puts a +1/+1 counter on every creature you control. With Iron Hills Blacksmith and Fíli running, that is a real anthem on turn four, on top of the Equipment pumps.

Note on the set: The Hobbit set releases on Arena on 2026-08-14. Arena IDs are still empty for most of the set’s printings in the card database, so the import list below takes effect from that release date.

Approach

Game plan phases

Phase Turns What the deck is doing
Open 1 to 2 One threat a turn, all of them one or two mana: Dwarven Mauler is a 2/1 for one that makes every later equip cost {2} less, Bofur, Reliable Guardian is a lifelink blocker that can also save an Equipment with its adventure, and Iron Hills Blacksmith is a double striker that leaves an Axe behind
Build 3 to 4 Three artifacts or legendaries means Storied is online, so Thorin Oakenshield’s ward, Óin the Brave’s haste and Kíli the Resourceful’s free equip all switch on. Fíli the Pathfinder and Dáin Ironfoot arrive and the board doubles in size
Alpha 4 to 5 Attach everything that is not already attached with Vow to Erebor and Kíli’s free equip, then attack with Dáin Ironfoot on the board: every equipped attacker has double strike
Close 5 plus The Lonely Mountain starts converting spare mana into 2/2 Dwarf tokens while Belladonna Took turns each of them into life, cards and counters, which is the fallback when the Equipment plan is answered

Two mana is where the deck lives, and Dáin Ironfoot at three is the only card that demands a specific colour pair. Four-land probability by turn four is 0.561 and turn-four consistency is 0.575, both above the 0.55 threshold the build is held to. Twenty-three lands is deliberate: the deck wants to hit four lands on time for Fíli the Pathfinder and Dwarven Shortsword, and then it stops wanting lands, because extra mana goes into The Lonely Mountain’s token ability.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are for bodies, not for tricks. Dwarven Mauler is the best one-drop because its {2} equip discount applies to every Equipment you attach to it later, which is what makes Dwarven Shortsword’s {2} equip and Glamdring’s {2} equip effectively free. Iron Hills Blacksmith on turn two is the strongest play in the deck: it is a double striker, and its Axe token is an artifact that counts for Storied, an Equipment that counts for The Lonely Mountain, and a Belladonna Took trigger.

Do not hold Vow to Erebor waiting for a perfect turn. It is a two-mana way to untap a creature, give it +2/+2 and attach an Equipment, and attaching at instant speed after attackers are declared is often two extra damage on a first-strike or double-strike blocker.

Mid game

Turn three is when the deck decides the game. Dáin Ironfoot on an empty board is an Axe, an attacker, and the double-strike clause waiting to be used. Fíli the Pathfinder on turn four usually makes a token immediately, because it triggers on its own entry, and then again for every other nontoken Dwarf after that.

Storied should be checked every turn. The count is artifacts, legendaries and Sagas: two Axe tokens plus any legendary Dwarf is three, which means Thorin Oakenshield’s ward {1} for the team, Óin the Brave’s haste and Kíli the Resourceful’s free equip are all live from turn three onwards. Ward {1} on everything is what keeps a four-creature Equipment board alive through a single-target removal spell.

The combo turn

The kill turn needs Dáin Ironfoot, two or more Equipment, and at least three attackers.

  1. Play Dáin Ironfoot if he is not already on the board. Each Axe he makes attaches immediately.
  2. Attach the spare Equipment with Kíli the Resourceful’s free equip, then with Vow to Erebor on the creature that is not yet equipped. Every attached Equipment is a point of power, and Dwarven Mauler makes that attach cost {2} less.
  3. Attack with everything. Dáin Ironfoot’s attack trigger gives each equipped attacking creature double strike.
  4. Order Nori, Teller of Tales’ first-strike grant on the largest unequipped attacker, or on the Axe-carrying token, so the first-strike damage happens before the regular damage step.
  5. After combat, activate The Lonely Mountain for {4}{R} reduced by one per Equipment to make another 2/2 Dwarf token, which triggers Belladonna Took.

Two Axes, one Dwarven Shortsword and a Glamdring on a board of four creatures is twelve or more points of double-strike damage from a curve that topped out at four mana. If the attack does not kill, the deck is still wide, and the tokens left behind trigger Belladonna Took again on the following turn.

Win condition 2

The token plan does not need Dáin Ironfoot at all. Fíli the Pathfinder makes a 2/2 red Dwarf token for each nontoken Dwarf that enters after it, Dwarven Shortsword makes another, and The Lonely Mountain turns a heavily discounted {4}{R} activation — one mana less for each Equipment you control — into one more per turn. Belladonna Took turns each of those tokens into 1 life, then a card, then a +1/+1 counter on every creature you control — so the third token of a turn is effectively an anthem plus a free card.

Against a deck that answers Equipment, the correct line is to stop attaching and start making tokens: keep Fíli the Pathfinder and one Equipment in play for Storied, and let The Lonely Mountain plus Belladonna Took win through counters. Against a deck with many cheap blockers, the opposite is true: attach everything to two creatures and use Dáin Ironfoot’s double strike on a board that is only two attackers wide, because doublestrike damage does not care how many defenders there are.

Mulligan guide

Hand shape Verdict
Two lands, Dwarven Mauler and Iron Hills Blacksmith Keep. This is the deck’s ideal opening: a double striker on turn two and a discount on every later equip
Two lands, Belladonna Took and any two Equipment sources Keep. Tokens arrive from turn two onward and she converts the third one into counters
One land, no one-drop Mulligan. The deck needs three mana before Dáin Ironfoot or Fíli the Pathfinder matter
Three lands, Dáin Ironfoot and one Equipment Keep. Dáin plus any Axe is the double-strike turn
Five lands, Glamdring and one two-drop Mulligan. Too much mana for a deck that stops at four, and blue is a splash
Any hand with two Plains and a Mountain Keep. That is the deck’s red-white core, and Multiversal Passage or Fields of Strife fixes the rest

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
Storied is permanent once online The condition is checked when it becomes true; an enduring story lasts the rest of the game even if you later control fewer than three artifacts, legendaries or Sagas. Axe tokens dying to a board wipe does not switch Thorin Oakenshield’s ward off
Axe tokens are Equipment, not creatures Iron Hills Blacksmith and Dáin Ironfoot create colorless Axe equipment tokens with “equipped creature gets +1/+0” and equip {2}. They count for Storied, for The Lonely Mountain’s discount and for Belladonna Took’s token triggers, but they cannot attack on their own
Belladonna Took’s three triggers The life, card and counter clauses trigger once each per turn, in that order. The counters clause is the one worth planning around: hold the third token of the turn, ideally The Lonely Mountain’s or Dwarven Shortsword’s, until after combat so the counters are not wasted
Dáin Ironfoot’s double strike is a trigger, not a static ability It happens when he attacks and it applies to attacking creatures that are equipped at that moment. Attach with Kíli the Resourceful’s free equip and with Vow to Erebor in the declare-attackers step, not after
Kíli the Resourceful’s free equip The discount applies only with an enduring story, only to the first equip ability you activate each turn, and only to the equip cost — not to other activated abilities of the Equipment. His draw trigger is once per turn as well, so play him before the Equipment, not after
Dwarven Mauler’s discount Equip abilities you activate that target the Mauler cost {2} less. It applies to any Equipment, including Axe tokens, and it is what makes a three-mana equip playable on a two-drop
Thorin Oakenshield and ward {1} The ward applies to artifacts and creatures you control, Axe tokens included, and it is applied to each permanent separately: an opponent answering a single Creature has to pay {1} or lose the spell
Vow to Erebor’s attach clause The +2/+2 and untap always happen; the free attach only happens if the target is a Dwarf. Casting it on a token Dwarf from Dwarven Shortsword is the best mode because the token is already the Equipment’s natural carrier
Glamdring’s cost reduction X is the equipped creature’s power, so the reduction applies to instants and sorceries only, not to the creature side of the deck, and it does nothing while Glamdring is unattached
The Lonely Mountain’s activation It is sorcery-speed, {4}{R} minus {1} per Equipment you control, and it makes a token, not a land — it is the deck’s only repeatable way to trigger Belladonna Took without spending a card

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain counters Race with double strike and do not over-commit Equipment. Bofur, Reliable Guardian blocks with lifelink, Asgardian Citadel and Stark Industries gain 1 life each, and Thorin Oakenshield’s ward {1} makes their single-target removal awkward. Block with the Axe-carrying token rather than a real Dwarf
Midrange The strong matchup. Dáin Ironfoot’s double strike beats one big blocker because first-strike damage lands first, and Nori, Teller of Tales gives first strike to whichever attacker is blocked. Belladonna Took’s counters make the token plan bigger than their blockers turn by turn
Control The weak matchup. Board wipes take the Equipment and the tokens with them, so keep Fíli the Pathfinder plus one token source in hand, and remember that Storied survives the wipe even when the artifacts do not. Glamdring’s Gleam of Death refills the hand after a wipe by milling six and returning every instant and sorcery
Combo The deck’s clock is turn four or five, which is faster than most combo decks in the format, so the plan is to attack every turn and force them to spend their turn interacting. Equip one creature rather than four so a single removal spell does not turn off the double strike
