Overview

Deathwatch is a Jund deck built on a simple promise: every creature that dies this turn is a resource. The two rolled mechanics are fused into one engine — sacrifice makes things die on demand, and morbid pays you at the end of each step in which anything died. The result is a deck that wants the same thing on every single turn: make a creature die, then collect.

The engine has three layers:

  • Death payoffs. Venerated Stormsinger and Cauldron of Essence both drain 1 life per creature that dies — with both on the table, a single sacrificed token costs the opponent 2 life and gains you 2.
  • Morbid end-step rewards. Needletooth Pack puts two +1/+1 counters on a creature, Cackling Prowler grows itself, Wardens of the Cycle draws or gains life, Slumbering Cerberus untaps, and Tragic Banshee arrives as a one-sided -13/-13 wipe. Each of them checks simply “did a creature die this turn” — your own sacrifices count, and so do your opponent’s trades.
  • Sacrifice fuel and recursion. Greed's Gambit bursts out three flying Bats plus three cards and six life; Lightless Evangel and Pirate Peddlers convert every sacrifice into +1/+1 counters; Cauldron of Essence and Sidisi, Regent of the Mire return dead creatures from the graveyard so the same bodies can die again.

The deck never needs a single big combo turn. It grinds a death toll: tokens attack, tokens get sacrificed at end step, drains tick, and the morbid engines put counters on whatever survives. Because the morbid checks fire at end step, the growth lands after combat — creatures attack first, die or trade, then come back bigger.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Establish the toll (turns 1–3): Lightless Evangel on turn 2 starts collecting counters from day one. Greed's Gambit on turn 3 draws three, gains six life, and puts three 2/1 flying Bats into play — instant fodder and early damage.
  2. Double the drain (turns 3–5): resolve Venerated Stormsinger and Cauldron of Essence so every death costs 2 life. Play the morbid engines — Needletooth Pack and Cackling Prowler — and start sacrificing at end step: one bat dies, two drains fire, Pack adds two counters to a survivor.
  3. Close the toll (turns 5+): sac and recur — Cauldron or Sidisi brings back the best body each turn so it can die again. Tragic Banshee wipes a single blocker with -13/-13. Swings become lethal as counters pile up and The Huntsman's Redemption hands out trample.

Curve and mana

The pile has no one-drops: it curves 2, 3, 4, 5 and never stalls on color — eleven black pips and ten green pips across the two-drops are covered by nineteen black and thirteen green sources. Red only needs eight sources for the two {1}{R} cards and Vents activations. Twenty-four lands with four each of Marsh, Tomb and Vents keeps the P(4 lands by turn 4) comfortably above the battery floor.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lightless Evangel on two is the ideal start; every future sacrifice makes it a real threat. Slumbering Cerberus is the aggressive alternative — a 4/2 that keeps untapping as long as creatures are dying, which in this deck is every turn. Hold Betrayer's Bargain for anything with more than 5 toughness or that needs exiling.

Mid game

Resolve the drain pair. Attack with Stormsinger to create the Mobilize Warrior (it dies at the start of your next end step, so it drains once on arrival turn if you sacrifice it). Greed’s Gambit Bats attack as evasive 2/1s, then one is sacrificed each end step — ordering your end-step triggers matters: let Gambit’s sacrifice resolve first, then the morbid triggers see the death and resolve. A typical late end step with Gambit, Stormsinger and Cauldron reads: sacrifice a Bat — opponent loses 2, you gain 2; Needletooth Pack puts two counters on a survivor; Wardens draws you a card.

The combo turn

The best turns chain sacrifice into recursion into more sacrifice: tap Cauldron of Essence to sacrifice a 1/1 Bat and return a 2-drop from the graveyard; untap-and-repeat next turn, or use Sidisi as a second ladder — sacrifice the Bat (mana value 1) to return a mana-value-2 creature, sacrifice that to return a 3-drop like Stormsinger, and keep climbing. Every rung of the ladder drains if the drain pieces are out. The Huntsman's Redemption chapter II turns any spare sacrifice into a tutor for the exact engine piece missing.

Win condition 2

If the drain is answered, the morbid growth plan takes over. Needletooth Pack adds two counters to Evangel or Peddlers every end step; Cackling Prowler grows itself behind ward {2}. After two or three dying turns these are 8/8s or bigger, and Huntsman chapter III gives two of them trample. Restless Vents joins as a 2/3 menace that cannot be chump-blocked by small utility creatures and rummages for more gas on contact.

Mulligan guide

Keep: any hand with a two-drop plus a sacrifice outlet or drain piece; Greed’s Gambit hands; hands with Cauldron or Stormsinger plus any creature. Ship: no-lander hands, hands with only five-drops, and hands with Betrayer’s Bargain as the only spell — removal does nothing until the engine is ticking. Four lands and three spells is the target shape.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Morbid checks “a creature died this turn” — any creature, including your opponent’s. Trades and their removal feed Pack, Prowler and Wardens for free.
  • End-step ordering: with Greed’s Gambit and morbid triggers waiting, resolve Gambit’s sacrifice first so the death is visible to the morbid checks. If nothing has died yet that turn, Gambit’s own end-step sacrifice is the trigger that makes the others fire.
  • Betrayer's Bargain exiles the creature instead of letting it die — the right call against recursion decks, but it will not feed morbid.
  • The Mobilize Warrior from Stormsinger is sacrificed at the beginning of your next end step, so a Warrior created this turn drains only if you sacrifice it yourself now.
  • Sidisi cannot sacrifice itself and needs another creature of mana value X; a Bat (1) chains to a 2-drop, a Warrior (1) to a 2-drop, an Evangel (2) up to a 3-drop.
  • If Cauldron or Gambit leaves play, the engine keeps running on combat deaths — don’t over-commit all recursion to one basket.
