Plot Twist

Simic (Blue / Green) — Plot

Overview

Plot lets you pay a cost up front and exile a card face down, then cast it on a later turn without paying its mana cost. Every threat in Plot Twist is a plot card, so mana spent in the early turns buys a whole second wave of free spells. A plotted Freestrider Commando was never cast, so it enters with two extra counters as a 5/5; a plotted Djinn of Fool’s Fall costs four mana today and flies in free tomorrow.

The payoff is Railway Brawler: whenever another creature you control enters, it gets +1/+1 counters equal to its power. With a Brawler on the battlefield a 5/5 Freestrider enters and is immediately made 10/10; a second Brawler via Visage Bandit doubles the triggers, and Tumbleweed Rising turns the board’s biggest power into a token that the Brawler then doubles again. Fblthp, Lost on the Range is the engine that keeps it going — his plot means the top card of your library can be banked face down for free, and Outcaster Trailblazer and Beastbond Outcaster turn every big entry into cards and mana.

Curve and mana

Twenty-four lands: eleven basics and thirteen UG fixing or utility lands, with the two land win conditions in the mix. Green is the engine color — Freestrider, Brawler and the Outcasters — so it is deepest (six Forests plus eleven green dual sources). Blue feeds Fblthp, the Djinn and the countermagic. The deck is a “plot curve”: the three- and four-drop slots pay plot costs today and the five-drops arrive free, which is why the average nonland mana cost of 3.6 plays much faster than it reads.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Fblthp on three is the best opener: from then on the top card of the library can be plotted for its mana cost, banking extra gas that discard and disruption cannot touch. Otherwise plot a Freestrider Commando on three, a Djinn or Brawler on four. Hold Essence Scatter for their key creature and Spell Pierce for sweepers and value spells; the deck wins by keeping one threat alive into the Brawler turn.

Mid game

Start the free-cast wave. A plotted Freestrider enters as 5/5 (not cast, so it gets its two counters), triggering Beastbond Outcaster for a card. Resolve Railway Brawler, and every creature from then on arrives with its own power in counters: the next Freestrider is 10/10, a Djinn is 8/7 flying. Trailblazer turns each of those entries into another draw, and the hand never runs out.

The combo turn

With a Brawler on the battlefield, cast Visage Bandit as a copy of it — the original Brawler immediately makes the copy a 10/10, and now two Brawlers are live. Cast Tumbleweed Rising: the token is X/X where X is your biggest power (10 or more), and each Brawler trigger adds counters equal to its power as it resolves, so the token lands closer to 30/30. Attack with everything; reach and trample cover the air and the ground, and Rogue’s Passage punches the biggest body through for lethal. Against a single giant blocker, attack with Restless Vinestalk first and shrink that blocker to 3/3 before damage.

Win condition 2

The deck does not need the token kill: two or three free-cast threats behind a Brawler are already a lethal board. Djinn of Fool’s Fall flies over ground stalls, Spinewoods Paladin gains life and tramples through, and Restless Vinestalk is a repeatable 5/5 trample that can be activated every turn, doubling as a blocker that never dies to sorcery-speed removal. Plan the Heist keeps the engine stocked and Make Your Own Luck refills the plot bank from the top of the library.

Mulligan guide

Keep two to four lands with any engine piece: Fblthp, a plot creature, or an Outcaster plus one big plot card. Ship one-land openers, hands with three or more five-cost cards and no plot outlet, and hands without a turn-two or turn-three play. Against aggro favor Spinewoods Paladin and Essence Scatter; against control keep Fblthp and the fastest plot curve.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Freestrider Commando enters with its two counters whenever it was not cast or no mana was spent to cast it — a plotted cast spends no mana, so it is a 5/5. Casting it normally from hand makes it a plain 3/3, so always plot it first.
  • Railway Brawler puts counters equal to the entering creature’s power as the trigger resolves, so creatures entering while other buffs are in play grow even larger. Two Brawlers (Visage Bandit copy) trigger twice, and each trigger sees the counters the previous one added.
  • Tumbleweed Rising’s X is locked in as the token is created; the Brawler triggers then pile on top of that size.
  • Fblthp’s plot works from the top of the library, not the hand: he does not discard, he banks. Plot only as a sorcery, and only nonland cards.
  • A plotted card is cast “from exile without paying its mana cost” — it still counts as a spell, so it triggers Trailblazer’s draws, flies into Essence Scatter, and counts for the Djinn and counters that care about casts.
  • Restless Vinestalk’s attack trigger can target any creature, including your opponent’s biggest blocker (base 3/3 until end of turn) or your own small creature to give it a passable body.
  • Breeding Pool can enter untapped for 2 life; with a 24-land mana base it is worth the life early and unneeded late.
  • Spell Pierce does not stop creatures — hold it for the sweepers and planeswalkers that threaten a developed board.

Matchups

  • Aggro (fliers, lifegain, cats): Essence Scatter and early plot creatures trade while Spinewoods Paladin gains life; a Brawler-backed 10/10 Freestrider races anything they play.
  • Midrange (counters, landfall): Trailblazer and the Outcasters out-draw them; every post-Brawler creature is a two-for-one.
  • Control: bank plots to play around sweepers — cards in the plot zone survive board wipes — then unload three or four free threats in one turn.
  • Combo and big mana: the deck is the aggressor; point countermagic at their engine and end through Rogue’s Passage before they go over the top.
