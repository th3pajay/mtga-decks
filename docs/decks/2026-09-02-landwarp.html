Overview

Landwarp fuses three mechanics into one combat engine. Landfall turns every land drop into power and board growth (Tifa Lockhart doubles her power, Mossborn Hydra doubles its counters, Mightform Harmonizer doubles any creature’s power, Eusocial Engineering and Felidar Retreat add bodies). Warp is the recursion half: every warp card can be cast from hand for a discount, is exiled at the next end step, and can be cast again from exile on a later turn, so engines like Mightform Harmonizer and Eusocial Engineering keep returning. Sneak is the surprise-attack half: when you have an unblocked attacker, you return it to hand during the declare blockers step and cast a Sneak card for its sneak cost — creatures enter tapped and attacking, which is how Michelangelo, Improviser lands on the board mid-combat and starts dumping lands and creatures from your hand.

The three mechanics reinforce each other. Michelangelo’s combat damage trigger plays an extra land and an extra creature, which feeds Landfall payoffs on the same turn they attack. Raphael, the Nightwatcher gives all attackers double strike, so a Landfall-doubled Tifa or Hydra ends games in one swing. Dark Leo and Shredder turn the ninja count into a second, independent kill: every combat with five or more Ninjas (turtles and spirit tokens included) makes the opponent lose half their remaining life.

Approach

Game plan by phase

Early game (T1-T3). Develop: Sazh’s Chocobo, Tifa Lockhart, Splinter (menace gives you unblocked attackers), and an engine (Mightform Harmonizer, Eusocial Engineering via warp, Felidar Retreat). Play a land every turn; every landfall trigger is a pump or a token. Hold combat tricks (Shredder’s Technique) for must-kill blockers.

Mid game (T4-T5). Start the sneak turns. Attack with a menace ninja or an evasive token; if it goes unblocked, return it to hand during declare blockers and cast a Sneak card for its cost: Michelangelo (drops land and creature from hand), Oroku Saki (draws), Dark Leo and Shredder (makes a Ninja token), or The Last Ronin’s Technique (three Spirit tokens, tapped and attacking when sneaked).

The combo turn. With Michelangelo and Raphael plus a landfall engine in play: attack, sneak Michelangelo in tapped and attacking, his damage trigger puts a land and a creature from hand onto the battlefield, the land triggers every Landfall payoff (Tifa, Hydra, Harmonizer, Eusocial robots, Felidar cat), Raphael makes the whole team double strike, and Restless Cottage or a Robot token closes.

Curve and mana

The nonland curve averages 3.1 CMC (1.9 counting lands), with most expensive pieces playable at a discount: warp costs run one to three mana below the printed cost, and sneak costs are even cheaper on a developed board. The manabase is 24 lands: eleven basics for the verge conditions, six verge duals covering every color pair, Restless Cottage, and four fetch lands that double as landfall triggers. New Generation’s Technique (sneak {2}{G}) is effectively a fifth ramp spell that fetches two basics tapped and fires two landfall triggers.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Prioritize land drops over everything: Sazh’s Chocobo and Tifa Lockhart grow on every landfall, so the first three turns are about curving lands and cheap green threats. Splinter, Hamato Yoshi is the best two-drop: menace is your most reliable source of unblocked attackers for sneak costs, and it pumps the other Ninjas. If the opener has a warp engine (Mightform Harmonizer or Eusocial Engineering), warp-cast it cheaply: it will be exiled at end step and come back next turn, giving a second round of landfall triggers.

Mid game

Set up the kill: keep an engine on the board, hold a land in hand for Michelangelo, and attack with the ninja package. Do not overextend into sweepers; All-Fates Stalker and Shredder’s Technique keep the opponent’s blockers and bombs honest, and Splinter’s Technique fetches exactly the piece the board state needs.

The combo turn

  1. Attack with a ninja or menace creature.
  2. If it is unblocked, return it to hand in the declare blockers step and cast Michelangelo, Improviser for its sneak cost {2}{G}{G}; he enters tapped and attacking.
  3. Michelangelo’s combat damage lets you put a creature card and/or a land card from hand onto the battlefield.
  4. The extra land drop fires every Landfall ability: Tifa and Hydra double, Mightform doubles another creature’s power, Eusocial makes a Robot, Felidar Retreat makes a Cat or pumps the team.
  5. With Raphael, the Nightwatcher also attacking, all attackers have double strike; Restless Cottage and Robots add the final points.

Win condition 2: the ninja swarm

Dark Leo and Shredder creates a 1/1 Ninja token every time it deals combat damage, and each such trigger checks the ninja count: if you control five or more Ninjas, the opponent loses half their life, rounded up. Turtles and Splinter are Ninjas, The Last Ronin’s Technique’s three Spirits are Ninja Turtle Spirits, and every Dark Leo hit adds another token, so two solid combat steps with the swarm end the game regardless of the landfall math.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two or three lands and a cheap threat (Sazh’s Chocobo, Tifa, Splinter). Keep hands with a warp engine plus enough lands to cast it or warp it by turn three. Ship hands that are all four-drops or missing color sources for their early cards; the deck wants to use its lands every turn.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Sneak requires an unblocked attacker: if your only attacker is blocked, you cannot pay sneak costs that turn. Menace (Splinter) and evasive tokens are the safety valves.
  • Warped permanents are exiled at the beginning of the next end step and can be cast from exile later, so warp is not a one-shot discount: it is a repeatable engine, and warp costs can be paid again each turn.
  • All-Fates Stalker exiles a non-Assassin creature until it leaves the battlefield; warp-casting it each turn gives repeatable exile removal.
  • Michelangelo’s trigger is optional (“you may”), and you can choose only a creature, only a land, or both.
  • The Last Ronin’s Technique’s Spirits enter tapped and attacking only when the sneak cost was paid; cast normally they are just three chump-blockers/attackers.
  • Weftstalker Ardent pings each opponent for every creature or artifact that enters under your control, so token-making turns (Eusocial Robots, Felidar Cats, Dark Leo Ninjas, Last Ronin Spirits) double as reach.
  • Shredder’s Technique destroys enchantments too, and you lose 2 life only if it destroys an enchantment.

Matchups

Against aggro, block early with Felidar Retreat Cats and Sazh’s Chocobo, and use Shredder’s Technique and Jennika-style tempo to stabilize; lifegain from Restless Cottage Food and the Cat token race matters. Against midrange, warp recursion out-values them: engines keep coming back from exile while their one-for-one removal runs dry. Against control, spread the clock: Ninja tokens and Restless Cottage survive sweepers as lands, Splinter’s Technique finds the finisher, and Raphael plus double strike punishes tapped-out turns. The deck’s weakness is fast combo and wide flyer strategies, where the manabase’s tapped fetch lands cost a tempo turn.
