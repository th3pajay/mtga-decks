Undertow Armada

Blue / Black / Red / Green — Amass × Disappear × Waterbending: a token-sacrifice engine that pays for its spells by tapping its own board

At a glance

Attribute Value
Format Standard (MTGA)
Colors Blue / Black / Red / Green (four-color, black base)
Archetype Token-sacrifice combo-midrange (avg spell CMC 2.9)
Size 60 cards, 24 lands
Primary win One Goblin Army token, grown by every Amass trigger, made unblockable by Rogue’s Passage
Secondary win Bloodthirsty Conqueror plus Enduring Tenacity: any life gain loops into infinite opponent life loss
Land win con Rogue’s Passage (unblockable Army); Fabled Passage is also a Disappear trigger, since cracking it is a permanent leaving the battlefield

Overview

Three mechanics share one currency: permanents you control leaving the battlefield. Amass builds a single Goblin Army token out of +1/+1 counters, Disappear rewards you at end of turn whenever a permanent left the battlefield under your control, and Waterbend lets you pay generic costs by tapping your own creatures and artifacts rather than with mana.

The glue is the graveyard-to-hand-and-back loop. Along the Crooked Way amasses a Goblin every time a creature card leaves your graveyard, and Insidious Roots makes a 0/1 Plant with “{T}: Add one mana of any color” on the same event. Rat King, Verminister returns a creature card and every copy of it from the graveyard to the battlefield, so one recursion spell pays you twice: Goblin counters and Plant tokens. Those Plants and Rats are then the tap-payment for Waterbend costs and the sacrifice fodder for Disappear, Rhovanion Rampager, Lord Dregg and West Wind Avatar.

The result is a deck that never runs out of bodies. Every sacrifice is a Disappear trigger, every recursion is an Amass trigger, and every cheap noncreature spell is another Amass trigger off Bothersome Noisemaker.

Approach

Game plan phases

Phase What happens
Turns 1-3 Land a Goblin: Tidings of War or Flunkies to start the Army, Bothersome Noisemaker to tax every later spell. Rhovanion Rampager or Azog clears the road.
Turns 3-5 Build the token board: Rat King and Along the Crooked Way turn the graveyard into Rats and counters, Insidious Roots turns recursion into mana Plants. Sacrifice something small every turn to keep Disappear live.
Turns 5-7 Convert: tap the board for Waterbend to cast above curve, suit up the Army, and either push it through Rogue’s Passage or assemble Conqueror plus Tenacity.

Curve and mana

Twenty-four lands, thirty-two spells, average spell mana value 2.9. The curve is deliberately bottom-heavy: twenty-seven cards cost three or less, and only two cards cost five or more, both finishers rather than plan. Black is the primary color with twenty-three black or hybrid cards; red is a two-drop splash (Noisemaker, Flunkies, Tidings), blue is a three-drop splash (Katara, North Pole Patrol, Waterbending Lesson), green is the recursion and token color (Roots, Putrid Pals, West Wind Avatar). Because Waterbend converts spare creatures into generic mana, the deck functions on three lands far more often than the four-color frame suggests.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with the cheapest Army builder. A turn-one Tidings of War is not wasted: it grows the Army, and its Flashback later Amasses 3 for four mana. Bothersome Noisemaker is the better two-drop when your hand holds removal or Waterbend spells, because each one adds a counter. Hold Azog until there is a creature worth killing, since Amass scales with the dead creature’s power.

Mid game

From turn three the plan is to make two permanents leave the battlefield every turn cycle: one sacrificed, one cracked or reanimated. Rhovanion Rampager attacking into a spare Rat or Plant is the cleanest repeatable sacrifice, and it triggers Disappear while growing itself. Rat King converts dead creatures into a Rat per turn, and three Rats later become a full reanimation.

The combo turn

The primary kill is arithmetic, not a loop. Count the Army’s counters, add Rogue’s Passage’s four mana, and attack for the whole total unblocked. Fearsome Goblin Pair dying into Amass 4, Rhovanion Rampager dying into Amass equal to its power, and a flashed-back Tidings for Amass 3 routinely add eight to twelve counters in a single turn. Waterbend those costs by tapping Plants and Rats in your second main phase, after attackers are declared, so the Army stays free to swing.

Win condition 2

Bloodthirsty Conqueror plus Enduring Tenacity is a closed loop: Tenacity turns any life gain into opponent life loss, Conqueror turns opponent life loss back into your life gain. One point of lifegain is enough to start it, and this deck has many: Foot Mystic connecting, West Wind Avatar sacrificing a token, Ruinous Waterbending’s Waterbent death trigger, or simply a Vengeful Bloodwitch watching a token die. Both pieces are single copies, so treat them as a bonus line rather than a plan; Waterbending Lesson and Tidings of War’s Flashback dig toward them.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands that include a black source, one cheap Goblin, and one payoff. A hand of all fixing and no engine is a mulligan even at four colors. Do not keep one-land hands hoping for Waterbend, because there is nothing to tap. Six-card hands are fine when they contain Bothersome Noisemaker plus two lands, since the Noisemaker converts every subsequent spell into counters.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Disappear checks the whole turn, not the moment: sacrifice during combat and the trigger still fires at end of turn.
  • Cracking Fabled Passage, sacrificing a Map or a Food, and West Wind Avatar eating a spare land all count as permanents leaving the battlefield.
  • There is only ever one Army. Extra Amass triggers add counters to it, so never trade it away in combat; if it dies, Rhovanion Rampager’s death trigger is the cheapest way to rebuild.
  • Waterbend taps creatures and artifacts for {1} each. Tapping the Army removes it from combat, so pay Waterbend costs in the second main phase.
  • Along the Crooked Way and Insidious Roots trigger on creature cards leaving the graveyard, not on spells. Rat King’s mass reanimation is the biggest single trigger in the deck.
  • Enduring Tenacity returns as an enchantment when it dies, so the loop survives one removal spell; exile effects are the real answer.
  • Katara only grows if she is tapped at end of turn, so attack or Waterbend with her rather than leaving her back.

Matchups

Against aggro, Putrid Pals and Foot Mystic trade up while Ruinous Waterbending sweeps a board of one- and two-toughness attackers and starts the lifegain chain. Against control, the deck wins through repetition: every countered engine piece is one more card in the graveyard for Rat King, and the Army survives most sweepers because it is a single large token with more than three toughness. Graveyard hate is the difficult axis, since it shuts off Along the Crooked Way, Insidious Roots and Rat King at once, so lean on the Goblin beatdown plan with Fearsome Goblin Pair and West Wind Avatar if that happens. Against combo, the clock is fast enough that Azog and Rhovanion Rampager should be aimed at the opponent’s creatures rather than held for value.
