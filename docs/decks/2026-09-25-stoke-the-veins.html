Overview

Stoke the Veins fuses the two mechanics rolled for the day, Firebending and Infusion, into one turn cycle instead of running them side by side. Firebending pays red mana when a creature attacks, and that mana expires at end of combat. Infusion upgrades a spell only if you gained life this turn. The deck is built so that the same attack step delivers both halves: Pests and Teacher’s Pest gain life as they attack, firebenders add red in the same window, and the red is immediately spent on Infusion instants that are already switched on.

Infusion is the payoff side. Lumaret’s Favor targets one creature for +2/+4 and copies itself when you have gained life, which is +4/+8 across two bodies for two mana. Efflorescence puts two +1/+1 counters on a creature and, with life gained, grants trample and indestructible. Foolish Fate destroys a blocker and drains 3. Follow the Lumarets keeps the engine fed by digging two creature or land cards deep. Every one of those cards is a full-rate card on a turn where a Pest has attacked, and a half-rate card on a turn where it has not, which is exactly why the deck runs twelve lifegain triggers.

Firebending is the mana side. Firebending Student, Fire Sages and Vindictive Warden each add red when they attack, and Firebending Student adds mana equal to its power, so prowess triggers and pump spells convert directly into more mana in later combats. That red is what pays for the Infusion instants during combat, for Vindictive Warden’s {3} activations, and for the generic half of every pump spell.

The bridge between the halves is the Pest. Moseo, Vein’s New Dean and Send in the Pest both create 1/1 Pests with “whenever this token attacks, you gain 1 life”, and Teacher’s Pest gains life on its own attacks. Those triggers resolve in the same declare-attackers window as the firebending triggers, so the deck can order its own stack: gain the life first, add the red second, then cast the doubled spell with both conditions satisfied. Pest Mascot and Blech, Loafing Pest turn every one of those lifegain triggers into +1/+1 counters, so the board grows as a by-product of casting spells that were going to be cast anyway.

Curve and mana

Twenty-four lands support a curve where twenty-one of the thirty-six nonland cards cost two or less. The deck wants two coloured sources by turn two and three by turn four, which the twelve untapped black and green sources plus the eight lifegain taplands deliver. Red is the splash colour for the firebenders, and every red card in the list is castable at two or three mana, or in the case of Vindictive Warden, with black mana through the {B/R} hybrid symbol. Green is the Infusion colour: Lumaret’s Favor, Efflorescence and Follow the Lumarets are all green, so the manabase keeps nine green sources without slowing the black-red core.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a tapland that gains life, because turning Infusion on for free is worth more than the tempo. Turn two is Teacher’s Pest, Bogwater Lumaret or Firebending Student. Turn three is the pivot: attack with whatever has summoning sickness clear, gain life from the Pest, then either cast Lumaret’s Favor on the attacking creature before blockers are declared or hold it for a later combat where the copy matters more. Using the first Infusion spell as a two-for-one on two attackers is the goal, not a one-for-one pump.

The combo turn

The engine turn is one declare-attackers window and it resolves in a fixed order.

Step Action
Declare attackers Attack with every Pest, Teacher’s Pest, firebender and Pest Mascot that can profit
Stack the attack triggers Put the firebending triggers on the stack first and the lifegain triggers on top, so the life is gained before the red arrives
Resolve Pest and Teacher’s Pest triggers gain 1 life each, Bogwater Lumaret triggers add on any creature entry, and then each firebender adds its red, all lasting until end of combat
Spend With life gained and red floating, cast Lumaret’s Favor (copied for +4/+8), Efflorescence (counters plus trample and indestructible), Foolish Fate (destroy and drain 3) or Follow the Lumarets, or activate Vindictive Warden at {3} per damage
Damage and end step An indestructible trampler ends the game or survives it; Moseo’s Infusion trigger then returns a creature with mana value up to the life gained this turn

The sequencing rule is absolute: firebending mana empties at end of combat, so every Infusion spell and every Vindictive Warden activation has to be spent inside the combat phase. Do not plan to use that red in the second main phase.

Win condition 2: the drain line

When the board stalls, the deck stops needing combat damage. Fire Nation Palace grants firebending 4 to a single creature for {1}{R}, and a creature with firebending 4 adds four red mana on attack. Four red pays a Vindictive Warden activation with one mana to spare, so with two Wardens on the board the Palace alone converts one attack into 2 damage to each opponent, or 6 damage across three attackers. Foolish Fate adds 3 more life loss every time it is cast with Infusion on, and the Pest tokens keep attacking for chip damage. This line also sidesteps blockers entirely: Palace mana is paid before damage, and Rogue’s Passage takes the ground game out of the equation when the drain is not enough.

Mulligan guide

Keep Ship
Two or three lands with a Pest or Firebending Student and one Infusion spell One-land hands without a tapland that gains life
Any hand with Moseo, Vein’s New Dean plus two lands: the Pest it makes is both a lifegain trigger and a chip attacker Hands of only payoffs and pumps with no lifegain source: Infusion spells at half rate lose to everything
Hands with a lifegain tapland, Teacher’s Pest and Lumaret’s Favor, even with a single Forest in play Fives and sixes of lands, or Vindictive Warden plus removal with no threat to pressure

The deck wins on turn five or six, so a hand without a two-drop is a mulligan even if the colours are perfect; a hand with two lands, a Pest and a pump is a keep even if the third land costs two life.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Infusion checks the whole turn, not just combat. Bloodfell Caves and Illegitimate Business gain 1 life as they enter, so a spell cast in the first main phase of the turn a tapland lands is already doubled. Bogwater Lumaret does the same for every creature that follows it.
  • Losing life never turns Infusion off. Overgrown Tomb’s 2 life, Razortrap Gorge’s pain and combat damage are all irrelevant; only “gained life this turn” matters.
  • Firebending mana dies with the combat step. There is no window in the second main phase. Cast the pump, the removal and the drain activations while attackers are still attacking.
  • Firebending Student’s X is its power at the moment the trigger resolves. Casting Lumaret’s Favor after attackers are declared does not retroactively increase its mana, but it does make next turn’s burst larger.
  • Efflorescence is only a trick with Infusion on. Without life gained it is two counters at sorcery speed value; with Infusion it is indestructible plus trample, which is the card that beats a wipe or a trade.
  • Vindictive Warden costs {3} of any colour. Fire Nation Palace’s four red mana, the burst from a firebender attack and even spare basic lands all pay for it; it is the deck’s best mana sink once the ground is locked.
  • Teacher’s Pest and Moseo are a loop, not a one-shot. {B}{G} returns Teacher’s Pest tapped for another lifegain attack, and Moseo returns whatever the Pest traded with, so a stalled board still generates Infusion fuel every turn.
  • Blech, Loafing Pest is legendary. Hold the second copy as recursion fodder for Moseo or a Pest attack trigger rather than deploying a dead card.
  • Rogue’s Passage needs {4} plus its own activation tap. Plan the alpha strike a turn ahead, and remember that the same four mana cannot also pay a Vindictive Warden activation.
  • Dark Fortress needs a basic land or its first turn. It is an untapped source on the turn it enters and a reliable black-red source once a Forest, Swamp or Mountain is down.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Gain the life back: Pest attacks, Bogwater Lumaret entries and the lifegain taplands turn their race into your race, and Efflorescence with Infusion on blanks a removal spell or a big attacker for the turn
Midrange Do not trade pumps for single blocks; keep attackers wide, use Foolish Fate plus drain as removal-on-advantage and grind with Moseo’s end-step recursion
Control Play one threat at a time, keep two mana up for Lumaret’s Favor as a combat trick, and hold Follow the Lumarets for the turn they tap out; Pest Mascot’s trample plus Rogue’s Passage ends a tapped-out control player
Combo Attack first, aim Foolish Fate at the combo piece, and race with the Pest board; the deck’s clock is fast enough to force a combo deck to play defense
Ramp and big mana Use Rogue’s Passage and Vindictive Warden drains to push damage without walking into their blockers, and keep firebending mana available as the burst that finishes after their big play
