Reality Fracture is Standard-legal, but every card in the set still carries no arena_id in the card data. The import list at the end becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there.

Overview

Reality Fracture’s graveyard theme is built on surveil, threshold and milling yourself. This list attacks that theme from the cheap end: eleven one- and two-mana permanents fill the graveyard, three prepared creatures convert “prepared” into a free mill spell every turn, and every payoff is switched on by the same seven-card count.

Denzilore Fatehold is the hinge. The 3/4 flash flier reads “whenever you scry or surveil, put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control.” Surveil is cheap here — Eye of Jace surveils every upkeep, Proctor of Potential surveils on every creature entry, and the three surveil dual lands surveil as they enter. A single Denzilore turns a normal curve into a team that grows two to four counters a turn.

Proctor of Potential is the loop. It is a 3/1 for {W}{U} that surveils when it or any other creature enters, and it can be bought back from the graveyard for {W}{U} as long as you have scried or surveilled this turn — which its own entry trigger guarantees. Every turn it comes back it surveils again, so with Denzilore in play {W}{U} reads “put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control and get a 3/1 attacker.” With Saheeli, Consul of Oversight out, the same mana also makes a 1/1 flying Thopter.

The prepared cycle is the mill engine. Theorix Metamage, Void Extrapolator and Paradox Shaper each enter prepared (Paradox Shaper re-prepares every upkeep), and while prepared you may cast a copy of Omit Variables — mill three — without paying its cost. That is a free three-card mill every turn from each body, which is how the deck reaches threshold by turn four or five without spending a card.

At seven cards in the graveyard three separate payoffs turn on at once: Theorix Metamage becomes a 3/3 flier, Void Extrapolator becomes a 3/3, and Proft, Sinister Mastermind becomes castable as a 5/5 menace for {2}{B}. Eye of Jace also cashes in at that point, sacrificing itself for 2 damage to each opponent. The deck does not need a single big combo turn; it needs the yard at seven and a Denzilore, and the board does the rest.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-2, load the yard. Eye of Jace on one is the best opener: it surveils every upkeep and is a threshold burn spell later. Proctor of Potential on two attacks for three and surveils on entry. Surveil duals (Undercity Sewers, Meticulous Archive, Shadowy Backstreet) are worth playing on curve here — the tapped turn buys a surveil, which is a card in the graveyard and a counter on the team once Denzilore lands.
  2. Turns 3-4, deploy the mill bodies. Theorix Metamage and Void Extrapolator arrive prepared, so each one immediately casts a free Omit Variables for three more cards. Paradox Shaper does it every upkeep after that. Liliana the Repentant mills two more on each of your creature entries. Seven cards is normally reached on turn four.
  3. Turns 4-6, convert. Denzilore Fatehold at flash speed, then attack with threshold fliers and Proft, Sinister Mastermind. Saheeli, Consul of Oversight adds a Thopter every turn you surveil. From here the Proctor of Potential rebuy is the engine’s heartbeat, and Eye of Jace plus Hall of Echoes close games that stall on the ground.

Curve and mana

Four cards cost one mana, twenty cost two, six cost three, four cost four and two cost five. Twenty-four lands carry a curve that stops at Saheeli, Consul of Oversight: the deck’s real turn is turn two, where Proctor of Potential, Paradox Shaper, Void Extrapolator, Liliana the Repentant, Vraska's Final Mercy and Theorix Charm all sit.

Sources: nineteen blue, thirteen white, eleven black, plus Hall of Echoes for colourless. Black is the bottleneck — Liliana the Repentant, Vraska's Final Mercy and Proft, Sinister Mastermind all want it early — so the six surveil duals double as fixing and as engine triggers. White is only needed for Denzilore Fatehold and Saheeli, Consul of Oversight; do not keep a hand whose only white source is a Deserted Beach you cannot turn on.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Open with Eye of Jace or a surveil dual. The artifact is the stronger play because it starts surveilling on the very next upkeep and becomes reachable burn later; a surveil dual is the better turn one when you already hold a two-drop and need the colours. Proctor of Potential on two is the default curve play: three power attacks immediately and the entry trigger loads the yard.

Do not trade the Proctor away for nothing. It is the deck’s only repeatable surveil, and every copy that dies is one you have to buy back for {W}{U}. Against aggro, Vraska's Final Mercy and Theorix Charm are the early interaction, and both cost two, so holding one up on turn two is normal.

Mid game and the combo turn

The engine turn is a sequence, not a single card. With Denzilore Fatehold in play the deck wants as many surveil events as possible in one turn: Eye of Jace surveils in the upkeep, Proctor of Potential rebuys itself for {W}{U} and surveils on entry, and any land you play from the surveil cycle surveils too. Each of those events puts a counter on every creature, so a board of four creatures gains four to twelve power in a turn without spending a card.

Paradox Shaper is the quiet engine. It re-prepares on every upkeep, so the free Omit Variables copy is available every turn, and because casting the copy unprepares it, the Shaper’s own upkeep trigger is what resets it. Theorix Metamage and Void Extrapolator only enter prepared once, so their free mill is a one-shot; cast it the turn they land, before combat, so the yard count is live for threshold.

The prepared rules matter in practice. While a creature is prepared you may cast a copy of its spell, and doing so unprepares it — the copy resolves normally and the creature stays. You can hold the copy for a turn when you want the mill to happen after a Proft, Consulting Detective trigger, but there is rarely a reason to wait: threshold is the whole point.

Win condition 2 — the threshold burn

Eye of Jace is a one-mana artifact that surveils every upkeep and then, once there are seven or more cards in your graveyard, sacrifices itself: 2 damage to each opponent and you gain 2 life. Four copies are four separate triggers, and the deck fills its yard fast enough that the first one is live on turn four or five. Two Eyes are 4 damage that no blocker stops, and they are also the answer to a stalled ground board where the counter swarm cannot get through.

Sequence them rather than cracking all at once. The first Eye is usually worth cashing in as soon as threshold is live, because the two life matters against aggro and the surveil it gave you is already spent; holding the second one for the turn you attack means the opponent has to block the team and still take the burn.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands with Eye of Jace or Proctor of Potential, and keep any hand with a surveil dual plus a two-drop. Keep one-land hands only when the land is a surveil dual and the hand has two one-mana plays.

Ship hands with no black source — Liliana the Repentant, Vraska's Final Mercy and Proft, Sinister Mastermind are the cards that keep the deck alive — and ship hands that are all five-drops and lands. Denzilore Fatehold is the best card in the deck, but it is not an opener; it is a turn-four play that follows a loaded graveyard.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Milling is not surveilling. The free Omit Variables copies and Liliana the Repentant’s mill do not trigger Denzilore Fatehold, Saheeli, Consul of Oversight or Proft, Consulting Detective; only the word surveil or scry does. The surveil duals and Eye of Jace are the reliable triggers.
  • Proctor of Potential can only rebuy itself if you have scried or surveilled this turn. Its own entry trigger satisfies the condition, which is why the loop works, but a turn with no other surveil and an empty graveyard cannot start it.
  • The Proctor returns with a finality counter. If it dies again it is exiled, so the loop is not infinite — expect two or three rebuys per copy, not ten.
  • Proft, Sinister Mastermind cannot be cast at all below seven cards in the graveyard. It is a two-of for that reason: it is the best topdeck after threshold and the worst card before it.
  • Vraska's Final Mercy mode two (empower Jace 6) creates a Jace planeswalker token with “[−1]: Surveil 1” and “[−3]: Draw a card”. That token is a legal surveil source for Denzilore, and a real fallback when you have no other trigger. Mode one is the default.
  • Hall of Echoes taps for colourless only, so it never fixes a colour; it is a spell land. Its {5} ability copies a creature, and the legend rule does not apply that turn, so copying Denzilore Fatehold or a second Proft, Sinister Mastermind works.
  • Saheeli, Consul of Oversight triggers only once per turn no matter how many surveils you stack, so resolve the surveil you care about first if the board state is tight.
  • Threshold is a static check on seven or more cards in your graveyard, not a trigger. It turns off if the yard is emptied, so do not exile your own graveyard with Paradox Shaper’s second ability unless the card you are recycling is worth more than the threshold body.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Trade with Vraska's Final Mercy and Theorix Charm, then stabilise behind Denzilore Fatehold as a 3/4 flash blocker. The counter swarm wins the board fast once one Denzilore is down
Control Do not overextend into a sweeper. Land one prepared miller at a time, keep Theorix Charm for their key spell, and use Eye of Jace burn plus Hall of Echoes to finish without committing the team
Midrange Out-grow them: every surveil is a counter on every creature, so a stalled board favours you. Proft, Sinister Mastermind trades up with anything
Lifegain Race with fliers and the Eye of Jace burn; the deck’s damage is spread across many small attackers, so a single lifelink blocker is not enough
