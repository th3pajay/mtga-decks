Overview

Prismatic Departure is a four-color deck built on a feedback loop between two keywords that feed each other: Disappear wants you to lose permanents, and Vivid wants you to keep every color on the board. The bridge is colorless fodder. Six fetch lands (four Evolving Wilds, two Fabled Passage) crack for fixing and count as a permanent leaving the battlefield under your control, so every cracked fetch flips on every Disappear card at your end step: Rat King, Verminister grows and makes a Rat, Lord Dregg makes a flying Insect, Pizza Face, Gastromancer piles +1/+1 counters onto a creature. Bargain cards like Candy Grapple sacrifice tokens for the same switch while doing their job.

The Vivid side turns that growing rainbow board into damage and mana. Squawkroaster has double strike and power equal to the number of colors among permanents you control, so a typical board (green Bloom Tender, black Rat King, red Squawkroaster, blue Krang and Shredder, plus a white Spirit token from Phantom Interference) makes it a 5-power double striker. Bloom Tender taps for one mana of each of those colors, which is enough to chain the five-mana payoffs and activate the big lands. Shimmercreep turns the same count into a life swing, and Krang and Shredder turns every Disappear end step into a free spell from the opponent’s deck.

The Spree suite is the interaction layer and doubles as engine fuel. Phantom Interference counters spells or makes a white Spirit (a fifth color for Vivid), Metamorphic Blast shrinks attackers or draws, Lively Dirge finds and reanimates engine pieces, and Insatiable Avarice tutors the card you need on top.

Curve and mana

Twenty-seven lands: six fetches, sixteen nonbasic duals and utility lands, and four basics (one of each color). The mana base is built to hit green or black on turn one or two, blue and red by turn three, and to keep a fetch uncracked when a Disappear trigger is needed later.

The nonland curve is 33 cards with an average mana value of about 3.0: five one-drops (the Spree instants and Insatiable Avarice), fourteen two-drops (Bloom Tender, Explosive Prodigy, Rat King, Candy Grapple, Lively Dirge), six four-drops (Squawkroaster, Lord Dregg), six five-drops (Pizza Face, Shimmercreep, Glister Bairn) and two six-drops (Krang and Shredder). The deck reaches four lands on turn four roughly three games out of four, and the goldfish tests show a consistent turn-four board.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a two-drop that puts a color on the board: Bloom Tender (green), Explosive Prodigy (red, and it removes a creature on entry), or Rat King, Verminister (black). Hold fetch lands. A hand of fetch plus Rat King is the dream opener: turn two Rat King, turn three crack the fetch for a basic, and the Rat King’s Disappear trigger makes a Rat and grows itself at your end step. Phantom Interference and Metamorphic Blast are fine turn-one plays against aggressive decks only when needed; mostly you want them saved as answers.

Mid game

Land Lord Dregg, Insect Invader or Pizza Face on four mana. Lord Dregg turns every triggered end step into a flying token and every spare token into a card. Pizza Face creates the Food token that keeps your colored permanents safe as bargain fodder. Squawkroaster wants to come down when at least three colors are on board so it attacks as a 3-power double striker immediately. Do not sacrifice colored permanents casually: the Vivid count is the deck’s mana and its damage, so sacrifice colorless tokens, Foods, and fetch lands first.

The combo turn

The signature sequence starts with a fetch in play and a Disappear card on the battlefield. Crack the fetch at instant speed during combat or in the opponent’s turn. At the beginning of your end step every Disappear ability sees a permanent left this turn and fires: Rat King makes a Rat and grows, Lord Dregg makes an Insect, Pizza Face puts three counters on your biggest creature. Then cash the tokens in: sacrifice a token to Lord Dregg to draw, or bank three Rats for Rat King’s reanimation later. With Krang and Shredder, attack first to exile cards from the top of the opponent’s library, then the same end step casts one of them for free. The kill turn: Bloom Tender taps for four or five mana, Glister Bairn pumps the Squawkroaster at the start of combat, and Rogue’s Passage makes it unblockable; a 4-color Squawkroaster hits for 8, a 5-color one for 10, and with Glister Bairn’s X pump the swing ends the game.

Win condition 2

The Shimmercreep drain loop. Each Shimmercreep entry deals X damage to the opponent and gains X life, where X is the color count, so a single 5-color entry is a 10-life swing. The deck recurs it: Rat King sacrifices three Rats to return a creature from the graveyard (Shimmercreep included), Lively Dirge returns engine pieces up to total mana value 4, and Krang and Shredder keeps the board and hand stocked by casting the opponent’s own cards. The drain, plus Food lifegain and Pizza Face’s 15-life activation, outlasts anything that cannot kill in one turn.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two or three lands and at least one two-drop creature in the deck’s early colors (green, black, or red). A fetch land plus Rat King, Verminister or Lord Dregg is a strong engine start. Ship one-lander openers, hands without colored sources for the first two spells, and hands of nothing but five-drops and six-drops. Against aggro, favor early removal (Explosive Prodigy, Candy Grapple) and lifegain pieces; against control, keep the Spree counterspells and a fast Squawkroaster clock.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Disappear checks the whole turn, not just your actions: a fetch crack, a bargain sacrifice, or a blocker dying all arm the end-step triggers.
  • The Vivid count is colors among permanents you control, so tokens matter: Phantom Interference’s white Spirit adds a fifth color even though the deck is GUBR, and colored tokens (Rats, Insects) add black.
  • Squawkroaster’s power is set by the count each time it is checked; it shrinks if you sacrifice your only red or green permanent, so keep one of each color when possible.
  • Bloom Tender produces one mana per color present, up to five, but it must tap after your colors are on board, so sequence spells before activating it.
  • Lord Dregg’s draw requires sacrificing a token, which itself arms Disappear for the same end step, creating a small loop.
  • Rat King’s reanimation needs exactly three Rats; Lord Dregg’s Insects do not count, so save Rats when planning to reanimate Shimmercreep.
  • Krang and Shredder exiles from each opponent and you may cast one card exiled with it per Disappear end step, for free.
  • Restless Vinestalk’s attack trigger sets another creature’s base power and toughness to 3/3, which would erase Squawkroaster’s color-count power, so never target it.
  • Rogue’s Passage’s activation is sorcery-speed only in the sense that it must be used before attackers are declared; activate it in the first main phase.
  • Metamorphic Blast’s Rabbit mode is tempo: it shrinks an attacker or blocker to 0/1 until end of turn, buying a full turn against a large threat.

Matchups

  • Aggro (fliers, lifegain, cats): Explosive Prodigy and Candy Grapple clear early threats, Lord Dregg’s flying Insects block, and Food lifegain plus Shimmercreep’s gain stabilize before the drain finishes the race.
  • Midrange (counters, landfall): the recursion engine out-values one-for-one removal; every Rat and Insect is a card through Lord Dregg, and Krang and Shredder mills their best topdecks.
  • Control: Spree counterspells protect the engine, Disappear triggers rebuild the board after sweepers, and the tutor suite (Insatiable Avarice, Lively Dirge) finds the answer.
  • Combo and big mana: the deck is the aggressor, clocking with a double-striking Squawkroaster through Rogue’s Passage before their engine comes online.
