Evergreen Requiem

Sultai (Blue / Black / Green) legendary-Elf graveyard engine — landfall Elf swarm, and a Thranduil that borrows the activated abilities of dead Elves

Overview

Evergreen Requiem is the Hobbit set’s Sultai Elf deck, built around one sentence of rules text: Thranduil, the Elvenking has all activated abilities of all Elf cards in your graveyard. Every Elf you discard, mill or lose is not gone — it is a permanent upgrade stapled to a 5/6 body. Discard Elrond, Moon-Reader and Thranduil gains a repeatable blink. Discard Woodland Weavemaster and he gains a mana ability that scales with his power. Discard Lathril, Blade of the Elves and he gains the kill: tap ten untapped Elves you control, each opponent loses 10 life and you gain 10.

That makes the deck’s own card selection into its engine. Thranduil draws two and discards one every time another legendary Elf enters, Silvan Reveler loots on entry, Confusticate and Bebother draws two and discards one, and the Silvan Rally adventure mills four cards and hands back up to two lands. Every one of those effects is a discard outlet pointed at the graveyard, and the graveyard is where the payoffs live.

The board is built the same way. Thranduil, Sindarin Liege gives other Elves +1/+1 and makes a 1/1 green Elf token on every landfall; Thranduil’s Company puts two +1/+1 counters on a creature on every landfall and lets you play an extra land; Down in the Valley fetches a land, then makes landfall tokens, then pumps the team. Ten Elves is not a fantasy in this deck — it is turn six. Once ten are untapped, the copied Lathril ability ends the game on the spot, and it does not care about blockers, life totals or fog effects.

The backup plan is combat, and it is real: Mirkwood Pathmaker is as large as your land count, Thranduil’s Company hands out counters with vigilance, Galion, Elvenking’s Butler copies a 4/4 onto the smallest attacker, Restless Vinestalk is a 5/5 trampler that is still a land, and Rogue’s Passage or the copied Elvenking’s Harper ability pushes the biggest one through unblocked.

Curve and mana

The 36 non-land cards are 2 one-drops, 4 two-drops, 15 three-drops, 10 four-drops and 5 five-drops, for an average CMC of 3.42 on the API’s score (midrange, with a combo tag from the deck’s tutor and engine density). Sixteen lands produce green, fourteen produce blue and eight produce black, with two Elven Passage to fetch whichever basic the hand is missing; colour consistency scores 100 on the API. Six lands always enter tapped — the four Elvenking’s Halls and two Restless Vinestalk — while Dreamroot Cascade and Deathcap Glade are untapped from the third land onward and Watery Grave is untapped for 2 life, so the deck usually opens with a tapland or a one-drop and starts double-spelling from turn four. The curve tops out at Thranduil and The Arkenstone: everything expensive in this deck is a payoff, never a filler.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

There is no pressure to attack. Turns one to three are about mana, land drops and stocking the graveyard. Woodland Weavemaster is the best turn two: it ramps only into Elf spells and Elf abilities, which is all this deck has, and its power grows every time another Elf enters. Thranduil’s Company should be played before you need the extra land, because its additional-land clause is what makes the landfall triggers happen twice a turn.

The first real decision is what to discard. Thranduil, the Elvenking’s entry trigger and Silvan Reveler both read draw one and discard one, and the correct discard is almost always an Elf you would rather have in the graveyard than on the battlefield: Elrond, Moon-Reader if you have Thranduil, Woodland Weavemaster if you need mana, Lathril, Blade of the Elves if you are building a wide board. Silvan Rally does the same job from the top of the library — mill four, keep up to two lands — and every Elf that lands in the graveyard is a tool Thranduil will pick up later.

Mid game

Turns four to six decide the game. Thranduil, the Elvenking is the priority: a 5/6 that blocks almost everything in Standard, does not die to most burn, and turns the rest of your deck into triggers. Each time a legendary Elf enters after him — another Thranduil, the Sindarin Liege, Galion, Llanowar Elves is not legendary, but the other Thranduils, Elrond, Lathril and Galion all are — you draw two and discard one, which is two cards deeper into the graveyard and one land closer to the drain.

Count Elves constantly. The kill needs ten untapped Elves, and the deck produces them from five sources: Thranduil, Sindarin Liege’s landfall tokens, Down in the Valley chapters II through IV, Lathril’s combat-damage tokens, Mirkwood Nurturer replayed lands, and the creatures themselves. Do not attack with Elves you need untapped unless the attack wins: menace on Lathril and vigilance from Thranduil’s Company mean several Elves can attack without ever tapping out of the drain.

The combo turn

  1. Have Thranduil, the Elvenking on the battlefield and Lathril, Blade of the Elves in the graveyard (which happens through any loot, any discard, any trade, or Silvan Rally).
  2. Build to ten untapped Elves. Landfall tokens do most of this work: a single turn with Thranduil’s Company plus two land drops is four new Elves with counters on them.
  3. Activate the inherited ability: tap ten untapped Elves you control. Each opponent loses 10 life and you gain 10 life. Thranduil may tap himself as one of the ten, since he is an Elf.
  4. If the game is not over, Elrond, Moon-Reader in the graveyard gives Thranduil {5}{U}{U}: exile up to two other nonland permanents you control and return them at the beginning of the next end step. Blink Thranduil, Sindarin Liege for a fresh landfall token, or blink Silvan Reveler to loot again and put a discarded land onto the battlefield tapped — the blink is not a win condition by itself, it is how the deck keeps the ten Elves coming after a sweeper.

A representative turn with seven lands in play and Thranduil plus Down in the Valley: play land (landfall makes a 1/1 Elf token), replay the land Mirkwood Nurturer bounced earlier (a second landfall, two more counters), cast Thranduil’s Company’s counter trigger on the token, then activate the drain for 10 through a board of mostly 1/1s.

Win condition 2

When the graveyard is not cooperating, the deck attacks. Mirkwood Pathmaker is the deck’s largest body for three mana once the land count reaches six or seven, and Rogue’s Passage or the inherited Elvenking’s Harper ability ({4}{U}: target creature can’t be blocked this turn) delivers it through any blocker. Galion, Elvenking’s Butler adds a free 4/4: on attack, the smallest Elf’s base power and toughness become Galion’s 4/4 until end of turn, which turns a 1/1 landfall token into a 4/4 attacker with no additional investment.

The third route is the win-condition land itself: Restless Vinestalk becomes a 5/5 green and blue Plant with trample for {3}{G}{U} and stays a land, so it is a threat that survives creature sweepers, and its attack trigger sets up a trade by making another creature’s base power and toughness 3/3. Between Pathmaker, Vinestalk, Galion’s copies, Thranduil’s Company counters and The Arkenstone’s team anthem, the deck has lethal combat damage available in any game where the drain plan is one Elf short.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two to four lands plus a two- or three-drop Elf, and keep a hand with one of Thranduil, the Elvenking or Thranduil’s Company plus a green source and a black or blue source. Ship hands with fewer than two lands, hands with no green source (the deck’s mana base leans green: sixteen of twenty-four lands produce green), and five-land hands where the only spells cost four or more — the deck needs landfall triggers, not a flooded draw. A two-land hand with Silvan Rally or Down in the Valley is a keep: both find the third and fourth land.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Thranduil copies activated abilities only. Elvenking’s Harper’s unblockable ability, Llanowar Elves’ mana ability, Woodland Weavemaster’s scaling mana ability, Elrond’s blink and Lathril’s drain all count; Down in the Valley and The Arkenstone are not Elf cards and grant nothing.
  • The drain ability’s cost is tapping ten untapped Elves, not sacrificing them. An Elf with vigilance, or one that has already attacked without tapping (Thranduil’s Company grants vigilance), can be used for the drain in the same turn it attacked.
  • Thranduil, Sindarin Liege and Thranduil, the Elvenking are both legendary; the extra copies are not dead, because discarding a duplicate legendary Elf is exactly the graveyard-filling play the deck wants.
  • Silvan Reveler’s landfall trigger returns it from the graveyard to your hand for {1}{G}{U}, which is a repeatable body and a repeatable loot if you have the mana; it is the deck’s most resilient engine piece.
  • Mirkwood Nurturer returns a permanent you control to its hand — target your own land or a Silvan Reveler, then replay it for a landfall trigger. Do not target the permanent you are about to sacrifice to Elvenking’s Halls in the same turn.
  • Elven Passage’s behold clause lets you untap the fetched land if you control or reveal an Elf, which is the difference between a two-mana turn and a full turn; hold it until you have an Elf in hand or in play.
  • Dreamroot Cascade and Deathcap Glade enter untapped only if you already control two or more other lands, so play basics and Watery Grave first and let the check lands follow.
  • The Arkenstone’s end step draw is a free card every turn, and it makes the whole team +1/+1 — with ten Elves it is also ten extra power on the drain turn’s follow-up attack.
  • Confusticate and Bebother’s discard half is a real engine play, not just a fallback: discarding Lathril, Elrond or a duplicate legendary Elf is often better than drawing two.
  • Menace on Lathril means it cannot be blocked by a single creature, which matters for the token plan but not for the drain; keep Lathril attacking only when the tokens are worth more than the drain turn’s timing.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Mirkwood Meditator, Thranduil and Thranduil’s Company’s vigilance bodies hold the ground; Mirkwood Meditator’s 2/4 blocks all day, and the drain for 10 swings any race you are losing
Midrange Your top end is bigger: Thranduil is a 5/6, Pathmaker grows with every land, and Galion makes a 4/4 for free each attack; counter-based removal and trading are what the graveyard plan wants
Control Discard outlets beat counterspells — an Elf in the graveyard is online even after a sweeper. Hold Silvan Reveler and Thranduil, the Elvenking for the turn after the wipe, and remember that Restless Vinestalk is a land, so it survives creature removal
Combo The deck is fast enough to matter from turn five, and Confusticate and Bebother’s counter mode is a real answer; the drain does not target, so hexproof and protection do not stop it
