Overview

Every card in this list is a counter event, a counter multiplier, or a way to spend counters. The plan is to make land drops that are also spells.

Bristly Bill, Spine Sower and Mossborn Hydra both read “Landfall — whenever a land you control enters”. Bristly Bill puts a +1/+1 counter on any creature you control and can double the counters on the whole board for {3}{G}{G}; Mossborn Hydra doubles its own counters instead, and it has trample. With seven fetch lands in the base (Terramorphic Expanse, Evolving Wilds and Promising Vein) plus Restless Prairie, the deck runs landfall triggers on turns where it is not casting anything, and those triggers arrive as counters that Ouroboroid then multiplies.

Ouroboroid is the engine’s ceiling. It puts X counters on each creature you control at the beginning of combat, where X is its own power — and the ability counts its power as the trigger resolves, so the first combat (X equals 1) hands out one counter to everything, the next combat X is larger, and one Caradora, Heart of Alacria adds a counter to each of those events because any time counters would be put on a creature you control, it puts that many plus one instead.

The support cards exist to make those counters lethal and to keep the deck from running dry. Training Regimen gives every creature with a counter trample and adds a counter of its own every combat. Pawpatch Recruit brings offspring {2} for a second body and tramples. Wildwood Scourge grows every time another non-Hydra creature of yours receives counters, so Ouroboroid’s combat trigger is a second Ouroboroid. Skyknight Squire converts three counters into a flier. Inspiring Call draws one card per creature with a counter and makes them indestructible for the turn. Stocking the Pantry turns every counter event into a supply counter and every two mana into a card.

The mana is deliberately boring: five Forests, four Plains, two Temple of Plenty, two Restless Prairie, and seven lands that fetch a basic. Green is needed early (Bristly Bill, Wildwood Scourge, Pawpatch Recruit, Stocking the Pantry), white is needed from turn two (Skyknight Squire, Take Up the Shield) and turn four (Caradora), and the fetch lands thin the deck and trigger landfall in the same motion. Nothing in the 60 costs more than four mana, and 24 of the 37 nonlands cost two or less.

Twenty-four of the thirty-seven nonlands cost two or less, and the deck never needs more than four mana for any single card. The two X-spells (Wildwood Scourge and Broodguard Elite) are counted at their minimum in the table and are usually cast for three or four mana once the third land is down. The twenty-three land base holds a four-land-by-turn-four rate of 0.584 with seven fetches, so the deck hits its fourth land on schedule while still triggering landfall on the way.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about putting counter sources on the board, not about pressure. Stocking the Pantry on one is the best opening because it converts every later counter event into a card; Pawpatch Recruit on one is the fastest, because a 2/1 trampler that can make a token copy gives Skyknight Squire and Wildwood Scourge two triggers in one card.

Turn two is Bristly Bill whenever the mana allows it. Bristly Bill is the card that makes the fetch lands matter, and it is also the only way the deck doubles the whole board, so it is worth cracking Terramorphic Expanse or Evolving Wilds the same turn to put a counter somewhere immediately. Skyknight Squire on two is the alternative, and it snowballs by itself once the deck starts dropping creatures.

Take Up the Shield is the turn-two play to hold rather than cast: it is a counter, lifelink and indestructible for two mana, and it is nearly always better spent protecting the creature that is about to be pumped than on a 1/1 on turn two.

Mid game

Turns three to five are about stacking counter events in a single combat rather than deploying one threat at a time.

The highest-value sequence is to land Ouroboroid and then crack a fetch land before the beginning of the next combat. Ouroboroid’s trigger puts X counters on every creature (X equals its power, so one counter per creature on the first combat), the fetch land adds a landfall counter through Bristly Bill or Mossborn Hydra, Caradora makes each of those events one larger, and Wildwood Scourge adds a counter for each non-Hydra creature that received counters. A single combat step with those pieces in play adds four to six counters across the board at a cost of zero cards.

Broodguard Elite is the deck’s insurance policy. Cast for three or four mana it enters as a 3/3 or 4/4 with warp, and if it dies its counters move to another creature, so the investment is never wasted; warping it back later re-triggers the entry. Wildwood Scourge is the other card worth protecting, because every future counter event is worth an extra counter while it is in play.

Do not over-commit into a sweeper. The deck’s answer is Inspiring Call: hold it until the opponent taps out for a board wipe, cast it in response, draw four or five cards and keep every creature with a counter alive for the turn.

The combo turn

The engine turn needs three things: Ouroboroid in play, at least two other creatures with counters already on them, and a land drop or a fetch land still to use.

  1. Start the turn with Ouroboroid and a board of counter carriers. Any Wildwood Scourge or Broodguard Elite counts double for the purposes of the next step.
  2. Beginning of combat: Ouroboroid triggers for X, where X is its power. With Caradora in play every one of those placements becomes X plus one.
  3. Crack a fetch land before the trigger resolves if the mana is open, so Bristly Bill or Mossborn Hydra adds its landfall counter to the same combat. Mossborn Hydra doubling its own counters is the single largest growth event in the deck.
  4. Training Regimen adds one more counter and turns the whole board into tramplers, so the counters convert to damage in the same turn.
  5. Attack with everything. Restless Prairie animates for {2}{G}{W} and pumps the rest of the team by +1/+1 when it attacks, which is the difference between a chump block and lethal.
  6. If one blocker matters, Rogue’s Passage for four mana makes the biggest attacker unblockable, and Take Up the Shield gives it lifelink and indestructible so the swing is safe.

A realistic turn five with Ouroboroid, Caradora and Training Regimen in play is six to eight counters on the team plus trample, which is lethal against most boards that do not have a sweeper.

Win condition 2

The second path does not need Ouroboroid: it needs counters on creatures that cannot be blocked.

Skyknight Squire is the cheapest route. It gains a counter every time any other creature enters, so a turn of Pawpatch Recruit plus offspring token, a fetch land and a Broodguard Elite takes it past three counters and turns it into a 4/4 or larger flier. Mossborn Hydra is the other: a landfall doubling trigger on top of trample makes it a two-turn clock from three mana, and its counters keep doubling for free as the deck plays lands.

The lands are part of both paths. Restless Prairie attacking gives every other attacker +1/+1, so the Llama is a team pump on a land that survives sorcery-speed removal, and Rogue’s Passage makes whichever of these creatures is largest unblockable. That combination is the deck’s answer to a stalled board: pump the team with the manland, clear the last blocker with the Passage, and attack for the win with counters rather than with card advantage.

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
Ouroboroid’s X is locked in as the trigger resolves It puts X counters on each creature, X being its power at that moment. Its own counters arrive with the same instruction, so its power grows every combat and the next trigger is larger
Caradora, Heart of Alacria adds one per counter event Any time one or more counters would be put on a creature you control, that many plus one are put on it instead. Ouroboroid’s mass placement counts as one event per creature, and so does a fetch land’s landfall trigger
Wildwood Scourge only counts non-Hydra recipients It is a Hydra itself, so Ouroboroid’s counters on it do not trigger it, but every non-Hydra creature that receives counters does. A mass trigger across four non-Hydra creatures is four separate counters on the Scourge
Mossborn Hydra doubles only its own counters Its landfall trigger is “double the number of +1/+1 counters on this creature”, so it does not interact with Caradora for the rest of the team. It does stack with Bristly Bill’s doubling on a later combat
Skyknight Squire counts three counters, not three triggers As long as it has three or more counters total it has flying and is a Knight. Broodguard Elite’s exit trigger and Take Up the Shield both push it over the line without a single landfall trigger
Broodguard Elite passes counters when it leaves The counters move to target creature you control, so using it as a chump blocker is a transfer rather than a loss. Its warp {X}{G} lets you recast it later, and the exit trigger fires again if it leaves a second time
Pawpatch Recruit’s second ability needs an opponent’s spell Whenever a creature you control becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, put a +1/+1 counter on a different creature you control. Point their removal at the Recruit and the counter lands on Mossborn Hydra or Skyknight Squire instead
The offspring token arrives with the entry trigger Paying the extra {2} creates the token copy as the Recruit enters, so Skyknight Squire and Wildwood Scourge see two entries, not one
Training Regimen triggers every combat, not every turn A single +1/+1 counter per combat step on a creature you control, and every creature you control with a counter has trample for as long as the enchantment is in play
Inspiring Call is a draw spell first It draws for each creature with a counter and only then grants indestructible, so cast it in response to a sweeper with the board already built. Board wipes that exile avoid it, and so do sacrifice effects
Take Up the Shield answers removal and wins races The counter is permanent, the lifelink and indestructible are until end of turn. Cast it on the attacking creature in the turn you go for lethal to gain four or more life and survive the crack back
Stocking the Pantry tracks events, not counters One or more counters is one supply counter, so Ouroboroid’s mass trigger adds one supply counter, not five. Each supply counter is two mana for a card at sorcery speed
Restless Prairie is a land until end of turn Animating it does not stop it from being a land, so landfall triggers still fire if it enters after animation, and it keeps tapping for mana. It also counts for Training Regimen because it has counters only if something puts them on it
Rogue’s Passage ability timing Four mana and a tap, at sorcery speed in practice because it is an activated ability of a land. Use it in the second main phase before attackers so the mana is not telegraphed, or in the mana you floated for a fetch
Fetches enter tapped but trigger landfall immediately Terramorphic Expanse and Evolving Wilds sacrifice themselves and put the basic in tapped, and the basic entering is what triggers landfall. Promising Vein costs one mana for the same effect on a colourless land

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain Race with counters and take the lifelink shield. Take Up the Shield is at its best here: the indestructible block plus four life swings the race by a full turn. Keep Skyknight Squire as a blocker until it has three counters, then race in the air
Midrange The best matchup. Midrange gives the deck time to assemble Ouroboroid plus Caradora plus Training Regimen, and a trampling counter board outclasses anything midrange can block. Fetch lands should be saved for the turn of the big attack so the landfall counters arrive with the Ouroboroid trigger
Control The hardest matchup. Play two threats at a time rather than four, keep Stocking the Pantry and Inspiring Call for the turns after a sweeper, and treat Broodguard Elite as the creature that wants to die because its counters move to whatever survived. Restless Prairie dodges creature removal entirely
Combo The clock is turn five or six, so spend every card on damage. Rogue’s Passage and Restless Prairie are the cards that end the game through a stalled ground board, and Inspiring Call is the only card worth holding back because it refills after an interaction-heavy turn

Iterations

The published list is version three. Version one failed the simulation and synergy gates, version two fixed the simulation with cheaper counter bodies but left cohesion short of the threshold, and version three rebuilt the top of the curve around landfall-fed counter creatures and a fetch-heavy mana base, which cleared both.
