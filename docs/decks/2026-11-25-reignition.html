Rakdos firebending combo — combat-step mana into extra combat steps

Overview

Firebending turns attacking into mana. Every creature with the keyword adds red mana when it attacks, and that mana lasts until end of combat — a resource that exists only inside the combat phase. Reignition is built to spend it there: it attacks, converts the attack into six or ten mana, and immediately buys another combat phase with it.

The engine has three moving parts:

  • Fuel: Firebending Student (firebending equal to its power), Fire Sages, Fire Nation Cadets, Zuko, Exiled Prince, Ingris Stingerquill, Vindictive Warden, and Cruel Administrator all generate mana or bodies that generate mana. Fire Nation Palace grants firebending 4 to any creature for {1}{R}, so a single attacker becomes a four-mana engine on its own.
  • Converter: Great Train Heist is the pivot. Its spree mode costs {2}{R} and untaps all your creatures, then adds an additional combat phase after this one. Because the spell is an instant, it is cast with the very mana the current attack just produced: the team untaps, attacks again, and produces the mana again.
  • Payoff: Ingris Stingerquill makes every attacking creature deal 1 damage to each opponent. That trigger fires on declaration, not on damage, so each extra combat phase is a guaranteed, unblockable sweep of the opponent’s life total on top of the combat damage itself.

Firebender Ascension is the multiplier that makes the chain self-funding. Its quest counters come from attacking creatures causing their own triggered abilities to trigger — exactly what firebending does. At four counters, the next firebending trigger is copied: one attacker producing four mana produces eight, which is enough to pay for a second Great Train Heist and keep the chain alive.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Establish the board (turns 1–3). Land a one-drop, then Ingris Stingerquill or Vindictive Warden. Firebending Lesson clears the first blocker. Do not trade creatures: every body on the board is mana and a ping.
  2. First engine turn (turn 4–5). Attack with everything, count the mana, and spend it inside combat. A Great Train Heist for untap plus extra combat is the default buy; Vindictive Warden activations are where surplus mana goes. Fire Nation Palace upgrades the weakest attacker into a four-mana source before combat.
  3. Close (turn 5–7). Sozin's Comet gives the whole team firebending 5 — with four attackers that is twenty mana in one combat, enough for two extra combat phases, a fistful of Warden pings, and lethal. Zuko, Exiled Prince turns leftover combat mana into cards so the chain does not run dry.

Lands as engines

Land What it does for the combo
Fire Nation Palace {1}{R}, {T}: target creature gains firebending 4. Applied to an unblocked attacker, one land produces four mana in the same combat, which is the exact cost of a Great Train Heist untap-plus-extra-combat. It is the deck’s cheapest way to start the chain with only one creature.
Restless Vents Animates into a 2/3 menace for {1}{B}{R} and attacks. That attack is a body for Stingerquill’s trigger and a firebending source once the Palace has touched it; the attack trigger also filters a card.
Rogue’s Passage {4}, {T} for unblockable. The pings do not need to connect, but the combat damage does, and this is how the last few points get through a stalled board.

Curve and mana

Twenty-four lands with nineteen red sources and fifteen black sources; the only real black requirement is Ingris Stingerquill’s single pip, the rest is hybrid or red. Blazemire Verge and Blood Crypt are untapped early, Fire Nation Palace wants a basic first, and Restless Vents is a fine turn-one play because it is a land that later becomes an attacker. The nonland curve tops out at five with only four such cards, so the deck functions on three lands and wins on five.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Sequence basics before Fire Nation Palace; it enters tapped otherwise. Fire Nation Cadets on turn one is the ideal opener because Firebending Lesson on turn two both answers a threat and turns the Cadets into a two-mana attacker for the rest of the game. Hold Great Train Heist — it is never a pump spell, it is the combo piece.

The combo turn

  1. Precombat: cast Firebending Lesson or activate Fire Nation Palace to set the mana count. Note that a prowess trigger from the Lesson makes Firebending Student a bigger firebender.
  2. Declare attackers. Firebending triggers resolve and add red mana; Ingris Stingerquill pings once per attacker.
  3. Still in combat, cast Great Train Heist with spree: pay the extra {2}{R} for untap plus additional combat phase. All creatures untap.
  4. Declare attackers again in the new combat. Firebending triggers again, Stingerquill pings again.
  5. Repeat while the mana allows, then dump the remainder into Vindictive Warden at {3} per ping.

The arithmetic that matters: with four attackers and one extra combat, Stingerquill alone deals 8 damage, and every attacker’s firebending trigger has paid for the Heist that produced it.

Win condition 2

Vindictive Warden does not need to attack to win. It is a 2/3 menace that blocks well, and its {3} ability is instant-speed and repeatable — so any combat where the board produced ten or more mana ends with several points of direct damage even if the opponent has a wall of blockers. Against decks that answer the board, this is the plan that gets there; against decks that cannot block, it is redundant.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands plus a one-drop and either Ingris Stingerquill or Vindictive Warden; the payoff is more important than the fuel because almost every creature in the deck is fuel. Ship hands with no one-drop and no payoff, and ship five-land hands — the deck needs action by turn two, not land drops.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Firebending mana is combat-only. It empties at end of combat, so it must be spent before the current combat phase ends. Cast Great Train Heist during combat, not in the second main phase.
  • Stingerquill pings on declaration. Blockers do not stop the 1 damage; only removing the creature before attackers are declared does. Attacking into a bigger board is often correct.
  • Firebender Ascension counts the creature’s own attack triggers. Firebending triggers from Student, Sages, Cadets, Zuko and Warden all count, as does Cruel Administrator’s token trigger; at four counters the next such trigger is copied, which doubles that creature’s mana or doubles the token.
  • Firebending Student’s X is locked at the trigger. Pump it with Fire Sages or cast a Lesson before attacking, not after.
  • Sozin's Comet stacks with existing firebending. Each instance is a separate trigger, so a Cadets with firebending 2 that gains firebending 5 adds seven mana. Cast it before the first attack of the combo turn, not mid-chain.
  • Great Train Heist spree modes are cumulative. Untap plus extra combat is the mode that matters; the first-strike mode is a reasonable add when the extra {2} is spare and the opponent’s blockers would otherwise trade.

Matchups

  • Aggro: you are the faster combo but a worse pure aggro deck. Trade with Firebending Lesson, block with the 1/4 Stingerquill, and race with pings rather than combat damage.
  • Midrange: their blockers are irrelevant to Stingerquill. Save Rogue's Passage for the turn the combat damage matters, and use Vindictive Warden to finish over the top.
  • Control: do not overextend into sweepers; keep one engine piece and one converter in hand. Zuko, Exiled Prince and Sozin's Comet’s foretell both let you hold mana and reload after a wipe.
