At a glance

Format Standard (60 cards, best of one)
Colors Naya — white, green, red
Archetype Saddled go-wide aggro combo
Primary win Saddled attack wave with trample, double strike and a flying Dragon
Secondary win Warleader’s Call drain off a token conveyor belt, plus manland attacks

Overview

Hoofbeat Holiday welds two Wilds-era mechanics onto one engine. Saddle is the tap engine: a Mount that attacks while saddled turns its attack step into a permanent generator. Celebration is the payoff: two or more nonland permanents entering under your control in a turn turns on the squad.

The bridge between them is the timing. Celebration’s static bonuses are evaluated continuously, so tokens that arrive mid-combat still switch the squad on for damage. Gilded Ghoda’s Treasure, Seraphic Steed’s Angel, Bridled Bighorn’s Sheep and Autarch Mammoth’s Elephant all enter during the attack step - the exact moment Goddric, Cloaked Reveler becomes a 4/4 flying Dragon and Gallant Pie-Wielder gains double strike.

Kolodin, Triumph Caster removes the usual saddle tax. Every Mount he watches enter is saddled until end of turn, so a Mount cast in the first main phase attacks saddled for free on that same turn. Cast a Mount and a second permanent, and Celebration is live before combat even starts. Alacrian Armory repeats the trick every combat afterwards.

Approach

Game plan phases

  1. Turns one and two: deploy a two-drop that pulls double duty. Gilded Ghoda and Kolodin, Triumph Caster are the ideal openers, since a hasty saddled Ghoda makes a Treasure on the first attack and single-handedly starts the Celebration count.
  2. Turns three and four: add Drover Grizzly for team trample and a Celebration payoff. From here each combat should produce two or more permanents, so the whole team attacks as the “celebrating” version of itself.
  3. Turns five and later: close with volume. Restless Ridgeline and Rogue’s Passage convert spare mana into unblockable damage, and Bulwark Ox protects the board from a sweeper.

Curve and mana

Thirty-seven spells, average mana value 2.6, with twenty-three lands. The deck tops out at one six-drop: everything else costs four or less, so double-spelling from turn three is normal. White is the heaviest requirement (Kolodin, Bulwark Ox, Gallant Pie-Wielder, Warleader’s Call), which is why the manabase carries ten white sources plus two Plains.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with the cheapest Mount you can saddle for free. Gilded Ghoda into Kolodin is the dream curve: the Ghoda arrives hasty and saddled, attacks for two, and hands you a Treasure that both fixes a color and counts as a permanent entering. Do not trade the Treasure for mana unless the extra mana casts a second permanent that turn - the token is worth more as a Celebration trigger than as a land drop.

Mid game

From turn three the deck wants two entries per turn, every turn. Combat is the best source: attack with a saddled Ghoda and a saddled Seraphic Steed and you have a Treasure and a 3/3 Angel before blocks. Those two entries turn Goddric into a 4/4 flying Dragon and Gallant Pie-Wielder into a double-striking 2/3, still in the same combat.

Sequence taps carefully. Alacrian Armory saddles one Mount at the beginning of combat, so keep the Armory’s target for the Mount that gains the most from being saddled - usually Seraphic Steed, whose Angel is the biggest single token in the deck. Tapping real creatures to pay a saddle cost is the fallback, not the plan; spend tokens first, and remember Drover Grizzly only needs one point of power to saddle.

The combo turn

The signature turn has three parts. First, cast two permanents in the first main phase so Celebration is already on. Second, attack with every saddled Mount; each one adds a permanent during combat, which keeps Celebration online and pumps Warleader’s Call’s drain. Third, after blocks, use Restless Ridgeline to animate, attack, and untap a creature that saddled something this turn - that creature can then block, or a second attacker gets +2/+0 out of nowhere.

Against a stalled board, Rogue’s Passage plus a trampling saddled Mount ends the game. Eight mana (four for the Passage activation, four to attack) is realistic by turn six in this deck.

Win condition two

Warleader’s Call is the second, non-combat clock. Every creature and token that enters deals one damage to each opponent, so a single combat with a Ghoda and a Seraphic Steed is three damage before blockers. Add Autarch Mammoth, which makes an Elephant on entry and another on every saddled attack, and the drain alone closes games through a board that has stabilized. Restless Ridgeline adds two more damage a turn from a land slot.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two lands and a two-drop Mount. Keep one-land hands only with Gilded Ghoda plus a second one-or-two-mana spell and a green source. Ship hands with no two-drop: the deck’s whole plan is turning on Celebration by turn three, and a hand of four-drops does that a full turn late. A hand with Kolodin plus two Mounts is always a keep.

Key interactions

  • Kolodin’s saddling ability triggers on the Mount entering, so a Mount cast after combat is still saddled but has nothing to attack with. Cast Mounts in the first main phase.
  • Saddle is sorcery-speed only. Alacrian Armory and Kolodin are the only ways to saddle mid-combat-step, and both happen before attackers are declared.
  • Miriam, Herd Whisperer gives Mounts hexproof during your turn only, which protects the engine from instant-speed removal on your own turn but not from sorcery-speed sweepers on theirs. Bulwark Ox’s sacrifice ability is the answer to those.
  • Goddric loses all other creature types when he becomes a Dragon. He is no longer a Human, so anything that cares about Human count or tribal bonuses stops applying.
  • Gallant Pie-Wielder’s double strike is a static ability, so it is live for damage even if the second permanent entered after attackers were declared.
  • Ash, Party Crasher checks Celebration on attack, not at the beginning of combat, so a token made by an earlier attacker in the same combat still counts.
