Overview

Wilderland Howl is a black-green creature deck with a red removal splash, and it is built on two interlocking Wilderlands mechanics. The first is ferocious. Wargling, Ravening Warg, Nighthowl Pursuer, Wilderland Scrounger and The Chief Warg all switch on the moment you control a creature with power four or greater, and this deck has an unusually easy time meeting that condition: Ordinary Bear is a four-power four-drop, Large Bear is a 5/5 with haste, and Beorn the Fierce is a 6/6 that also hands out a trample counter. A Wargling attack then gives the whole team trample, a Scrounger attack puts a +1/+1 counter on every creature, and The Chief Warg draws a card.

The second is the Bear payoff. Dancing from Dark to Dawn makes a 2/2 green Bear token on every land that enters, and Beorn the Fierce gives other Bears +2/+2, so the same token is a 4/4 trampler. Beorn’s combat trigger turns any creature you control into a Bear in addition to its other types, which means the Warg pack itself starts collecting the anthem. With three or more Bears, Beorn draws two cards at the start of combat, and the deck stops running out of gas.

The deck attacks from two directions and neither line depends on the other. The wide line is Dancing from Dark to Dawn plus Beorn the Fierce: play lands, make Bears, make them bigger, attack with trample. The tall line is counters. Dancing from Dark to Dawn puts counters equal to the mana value of every creature spell you cast, Mirkwood sacrifices itself for two more counters on a Bear or Wolf, and Quarrel and Pinecone Strike clear the blockers. Once one creature is large enough, Rogue’s Passage makes it unblockable and the game ends in one or two attacks.

Note on the set: The Hobbit set lands on Arena 2026-08-14; Arena IDs are still empty for many of these printings in the card database, so the list below imports by card name from that date.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about bodies that matter later. Wargling is the best turn-two play: it is a 2/2 that becomes a trample anthem the moment anything with four power is on the board. Ravening Warg trades with anything thanks to deathtouch and gains you 2 life on every attack once ferocious is live, which is the deck’s cheapest way to survive an aggressive start. Thrór’s Map on turn two is never wrong when the hand is short of lands, and Troop of Ponies should be held until it can be sacrificed in a turn where the land drop matters.

Do not panic about the tapped Mirkwood. Losing one turn of mana is worth a land that later adds two permanent counters to the creature of your choice.

Mid game

Turns three to five are where the deck takes over. Ordinary Bear is the pivot card at four mana because it turns on every ferocious trigger in the deck by itself. From that point Wargling attacks give the whole team trample, Wilderland Scrounger attacks put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control, and The Chief Warg draws a card every attack.

Chief Warg’s Company needs two other Wolves to attack, which the deck has no trouble supplying: Wargling, Ravening Warg, Nighthowl Pursuer, Wilderland Scrounger and the Wolf tokens it makes itself all count. Once it can attack it is a 5/3 trampler that also leaves a 2/2 Wolf behind every upkeep, and those tokens are the raw material for Beorn’s anthem and Mirkwood’s counters.

The combo turn

The engine turn is Dancing from Dark to Dawn, then Beorn the Fierce, then a land drop:

  1. Cast Dancing from Dark to Dawn. Every creature spell from now on puts counters equal to its mana value on a creature you control, and every land that enters makes a 2/2 green Bear token.
  2. Cast Beorn the Fierce. His own trigger puts five counters on the best creature you have, and from now on all other Bears get +2/+2, so each Bear token is a 4/4.
  3. Play a land. Landfall makes a Bear token, which Beorn immediately turns into a 4/4. If you have three or more Bears, Beorn’s combat trigger draws you two cards.
  4. Attack. Wargling gives the team trample, Wilderland Scrounger adds a +1/+1 counter to everything, and Beorn’s trample counter on a Wolf converts that Wolf into a Bear for the anthem.

Order matters: cast Beorn before the turn’s land drop if you can, so the Bear token arrives with the anthem already online.

Win condition 2

The second win is the counters line, and it is the plan when the ground is stalled. Every creature spell pumps a target for its mana value, Mirkwood sacrifices for two more counters, Little Bear untaps a Bear and adds a counter, and Quarrel trades the largest body for their best blocker. Once a single creature is at seven or more power, Rogue’s Passage makes it unblockable for four mana and it ends the game on its own — Beorn’s own trample usually makes the second Rogue’s Passage activation lethal.

Note the ordering detail on Mirkwood: it sacrifices itself as a sorcery, so activate it after the land has produced its mana for the turn.

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
Ferocious checks power four or greater All five ferocious cards check for any creature you control with power four or greater when the ability triggers. Ordinary Bear (4/5), Beorn the Fierce (6/6) and Large Bear (5/5) get there on their own; a Bear token with counters or a Wolf under Beorn does too
Beorn’s combat trigger makes new Bears The trample counter goes on up to one target creature you control and that creature becomes a Bear in addition to its other types. Point it at a Wargling or a Wolf token and the anthem begins applying to it, and it counts toward the three-Bear condition for the draw
Chief Warg’s Company needs two other Wolves It is a Wolf itself, but the attack restriction counts only other Wolves. Two of Wargling, Ravening Warg, Nighthowl Pursuer, Wilderland Scrounger, The Chief Warg or a Wolf token satisfy it
Dancing from Dark to Dawn counts creature spells only The counter trigger fires on casting a creature spell, so the activation uses the mana value of what you cast. Landfall tokens do not trigger it, but they do trigger on the land itself
Mirkwood is a sorcery-speed sacrifice The two counters must be used at sorcery speed, and the land is gone afterwards. Activate it after tapping it for mana in the same turn
Little Bear’s untap It untaps another target creature you control on entry; if that creature is a Bear it also gets a +1/+1 counter. Flashing it in during combat untaps an attacker you have already attacked with
Pinecone Strike is modal Three damage to a creature, or destroying an artifact token, or both if you have two targets. It is the cleanest answer to Treasure and Map tokens
No reach in the red splash Beyond Large Bear’s reach and Wilderland Scrounger’s size, the deck is soft to fliers. Quarrel and Pinecone Strike are the answers, and racing with trample is often better than trading

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain counters Ravening Warg’s deathtouch and lifegain is the stabiliser, and Pinecone Strike answers the first two-drop. Trade early, then take over with Ordinary Bear and Chief Warg’s Company
Midrange The strong matchup. Beorn plus repeated landfall Bear tokens outgrow one-for-one removal, and Mirkwood counters win the counter war against anything that does not fly
Control The weak matchup. There is no card advantage beyond The Chief Warg and Beorn’s draw, so commit two threats at a time and rebuild with Dancing from Dark to Dawn’s landfall tokens
Combo Quarrel and Pinecone Strike are the only interaction, so the plan is speed: Wargling and Nighthowl Pursuer pressure, then Beorn plus Rogue’s Passage to close before they assemble

Iterations

Two tuning rounds were run against the viability battery (three 500-game simulations per round). Both rounds were refinements after a passing list, not gate failures.

Round Change Result
