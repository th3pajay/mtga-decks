Overview

Cinder and Coin is today’s The Hobbit set identity deck, and it takes the Smaug x Treasure axis rather than the storied Dwarves, the amass Goblins, or the Elf legends. The thesis is short: everything in the deck is either gold, a dragon, or a way to move one into the other.

The engine is Treasure, produced five different ways — Smaug the Magnificent’s upkeep trigger, The Misty Mountains Cold’s four saga chapters, Dori’s enter trigger, Bejeweled Warg’s combat damage, and The Sackville-Bagginses’ sacrifice trigger. The payoff is Smaug himself: whenever he attacks he deals damage equal to the number of Treasures you control to any target, on top of a 4/3 flying hasty body. Six Treasures turns one attack into ten damage. The gold also pays for everything, which is the quiet part of the deck: in a three-color list, Treasures are the real mana fixing.

The second kill line is on the board, not in the graveyard. The Misty Mountains Cold sacrifices itself at four or more Treasures into a 6/6 flying Dragon token, and Last Light of Durin’s Day converts a Mountain count into a free Dragon from hand or library. Radagast of Rhosgobel rebates two mana off the first creature spell each turn and gives it flash, so those Dragons arrive early and at instant speed. Every card in the list feeds that loop: no filler, no sideboard cards in the maindeck.

The Hobbit set (hob, 193 cards) landed on Arena on 2026-08-14; the import list below works from that date.

Curve and mana

24 lands: 4 The Lonely Mountain, 8 Mountain, 6 Swamp, 6 Forest. Red sources 12, black 6, green 6. Red is the workhorse; black and green are supported but thin, which is why every Treasure token in the deck is also mana fixing — treasure gold is what actually casts Radagast and Wicked Worm on curve.

Nonland curve: 1-drop x2, 2-drop x12, 3-drop x12, 4-drop x8, 5-drop x2. Average nonland CMC 2.89. The deck wants a two-drop on turn 2, a Treasure maker or saga on turn 3, and Smaug the Magnificent on turn 4 with at least two Treasures already banked.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about the hoard and the board, in that order. The Sackville-Bagginses is the ideal turn-2 play: sacrifice nothing and it is just a 2/2, but sacrificing a later Treasure draws a card and mints a replacement, and every token sacrifice pings the opponent for 1. Bejeweled Warg is the aggressive alternative — a 3/2 trampler that converts its first connection into a Treasure. Hold Stir Up Trouble as a one-mana answer; the additional cost is any artifact or creature, so a Treasure token doubles as the removal spell’s fuel.

Do not be precious with Desolation Prowler. It is the cheapest way to keep the ground stable while the engine assembles, and 2 life for +2/+2 is a fine rate when the alternative is dying to a 3/3.

Mid game

The Misty Mountains Cold is the centerpiece of turns three through five. It makes a Treasure on every chapter and, at four or more Treasures, sacrifices itself to create a 6/6 red Dragon token with flying. Dori, Bearer of Friends accelerates the count; Bejeweled Warg and The Sackville-Bagginses add to it. Radagast of Rhosgobel is the mana engine that ties the phase together: the first creature spell you cast each turn costs two less and can be cast as though it had flash, which means Smaug the Magnificent can come down on turn four with mana held up, or at the end of an opponent’s turn once the board is clear.

Snowslope Hunter and Gundabad Opportunist are the card-flow pair. Both exile the top card of your library and let you play it; Snowslope Hunter pays a Treasure, Gundabad Opportunist pays nothing but arrives a turn later. Together they keep the land drops and the removal coming while the hoard grows.

The combo turn

  1. Bank the hoard. Four Treasures is the magic number — at four, The Misty Mountains Cold trades itself for a 6/6 flying Dragon.
  2. Cast Smaug the Magnificent for two red and two generic minus two if Radagast is on the battlefield. He has flying and haste, so he attacks the turn he lands.
  3. Attack. Smaug’s trigger deals damage equal to your Treasure count to any target — the opponent, a planeswalker, or the one blocker that matters — and his 4 combat damage rides along on top.
  4. Repeat. Every upkeep makes another Treasure, so each swing is one point bigger than the last. Four Treasures is 8 damage per turn, six is 10, eight is 12.

A typical kill: a Misty Mountains Dragon token plus Smaug attacking on turn five with four Treasures is 6 flying plus 8 to the face with 4 more from Smaug’s own body. Most boards that are not already dead are dead on the next swing.

Win condition 2

The Master of Lake-town converts the same attack into library damage: whenever a player loses life, that player mills that many cards. Smaug’s trigger causes loss of life, so a four-Treasure attack mills 8 and then hits for 8. When The Master dies, it draws a card for each graveyard holding seven or more cards, so the mill line refills your own hand as the opponent’s library empties. Last Light of Durin’s Day is the third door: with six quest counters it puts a Dragon directly onto the battlefield for free, and its Mountaincycling for two means the card never rots in hand.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two to three lands and either a Treasure maker (The Sackville-Bagginses, Bejeweled Warg, Dori, The Misty Mountains Cold) or Stir Up Trouble. The best openers are two lands, a two-drop, and a three-drop. Mulligan one-land hands and hands with five or more lands and no engine. Because green and black are light splashes, a hand of red lands plus a Treasure maker is keepable, but a hand that can never cast a two-drop is not.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Smaug the Magnificent counts Treasures you control at the moment the trigger resolves, not when you attack — do not sacrifice a Treasure for Stir Up Trouble’s cost before combat if you plan to attack with Smaug.
  • The Misty Mountains Cold checks four or more Treasures after each chapter, so with Dori and Bejeweled Warg already banked it can pop its Dragon as early as chapter two.
  • Last Light of Durin’s Day counts Mountains entering, not lands. The Lonely Mountain is a Mountain; Swamp and Forest are not. Search Mountains specifically if you are racing the six counters.
  • Smaug, Wicked Worm counts artifacts the opponent controls at entry, so it is weakest against creature decks and enormous against artifact and Equipment decks. Its second ability triggers on any spell cast with Treasure mana, which includes spells paid for by the Treasures it just made.
  • Snowslope Hunter’s ability is once per turn and only during your turn, and it can eat the Warg’s Treasure or a spent token — it is a Treasure outlet, not a Treasure tax.
  • Radagast of Rhosgobel’s rebate applies to the first creature spell each turn, so sequence a cheap creature first only if you do not want the discount on a Dragon. It grants flash to the spell it discounts.
  • The Lonely Mountain enters tapped unless you control an Equipment. This list plays no Equipment, so treat it as an untapped-red-land on turn one or a tapped red source later, and do not count on the Dwarf token ability as a plan.
