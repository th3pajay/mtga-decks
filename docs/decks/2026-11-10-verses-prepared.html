Reality Fracture is Standard-legal, but every card in the set still carries no arena_id in the card data. The import list at the end becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there.

Overview

Prepared is Reality Fracture’s flagship mechanic. A creature “enters prepared” (or becomes prepared from an upkeep trigger) and while it is prepared you may cast a copy of its spell; casting the copy unprepares it. The double-faced cards of the set are literally the payload: the front face is the body, the back face is the spell.

Most prepared creatures ask a real price for that loop — the Soul Tether cycle, for example, wants three mana for a mana rock. This deck picks the prepared creatures whose spells cost one mana, the ones you can actually cast every turn:

  • Pompous Battlemage ({R}, 1/1 prowess) hands you Improvised Act ({R}, loot) the turn it lands.
  • Stingerquill Voxmancer ({B/R}, 1/2) re-prepares itself for free at every upkeep, and its Vicious Verse ({B/R}) deals 1 damage to an opponent without ever attacking.
  • Pyre Rhymer ({1}{R}{R}, 3/3 prowess) gives Molten Tide ({R}): every Mountain you tap for the rest of the turn adds an additional {R}.

Everything else multiplies those three: Codie, Ravenous Codex copies each prepared spell you cast, Hexhaven Dueling Arena re-arms a creature that already attacked, and Winter, Team Player turns the spell count into team power. The black splash is cheap — Vicious Verse and Grim Repriser are hybrid, so only six Swamps and three Haunted Ridge pay for Mabel, Bitter Recluse, Rank Rat and Yargle, Glutton of Urborg.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-2, open on prepared bodies. Pompous Battlemage and Stingerquill Voxmancer both attack and both leave a spell behind. Cast the copy immediately: a turn-one loot or a turn-two drain beats a stored trick, and every copy triggers prowess. Lightning Strike and Sear clear the first blocker so the small bodies keep connecting.
  2. Turns 3-5, build the multiplier. Pyre Rhymer must be answered — 3/3 prowess plus Molten Tide makes the next spell effectively free. Codie turns one Verse per turn into two. Hexhaven Dueling Arena re-arms whichever prepared creature attacked, so the free spell keeps coming without spending a card, and Winter, Team Player convokes off the 1/1s.
  3. Turns 5-7, convert. Attack with the prowess team, hold Blossoming Defense or Snakeskin Veil for the removal spell that matters, and use Hexhaven after combat for one more copy on the lethal turn. If the ground stalls, the drain half finishes it: one Vicious Verse per upkeep, doubled by Codie, plus Yargle, Glutton of Urborg as a 9/3 that ends a race in two swings.

Curve and mana

Sixteen cards cost one mana, twelve cost two, five cost three, and only four cost five or more. The deck wants three lands and then stops caring, which is why 23 lands is right rather than 24: Hexhaven produces colourless mana and every extra land is a spell you did not cast. Rockfall Vale and Haunted Ridge need two other lands before they arrive untapped, so the first two drops should be basics.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead on Pompous Battlemage or Stingerquill Voxmancer, never on a tapland. Cast the prepared copy the same turn when the mana is free: the copy is a prowess trigger even when the effect is small. Against a creature deck, Shock or Lightning Strike on the first blocker usually beats holding it, because the one-drops stop mattering once the opponent stabilises.

Mid game and the combo turn

The engine turn is Pyre Rhymer plus Molten Tide. Molten Tide is not a classic ritual — it multiplies, so cast everything you can before it, then tap Mountains afterwards. Two copies (one from the creature, one bought back with Hexhaven after attacking) make each Mountain produce three red mana: enough for a protected Yargle, Glutton of Urborg and a Sear in the same turn.

Codie changes the maths on every prepared spell: one Vicious Verse becomes two damage, one Improvised Act becomes two loots. Its activated ability costs one mana of each colour, so treat Codie as a 1/4 body with a free copy clause.

Win condition 2 — drain

Without attacking, the deck still kills. Stingerquill Voxmancer re-prepares at every upkeep, so from turn two it is a free Vicious Verse each turn; with Codie that is 2 damage per turn from a one-mana creature. Grim Repriser turns those points into recursion, and Rank Rat plus Mabel, Bitter Recluse hold the board while the drain adds up. Against control this is the primary plan: attack only when the opponent taps out.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with a one-drop prepared creature plus two lands, and keep Pyre Rhymer with three lands. Ship hands that need both a third colour and a fourth land, and one-land hands without Shock or Lightning Strike — the deck has no way to dig out of mana trouble beyond Improvised Act. Six-land hands are keepable only with Codie.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Preparation exists only on creatures with prepare spells, and in this deck that is exactly the three double-faced creatures: Pompous Battlemage, Stingerquill Voxmancer and Pyre Rhymer. Hexhaven does nothing for the other six creatures.
  • Casting a copy unprepares the creature, so each body pays out once per preparation. Stingerquill Voxmancer is the exception: its upkeep trigger re-prepares it for free.
  • Hexhaven’s two-mana ability is sorcery-speed and needs a creature that attacked this turn; the four-mana ability has no such restriction and is the one to use on defence.
  • Molten Tide only counts Mountains: Rockfall Vale and Haunted Ridge are not Mountains, so the ritual is best with basics in play.
  • Vicious Verse targets an opponent, so it always satisfies Grim Repriser’s noncombat-damage condition — the Repriser is a repeatable two-mana rebuy in the drain plan.
  • Winter, Team Player counts every noncreature spell, including prepared copies: convoke it with the team, then cast two copies for six or more power of pump.
