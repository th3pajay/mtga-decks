Overview

The deck presses two clocks that are paid for by the same action: a creature turning face up.

Disguise supplies the bodies. Every disguise creature can be cast face down for three generic mana as a 2/2 with ward 2, so the deck’s early board dodges cheap removal and blocks well while holding mana up. Each disguise creature then turns face up for its disguise cost and pays off immediately: Dog Walker makes two 1/1 Dogs, Unyielding Gatekeeper exiles a nonland permanent, Nervous Gardener finds a land, Crowd-Control Warden arrives with a fistful of counters, and Concealed Weapon attaches itself to a creature you control.

Pyrotechnic Performer converts that motion into damage: whenever it or another creature you control is turned face up, that creature deals damage equal to its power to each opponent. A Lizard Assassin that flips for a single red mana is three damage; a Crowd-Control Warden that flips for five mana with four other creatures out is eight damage. Multiple flips in one turn stack into a burn kill.

Valiant supplies the second half of the engine. Valiant triggers the first time each turn a Valiant creature becomes the target of a spell or ability you control, and this deck is built to aim something at a Mouse every single turn. Seedglaive Mentor grows, Emberheart Challenger draws a card it may play, Whiskervale Forerunner cheats a small creature onto the battlefield, and Mouse Trapper taps down a blocker. The fusion is that the Disguise half generates the triggers: a flip ability that targets your own creature, Concealed Weapon’s attach and its repeatable equip, Ferocification’s beginning-of-combat choice, and Dual-Sun Technique all point at Mice while doing their own work.

Approach

Game plan phases

Phase Turns What you are doing
Set up 1 to 3 Deploy two-drops; at three mana prefer a face-down disguise body over a fragile threat. Valiant Mice come down and start pressuring.
Convert 4 to 6 Turn creatures face up in combat for surprise blocks or surprise damage; aim a target at a Mouse each turn to keep Valiant firing.
Finish 6 and later Chain flips with Expose the Culprit for a burn turn, or swing with counters plus double strike and an unblockable attacker.

Curve and mana

Twenty four lands, average mana value 2.44, and twenty four cards at two mana: the deck wants four lands, casts two spells a turn from turn three onward, and never needs more than five. Eight of the duals enter tapped with surveil attached, so a two land opening that holds a surveil dual is still a keep as long as the other land is a basic of the right colour. Green appears on only six cards, so a Forest count of four is enough; the green sources matter most on turn two for Nervous Gardener and on turn five for Crowd-Control Warden.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with whatever two-drop your colours support, and remember that a face-down disguise creature at three mana is often the strongest development: it dodges two-mana removal through ward 2, blocks anything up to a 3/3, and threatens a flip later. Aim your first target effect at the Valiant creature that matters most in that matchup: Seedglaive Mentor against decks you must race, Emberheart Challenger when you need to dig, Mouse Trapper against decks that attack.

Mid game

Flip on the opponent’s turn when it matters. Dog Walker flipping into two Dogs is a combat blowout and a burn trigger at the same time; Unyielding Gatekeeper’s flip removes a key permanent. Concealed Weapon is the quiet engine of this phase: flip it onto a Mouse for a Valiant trigger, then equip 1R during every following combat to trigger Valiant again and keep the plus three plus zero moving. Ferocification does the same job for free from turn four onward.

The combo turn

Set the board with face-down bodies and one face-up Pyrotechnic Performer, then flip as many creatures as your mana allows in one turn.

Sequence Damage to each opponent
Flip Pyrotechnic Performer for R 3
Flip Dog Walker for a hybrid mana 3, plus two 1/1 Dogs
Expose the Culprit, mode one, on a face-down Crowd-Control Warden 8 or more depending on your other creatures
Expose the Culprit, mode two, re-cloaking the flipped creatures 0 now, but repeats the entire turn later for their mana costs

Three mana of flips (a Pyrotechnic Performer at one red, a Dog Walker at two hybrid) is already six damage to each opponent on top of the board, and Crowd-Control Warden’s counters and Seedglaive Mentor’s Valiant counters push single flips into double digits. The re-cloak mode is what makes the plan repeatable: cloaked cards may be turned face up again for their mana cost, which is another turn-up event, which is another Pyrotechnic Performer trigger.

Win condition two

Counters plus double strike. Seedglaive Mentor collects plus one plus one counters from every Valiant trigger, Dual-Sun Technique grants double strike and draws a card once a counter is present, and haste means the damage starts the turn it lands. Mouse Trapper clears a blocker on the way in. When a single large attacker is the plan, Rogue’s Passage makes it unblockable, which is the deck’s answer to a stalled ground. Crowd-Control Warden is the deck’s biggest body and its best burn trigger; Whiskervale Forerunner can put a small creature directly onto the battlefield during combat, which is extra pressure and an extra body for the Warden’s flip.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands, at least one red source, and a two-mana play. A two land hand that contains Nervous Gardener or a surveil dual is a keep. Send back hands with no two-drop, all tapped lands with a five-cost plan, or fewer than two red sources. In this deck the disguise creatures are the flexible slots: they are castable at three mana face down regardless of colour, so a hand of three lands, a disguise creature and a Valiant Mouse is always functional.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Cloak is not the same as disguise. Expose the Culprit re-cloaks face-up disguise creatures, and a cloaked card turns face up for its mana cost rather than its disguise cost. That is a turn-up event, so Pyrotechnic Performer triggers and the creature’s own flip ability repeats.
  • Do not exile your own face-down creature with Unyielding Gatekeeper’s flip trigger if you want the flip payoff. It returns to the battlefield tapped and face up, which is not a turn-up event: no flip ability, no burn.
  • Valiant fires only the first time each turn. Order your enablers deliberately: Ferocification at the beginning of combat, then the equip, then a spell, so each Valiant creature gets exactly one meaningful target and the counters land where the attack is going.
  • Emberheart Challenger’s Valiant trigger exiles a card you may play this turn. A creature card exiled this way can still be cast face down for three mana, so the impulse draw feeds the disguise plan.
  • Whiskervale Forerunner’s Valiant trigger works at instant speed on your turn only for the battlefield clause; if it is not your turn the creature goes to hand instead, which is still a fine way to draw past lands.
  • Face-down 2/2s with ward 2 make cheap removal and targeted discard awkward. Do not flip early just because you can: hold the mana and the information.
  • Crowd-Control Warden’s counters are counted on entry or flip, so flip it after the Dogs from Dog Walker are already on the battlefield.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Race with haste and double strike, use Mouse Trapper’s flash trigger to tap their best attacker, Rabid Gnaw to trade up, and disguise bodies to block profitably.
Control Deploy face-down, force a sweeper, then flip in the window after. Ward 2 makes single-target removal cost two extra mana.
Midrange Trade early, then close with a counter-loaded Warden or Seedglaive Mentor made unblockable by Rogue’s Passage.
Combo Race. The burn plan does not need the attack step, so flips plus Dual-Sun Technique end games through a stalled board.
