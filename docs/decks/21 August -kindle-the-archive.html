Overview

Kindle the Archive is a five-color combo deck built around the fusion of three mechanics: Vivid (effects that scale with the number of colors among permanents you control), Firebending (attacking creatures generate red mana), and Surveil (dig toward the engine while filling the graveyard). The deck’s thesis: surveil to stock the archive, then let Iroh, Grand Lotus set the archive on fire.

Iroh, Grand Lotus ({3}{G}{U}{R}) grants every instant and sorcery in your graveyard flashback during your turn, and every Lesson flashback for just {1}. That turns the burn suite — Burst Lightning, Traumatic Critique, Firebending Lesson — into a reusable arsenal. Each turn you can recast the same burn spells from the graveyard, spending the red mana generated by Firebending attackers.

The deck plays three complementary engines:

  • Surveil digging — Glarb, Calamity's Augur, Snarling Gorehound, Risky Research, Prismari Charm, and the surveil lands fill the graveyard with burn spells and find Iroh. Glarb also casts spells with mana value 4 or greater from the top of your library, which is exactly Iroh’s price range.
  • The Iroh flashback loop — once Iroh is down, every burn spell in the yard is a live draw. Fire Lord Azula copies each spell cast while she attacks, Firebender Ascension copies Azula’s own trigger (and every Firebending mana trigger) once it reaches four quest counters, and Sozin's Comet or Fire Nation Palace turn your whole team into red-mana batteries.
  • Vivid payoffs — Squawkroaster, Glister Bairn, Shimmercreep, Explosive Prodigy, and Bloom Tender all scale with the five colors in your permanents. The manabase itself is the Vivid enabler: a five-color board means every Vivid X is 5.

Ozai, the Phoenix King is the capstone: he turns unspent mana red (so Firebending mana pools with everything else) and becomes flying and indestructible whenever you hold six or more unspent mana — which the Firebending engine produces almost every combat.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Build the archive (turns 1–3): surveil with Gorehound, Glarb, Risky Research, and the surveil lands. Let burn spells hit the graveyard naturally or through Traumatic Critique’s “draw two, discard one”. Keep the board with early burn and Prismari Charm.
  2. Light the flame (turns 4–6): resolve Iroh, Grand Lotus. Attack with Firebending creatures (Fire Lord Azula, Sun Warriors, Iroh himself) to stockpile red mana. Cast flashback burn from the yard — Azula copies it while she attacks.
  3. Burn the library down: loop the flashback chain each turn, escalate with Firebender Ascension (copying Azula’s copy trigger), Sozin's Comet (firebending 5 on every creature), and Ozai (unspent mana becomes red). Shimmercreep and Explosive Prodigy add on-entry damage; Vivid beatdown closes games where burn stalls.

Curve and mana

24 lands: 18 nonbasic fixing/utility lands plus 7 basics (2 Forest, 2 Swamp, 1 Island, 1 Mountain, 1 Plains). The manabase is the deck’s Vivid engine — five colors among permanents means every Vivid X equals 5, so fixing lands are payoff lands. Mirage Mesa and Jasmine Dragon Tea Shop (for the Ally engine) smooth the five-color demands; Bloom Tender and Training Compound accelerate into Iroh on turn 4.

The nonland curve is 2 one-drops, 6 twos, 5 threes, 10 fours, 6 fives, 5 sixes, 2 seven-plus — an average mana value under 3. The deck plays most of its burn at one or two mana and leans on Iroh (5) and Ozai (6) as the top end.

Gameplay strategy

Early game (turns 1–3)

  • Turn 1: Snarling Gorehound (every subsequent small creature surveils 1) or Burst Lightning for an early blocker. Surveil aggressively — the graveyard is the deck’s second hand.
  • Turn 2: Glarb, Calamity’s Augur (tap to surveil 2, and it casts your four- and five-drops from the top), Risky Research (surveil 2, draw two), or burn. Explosive Prodigy on turn 2 deals 3-4 damage to a creature and adds red to the color count.
  • Hold Prismari Charm or Unsubtle Mockery for the turn-2/3 threat. Discard lands or redundant burn to Traumatic Critique’s loot — the archive wants them.

Mid game (turns 4–6)

  • Resolve Iroh, Grand Lotus as the priority. With Glarb out, cast it from the top of the library. With Bloom Tender or a five-color board, it comes down on turn 4.
  • Once Iroh is down, the flashback suite is live: Burst Lightning for {R}, Firebending Lesson for {1}, Traumatic Critique for {X}{U}{R}. Attack with Firebending creatures first, generate red, then cast during combat while the mana is available.
  • Fire Lord Azula attacks with firebending 2, and every spell cast while she attacks is copied — a kicked Burst Lightning becomes 8 damage. Protect her with removal and Prismari Charm bounce.
  • Firebender Ascension accumulates quest counters every time an attacking creature triggers (Azula’s copy trigger, every Firebending trigger). At four counters it copies those abilities — Azula’s trigger copies twice, Firebending mana doubles.

The combo turn

  1. Confirm Iroh is on the battlefield and the archive holds at least two or three burn spells (surveil lands, Glarb, and Traumatic Critique’s loot keep it stocked).
  2. Enter combat. Attack with Fire Lord Azula and Iroh (firebending 2 each), ideally with Sozin's Comet cast first (firebending 5 on everything) or Fire Nation Palace activated ({1}{R}, {T}) to grant firebending 4 — an Iroh with both printed firebending 2 and the Palace’s firebending 4 generates 6 red mana per attack.
  3. With the combat mana banked, cast from the graveyard: Burst Lightning, Firebending Lesson, Traumatic Critique with X as high as the mana allows. Azula copies each spell; with Firebender Ascension at four quest counters, the copy trigger itself is copied.
  4. Repeat each turn. Shimmercreep and Explosive Prodigy add on-entry damage, Ozai turns leftover mana red and becomes an indestructible flyer at six-plus unspent mana, and the loop outpaces any removal that isn’t graveyard hate.

Win condition 2: the Vivid beatdown

Burn is plan A; the board is plan B. Squawkroaster is a double striker whose power equals the number of colors among permanents you control — 5 or more in this deck, so it attacks as a 5/5 double strike minimum. Glister Bairn makes it +X/+X bigger every combat, and Prismabasher-style team pumps come from the same Vivid scaling. Self-Destruct turns a pumped Squawkroaster into a one-shot fireball (it deals damage equal to its power to any target), and Ozai — trample, haste, firebending 4, flying and indestructible at six-plus unspent mana — closes the game alone. Shimmercreep’s life drain also wins without ever attacking.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: 2–3 lands covering red plus one other color, a surveil source (Glarb, Gorehound, Risky Research) or early burn, and a path to Iroh by turn 5.
  • Trim: 5+ land hands, single-color hands, and hands with only the top end (Ozai, Sozin’s Comet, multiple Traumatic Critiques) and no early play.
  • The deck is happy to trade card quantity for quality: Traumatic Critique, Glarb, and the surveil lands all convert surplus cards into archive fuel.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Multiple Firebending instances stack. A creature with firebending 2 (Iroh) that gains firebending 4 from Fire Nation Palace triggers twice: 6 red mana per attack. Sozin’s Comet on top makes it 9.
  • Firebending mana lasts until end of combat — cast the flashback burn during the declare-attackers step while the mana is available. Iroh’s flashback works any time during your turn.
  • Iroh’s flashback is only during your turn, and each cast exiles the card after resolution — the loop is per-turn, not infinite. Multiple copies of a burn spell in the yard extend the chain.
  • Firebender Ascension copies attacking-creature triggered abilities — Azula’s spell-copy trigger and every Firebending mana trigger qualify. It does not copy static abilities.
  • Traumatic Critique’s loot is a feature: discarding burn into the archive sets up next turn’s flashback chain.
  • Glarb casts MV 4+ spells from the top — Iroh, Ozai, Squawkroaster, and Shimmercreep all qualify; surveil first to put the right card on top.
  • Ozai’s mana conversion means unspent Firebending red, treasure from Flick a Coin-style effects, and other colors all become red, letting one combat’s mana cast any mix of flashback spells.
  • Graveyard hate is the deck’s Achilles heel — versus heavy exile, pivot to the Vivid beatdown plan (Squawkroaster, Ozai) and stop over-investing in the archive.

Matchups

  • Aggro: early burn clears the board; Explosive Prodigy and Shimmercreep trade life-for-life favorably; Ozai becomes an indestructible wall at six unspent mana. Surveil toward removal first, engine second.
  • Control: the flashback loop is card-advantage-proof — every burn spell is a reusable threat, and counterspells only delay it. Glarb’s top-of-library casting dodges hand disruption. Fire Nation Palace’s firebending grant is instant-speed and uncounterable.
  • Midrange: race their threats with copied burn; Azula plus a stocked archive out-damages most midrange clocks. Squawkroaster beats blockers with double strike.
  • Graveyard hate (Rest in Peace-style): swap to plan B — Squawkroaster plus Self-Destruct and Ozai beatdown; keep surveil minimal and use the Vivid creatures as the primary threat.
