Overview

Warp lets you cast a card for its warp cost instead of its mana cost, exiles it at the beginning of the next end step, and then lets you cast it from exile on a later turn. That single line gives the deck two things at once: a permanent that enters the battlefield for far less than its printed price, and a card that is not spent when it leaves — it comes back as a real card in exile, ready to be cast again when the mana is there.

The deck converts those entries into damage. Three cards are paid for the same resource: Impact Tremors, Warleader’s Call and Weftstalker Ardent each deal 1 damage to every opponent when a creature you control enters. With all three on the battlefield a single creature entering is 3 damage; with Delney, Streetwise Lookout doubling Weftstalker Ardent’s trigger it is 4. Warp keeps supplying entries for two mana, and the token makers — Knight Luminary, Rayblade Trooper, Exalted Sunborn, Fountainport — turn one entry into three.

Approach

Game plan by phase

  1. Turns one and two: Impact Tremors is the best opening in the deck, and Lightning Helix is the interaction that keeps you alive while the enchantments come down. A warped Knight Luminary or Weftstalker Ardent on turn two is already two damage per opponent before any attack.
  2. Turns three and four: add the second and third payoff. Warleader’s Call pumps the board, Weftstalker Ardent stacks another point per entry, and Delney doubles it. Tannuk, Steadfast Second gives the team haste, which matters because most of the bodies only live for one turn.
  3. Turns five and up: token doubling and reach. Exalted Sunborn makes every Soldier and every Rayblade Trooper death token into two, Fountainport adds a Fish every turn, and Rogue’s Passage pushes a 4/5 flier or a pumped token through for the last points.

Twenty-four lands, twenty white sources and nineteen red ones once Evolving Wilds is counted for either. The printed curve is deliberately top-heavy: the four, five and six mana cards are all warp cards, so their real cost on the turn that matters is two or three mana, and only the cards you draw late are paid for in full.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with Impact Tremors whenever it is in the opening hand; it turns every later warp into damage and costs only two mana. On turn two the correct play is usually a warped one: Knight Luminary for two mana brings a Soldier, Weftstalker Ardent for one red mana is the cheapest ping in the deck, and Haliya for one white mana starts the lifegain count that draws cards later. Lightning Helix is for the creature that would otherwise eat your enchantments, not for the face. The warped permanent leaving at end of turn is not a loss: it goes to exile and stays castable.

Mid game

Turns three to five are about stacking payoffs. Warleader’s Call is the single most important card in the mid game because it both pings and pumps, which is what lets a board of 1/1 Soldiers actually attack. Tannuk, Steadfast Second is what makes the plan work on the turn it is played: the whole team has haste, so a warped Nova Hellkite or a Knight Luminary can attack immediately and then leave at end of turn with its damage already dealt. Delney is the other key piece, since doubling Weftstalker Ardent doubles the deck’s main clock.

The combo turn

The deck’s best turn is a two-mana warp that becomes four entries. With Impact Tremors, Warleader’s Call and Weftstalker Ardent on the battlefield, and Delney out, every creature that enters deals 4 damage to each opponent. Warp Knight Luminary for two mana: the Knight is one entry, and with Exalted Sunborn on the battlefield its Soldier is doubled into two more. Three entries is 12 damage to each opponent for two mana, and the same turn the Soldiers are 1/1s with Warleader’s Call’s +1/+1 on them. Recast the same Knight from exile later in the game for four mana and do it again; Possibility Technician turns each Kavu entry into an extra card off the top, so the loop refuels itself.

Win condition two

When the drain is not enough, the deck attacks. Nova Hellkite is a 4/5 flier with haste that also pings a creature on entry, Exalted Sunborn is a 4/5 flier with lifelink that doubles every token you make, and Warleader’s Call plus Memorial Team Leader make even the Soldier tokens into a real board. Rogue’s Passage is the finisher: four mana makes the largest body unblockable, which is usually the difference between a stalled board and lethal. Fountainport belongs to this plan too, since a free 1/1 each turn both adds to the attack and triggers all three drain payoffs.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands with at least one Impact Tremors or Weftstalker Ardent, plus a cheap warp card to cast. Hands with two payoffs and no action are worse than they look, because the payoffs do nothing alone. Ship hands that need double white or double red on turn three without the matching basics, and ship hands whose only plays cost four or more unless Tannuk is among them, since Tannuk is what makes those cards castable early.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Warp exiles the permanent at the beginning of the next end step, so a warped creature gets exactly one combat step and then leaves. Attack first, then let it go.
  • An exiled warped card can be cast again on a later turn, but that cast pays the printed cost, not the warp cost. Warp is a discount once per copy, not a permanent discount.
  • All-Fates Stalker exiles a creature until it leaves the battlefield, so warping it out returns the creature you removed. Cast it for four mana when you want the exile to stick.
  • Delney only doubles creatures with power 2 or less: Weftstalker Ardent, Rayblade Trooper and All-Fates Stalker qualify, while Haliya, Knight Luminary, Tannuk and Possibility Technician do not.
  • Haliya’s draw needs three or more life gained in the same turn. Three entries does it, and so does any entry plus Lightning Helix.
  • Rayblade Trooper only makes its Soldier when a nontoken creature with a +1/+1 counter dies. Warped creatures are exiled, not destroyed, so the trigger usually comes from a counter placed by Rayblade Trooper itself or by Weftblade Enhancer.
  • Tannuk, Steadfast Second is legendary. Warping a second copy while one is on the battlefield means one of them dies to the legend rule, so hold the second copy until the first is gone.
  • Possibility Technician needs a Kavu on the battlefield to cast the card it exiles. It is a Kavu itself, so it works alone, but if it is removed the exiled card is stranded until Tannuk or Memorial Team Leader arrives.
  • Exalted Sunborn doubles tokens, not entries: the two Soldiers it creates are two separate drain triggers, which is exactly why it is in the deck.

Matchups

Against aggro the deck is a favourite after sideboarding in spirit rather than in cards: Lightning Helix and All-Fates Stalker buy the turns, and Weftstalker Ardent plus Impact Tremors race small creature decks well because the damage does not require attacking. Against midrange the plan is to grind: every warped card is a second card later, and the drain payoffs do not care about blockers. Against control the deck wants Impact Tremors and Warleader’s Call down before the sweeper, since enchantments survive creature removal, and Delney makes the Soldier tokens unblockable against large blockers. Against combo there is no counterspell in the list, so the deck has to race with the drain plan and use Rogue’s Passage for the last points.
