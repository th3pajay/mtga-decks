Overview

Reality Fracture’s flagship mechanic is prepared: a creature enters prepared or is re-armed by a trigger, and while it is prepared you may cast a copy of the spell printed on its back face. Casting that copy unprepares the creature. Most prepared creatures therefore give you one free spell. This deck is built on the small group that gives you one every single turn.

Two of them are the engine. Woodwork Prodigy ({2}{R/G}, 3/3 Cat Druid) becomes prepared at the beginning of your upkeep if it is not already, and its back face is Soul Tether ({2}{R/G} sorcery: create a Heartwood token, a red and green artifact with {T}: Add {R} or {G}). Stingerquill Voxmancer ({B/R}, 1/2 Goblin Sorcerer) has the same upkeep trigger with Vicious Verse ({B/R} sorcery: deal 1 damage to target opponent) on the back. One grows your mana every turn for free; the other bleeds the opponent every turn for free.

The rest of the list is built to convert “free spell, every turn” into damage. Noncreature-cast payoffs turn each copy into extra points: Firebrand Archer and Firespitter Whelp each deal 1 damage to every opponent, Chandra’s Emberling grows, Grim Repriser comes back from the graveyard, and every prowess creature in the deck gets bigger. Because the copies cost no mana, the engine runs while your lands are spent on creatures.

The front halves matter too. Heartwood Crafter and Konstrari Improviser enter prepared, so they are a body plus a free Heartwood on curve. Pompous Battlemage is a one-mana prowess threat with a free loot. Codie is the force multiplier: each prepared spell you cast is copied, so one free Vicious Verse drains twice and one free Soul Tether makes two Heartwood tokens.

Twenty-six lands, eleven one-drops and nine two-drops: the deck has a prepared body on turn one in most games, which is what holds goldfish turn-four consistency at 0.73 and P(four lands by turn four) at 0.737. Hybrid symbols ({R/G}, {B/R}) mean almost every land casts almost every spell, which is why 24 basics are enough for three colours.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a prepared body, not with a payoff. Heartwood Crafter on one is the ideal curve: it enters prepared, so turn one is a 1/1 plus a free Soul Tether and a Heartwood token, and every later Soul Tether copy is another mana rock. Its own {T}: Add {C} cannot pay for spells cast from your hand, so treat that ability as a bonus rather than a plan.

Stingerquill Voxmancer on one is the best turn-one play when you expect a long game — it is the cheapest repeatable engine piece in the deck. Konstrari Improviser on two is the stronger clock: a 2/2 plus a Heartwood. Do not trade Heartwood tokens for damage; they are the mana that casts Codie and Pyre Rhymer, and each one is also an artifact body that survives a sweeper.

The combo turn

The engine turn is not a single explosive loop — it is a repeating sequence you set up and then repeat every upkeep.

  1. Have Woodwork Prodigy and Codie, Ravenous Codex on the battlefield by turn five.
  2. In your upkeep, Woodwork Prodigy becomes prepared. Cast the Soul Tether copy. Codie copies it: two Heartwood tokens, two mana rocks, zero mana spent.
  3. Cast anything else with a back face that is still prepared (Konstrari Improviser, Heartwood Crafter, Pyre Rhymer). Each cast is copied by Codie, and each cast triggers prowess, Firebrand Archer, Firespitter Whelp and Chandra’s Emberling.
  4. Attack. After attackers are declared, pay {2} and tap Hexhaven Dueling Arena to make the best attacker prepared again, then cast that back face in your second main phase for one more round of triggers.
  5. With Stingerquill Voxmancer also on the board, the same upkeep produced a free Vicious Verse, which already turned on Grim Repriser’s recursion clause for the turn.

The damage adds up from three directions at once: Vicious Verse copies hit for 1 each (2 with Codie), the ping payoffs add 1 per opponent per copy, and every prowess attacker is 1/1 to 3/1 larger than printed by the time combat starts.

Win condition 2 — Codie’s mass preparation

Codie’s second ability, {W}{U}{B}{R}{G}, {T}, makes each creature you control prepared. Because every creature in this list has a back face, that is a board-wide free-spell turn: cast each back face, each one copied by Codie, each one triggering the entire payoff team. Two prepared bodies and a ping team is usually eight to twelve points of noncombat damage plus a lethal prowess swing. Save it for the turn you can also attack, since the copies are sorceries and instants you cast in the second main phase.

Against a stalled board, the same turn is often just Pyre Rhymer’s Molten Tide: for {R} every Mountain you tap adds an extra {R}, which pays for a second and third prepared spell in the same turn.

One land, two one-drops, no two-drop Yes on the play Eleven one-drops make this functional; on the draw it is a mulligan
Codie plus two ping payoffs, no prepared creature No Codie copies nothing without prepared spells to cast
Five lands, Grim Repriser, Firespitter Whelp No No engine, and Grim Repriser cannot return without noncombat damage

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Prepared is one-shot per preparation. Casting the back-face copy unprepares the creature. Only Woodwork Prodigy and Stingerquill Voxmancer re-arm themselves, and only in your upkeep — so removal pointed at them costs you every future free spell, not just a body.
  • Codie copies, it does not cast. The copy happens without being cast, so it does not trigger Firebrand Archer or prowess a second time. The value is the duplicated effect, not duplicated triggers.
  • Hexhaven Dueling Arena is sorcery speed on its {2} mode. You must activate it in your second main phase after attackers are declared; the {4} mode has no attack requirement, which is how you re-arm a blocker.
  • Grim Repriser needs noncombat damage. Vicious Verse copies, Firebrand Archer and Firespitter Whelp all satisfy it. In a turn where none of them resolved, the graveyard ability is switched off.
  • Molten Tide only counts Mountains. It is a one-turn ritual for {R} per Mountain tap, not a permanent effect.
  • Codie is a 1/4 artifact creature. It blocks well but dies to any two-power attacker.
  • A copy of a sorcery still obeys timing. Sorcery back faces are main-phase only, so engine turns are sequenced around combat.

Matchups

Opponent Plan

Control Hold Woodwork Prodigy until they tap out on their turn, and keep a second engine piece in hand. Codie’s mass-preparation turn is the one they must counter
Midrange Grind with Grim Repriser recursion; their one-for-one removal cannot keep up with two engines
Combo No countermagic, so the plan is speed: turn-one prepared body, turn-three payoff, and Vicious Verse copies to close the last points
