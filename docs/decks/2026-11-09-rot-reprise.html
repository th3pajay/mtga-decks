Rot Reprise

Four-color blight and flashback: your own tokens are the fuel

Overview

Blight is a cost, not a payoff: “blight N” means put N -1/-1 counters on a creature you control. On a 1/1 token that is a sacrifice with no extra card and no extra mana attached. Rot Reprise is built to pay that cost as often as possible, and to get paid for it four different ways.

The engine runs on three death-triggered payoffs. Arnyn, Deathbloom Botanist drains 2 for every creature with power or toughness 1 or less that dies, which is every Goblin token, every Rat token and Gristle Glutton. Boggart Mischief drains 1 more whenever a Goblin dies, and Al Bhed Salvagers drains 1 whenever any creature dies. Heirloom Auntie surveils on each death and sheds a -1/-1 counter while doing it, so the same deaths that drain also fill the graveyard and turn your 2/2 back into a 4/4.

Flashback is the second half of the loop, and every token maker in the deck has it: Tidings of War amasses 3 from the graveyard, Revenge of the Rats makes a Rat per creature card in the yard, Plunder the Trollshaws draws two when it comes back. Blight empties the board, flashback refills it, and the refill is worth more than the first cast. Gristle Glutton is the hinge, a repeatable loot that discards the flashback spells and hands you the cards to cast them.

The deck does not need to attack to win, but it can: two Gutsplitter Gang and the manland carry the combat plan while the drain does the rest.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Set the table (turns 1 to 3). Land a threat that survives its own blight costs: Heirloom Auntie arrives a 2/2 with two counters and grows back to 4/4, Gristle Glutton is a 1/3 that absorbs a counter and keeps looting. Tidings of War on turn one leaves a Goblin Army to spend later.
  2. Establish the drain engine (turns 3 to 5). Arnyn plus Boggart Mischief is the core pair: every blight payment now costs you a token instead of a card and deals 3 to 4 damage. Al Bhed Salvagers adds a fourth point. Start tracking deaths; each one also triggers Heirloom Auntie.
  3. Refill and finish (turns 5 onward). Cast Revenge of the Rats from hand, then flash it back for a second wave, and flash back Tidings of War for a 3/3-ish Army. Spend the new tokens on Shadow Urchin attacks and Pyrrhic Strike, and let the drain close the game.

Curve and mana

The deck wants two lands and a two-drop, not a five-drop. Nineteen of the thirty-six nonlands cost three, so the ideal curve is one-drop, two-drop, then two spells at three. Twenty-four lands at an average mana value of 2.67 gives a 65 percent chance of four lands by turn four.

Sources: 17 black, 14 red, 7 blue, 6 white. Black is the primary, red the secondary, and blue and white are true splashes of three and two cards. Dark Fortress only produces colored mana on the turn it enters or while you control a basic land, so count it as a colorless land when you keep a one-lander.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with lands that enter untapped. Raucous Carnival, Murky Sewer and Neglected Manor are all live early only once someone is at 13 life or less, so plan on Oscorp Industries or a basic first. Turn-one Tidings of War is the best opening: it is a Goblin that blight costs can eat, and it is a flashback card that will be worth three counters later.

Mid game and the combo turn

The combo turn is any turn with two or more blight payments and a death payoff on board. Sequence it like this:

  1. Make tokens first: Boggart Mischief (blight 1 for two Goblins) or cast and then flash back Tidings of War.
  2. Pay each blight cost on a token, not on your engine creatures. Gristle Glutton’s loot, Shadow Urchin’s attack trigger, Pyrrhic Strike and Requiting Hex are all blight outlets.
  3. Let the death triggers resolve. With Arnyn, Mischief and Salvagers all out, one token dying is 4 damage and 4 life; two blight payments in a turn is 8.
  4. Spend the resulting cards: Shadow Urchin exiles one card per counter on the dead creature, and Heirloom Auntie’s surveil decides what reaches the yard for Revenge of the Rats.

Gutsplitter Gang is not optional about blighting. If you have no token to feed it, it costs you 3 life every first main phase, so cast it only when a token is available or when 3 life is a price you are willing to pay for a 6/6.

Win condition 2

Flashback turns one token spell into two boards. Revenge of the Rats counts creature cards in the graveyard at resolution, so cast the hand copy first, blight the Rats away for drain, then flash it back once the yard is deeper. Tidings of War does the same trick with a Goblin Army: amass 1 from hand, amass 3 from the graveyard, and the Army keeps its counters between casts.

The manland is the third angle. Restless Vents attacks as a 2/3 with menace, which is a real clock once the ground is cluttered with tokens, and its loot trigger is a free discard for the flashback plan.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands, at least one black source, and one of Heirloom Auntie, Gristle Glutton or Boggart Mischief. A hand of only three-drops with no turn-one or turn-two play is a mulligan: the deck’s engine needs a body that survives a counter before the drain payoffs matter. One-landers are acceptable only with Oscorp Industries or a basic plus a one-drop.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Counters cancel. A +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter do not coexist; one removes the other. So blighting a Goblin Army that has an amass counter does not kill it. Blight the Army only when you want it to survive and shrink, never when you need a death trigger.
  • Blight is a cost. It cannot be responded to by killing the target, and the creature is chosen as you pay. A 1/1 that takes a -1/-1 counter dies to state-based actions before any trigger resolves, which is why the death payoffs always see it.
  • Shadow Urchin is mandatory. Its attack trigger blights whether you like it or not, so do not attack with it if the only legal blight target is a creature you need.
  • Trigger order matters. Put the drain triggers on the stack first and the value triggers last when you want to resolve surveil before drawing, and remember Heirloom Auntie removes a counter only if it still has one.
  • Flashback exiles. A flashed-back card is gone for good. Cast the hand copy of Revenge of the Rats before the graveyard copy to maximize the token count.
  • Pyrrhic Strike needs the blight. Without paying blight 2 you get one mode; with it you get both, which is usually a two-for-one.

Matchups

  • Aggro. Arnyn is a deathtouch wall, Requiting Hex and Pyrrhic Strike trade up, and the drain race is close but winnable because your removal gains life.
  • Control. Your threats are cheap and recursive, and the drain does not care about blockers. Hold Revenge of the Rats for a turn where a wipe has already happened, and remember a flashed-back spell is immune to discard.
  • Midrange. Gutsplitter Gang trades with almost anything and the manland gets through stalled boards. The matchup is decided by whether Boggart Mischief resolves.
