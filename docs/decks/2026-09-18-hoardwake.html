Blue / Black / Red (three colors) — Hobbit Treasure engine into Dragon payoffs

Overview

Hoardwake is the Treasure build of The Hobbit. The set’s red half is a hoard engine: Dori, Bearer of Friends, The Misty Mountains Cold, Smaug the Magnificent and Smaug, Wicked Worm all make Treasure tokens, and two of them are paid directly in Treasures — Smaug the Magnificent’s attack trigger is “damage equal to the number of Treasures you control”, and The Misty Mountains Cold cashes four Treasures in for a 6/6 flying Dragon token. Treasure is therefore not mana in this deck. It is the counter that the deck’s two biggest payoffs read.

The rest of the list is built to keep that counter climbing without spending cards. Fateful Discovery draws a card off every artifact that enters, and a Treasure token is an artifact, so every Treasure the engine makes is also a card. Long-Bodied Grey Dog is a three-mana flash 2/2 that makes a tapped Treasure as it enters, which turns turn three into a free card off Fateful Discovery and a free artifact for the sacrifice outlets. The Arkenstone is a five-mana artifact that gives the whole team +1/+1 and draws a card at every end step — again a Treasure trigger and a body pump in one card.

The black and blue pieces are the two things the hoard lacks: a sacrifice outlet and a way to spend the mana. The Sackville-Bagginses draw a card and make a Treasure when they eat another creature or artifact, and they drain an opponent for 1 life on every token you sacrifice afterwards. Stone-Giant of High Pass is the other end of that: {2}{R} and an artifact to deal 4 damage to any target. Because a Treasure is an artifact and a Stone Boulder token is an artifact creature, the Giant turns spare hoard pieces into 4-damage burn, and Getaway Barrel makes that trade better — when the Barrel goes to the graveyard it reveals the top thirteen cards and puts a random creature card from among them directly onto the battlefield.

The deck is a loop rather than a two-card combo. Treasures make cards through Fateful Discovery, cards find Smaug and the saga, Smaug counts the Treasures, the saga eats four of them for a Dragon, spare Treasures and Barrels feed the Giant, and the Giant’s sacrifice pays the Sackville-Bagginses’ drain. Every card in the list either adds to the hoard, reads the hoard, or spends it.

Thundering Falls, Undercity Sewers 2 / 2 Blue-red and blue-black duals that enter tapped and surveil 1. The surveil is not incidental: getting a fifth land or a Treasure piece into the graveyard, or keeping one on top, is how the deck finds its fourth land on curve
Mountain, Swamp, Island 4 / 5 / 2 Basics. Red carries the Dragons and the hoard payoffs, black carries the Sackville-Bagginses and the black-red Dragon, blue carries Fateful Discovery and Bilbo

Coloured sources: nine red, eleven blue and ten black across the twenty-four lands. Red is the scarcest source but carries double-red costs, which is why The Lonely Mountain is a full four-of Mountain subtype in the land count rather than a utility land, and why Glóin the Mighty (who adds {R}{R}) is in the deck at all. Twenty-four lands is deliberate: the deck wants four lands on turn four for Smaug and a fifth not long after for Fateful Discovery or the Giant.

The deck lives at three and four. Seventeen of the thirty-six nonlands cost three or less, and the expensive end is deliberately made up of cards that are playable in two halves: Glóin the Mighty adds {R}{R} the turn he lands, Smaug, the Great Calamity carries a 5-damage removal spell on its adventure, Desolation of Smaug adds four mana back when it sweeps, and Stone-Giant of High Pass makes its own sacrifice fodder. Nothing in the list needs more than six mana in one turn, and Treasure tokens pay for the top end.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about getting the hoard started and getting the first non-Treasure artifact on the board. Bilbo, Thief in the Night on two is the strongest opening: he is a 2/2 that can be attacking on turn three and, from then on, every attack lets you recast an artifact or a removal spell out of the graveyard. The Sackville-Bagginses on two is the value alternative — hold them until you have a Treasure or a spare body, because eating one on entry is a card and a Treasure at the same time.

Turn three is Dori, Bearer of Friends or The Misty Mountains Cold. Dori is the faster body (3/2 trample plus a Treasure), the saga is the better long game (a Treasure every chapter and a Dragon at four). Long-Bodied Grey Dog is the third option and the one to play at instant speed when the opponent attacks — a flash 2/2 with reach that makes a Treasure is a clean two-for-one against a flier.

If you have a Fateful Discovery in hand, play it before the next Treasure rather than after: a Treasure made with Discovery on the battlefield is a card, and the sequencing is the difference between a two-card turn and a three-card turn.

Mid game

Smaug the Magnificent on four is the pivot. He has haste, so he attacks the turn he lands, and he is only worth attacking with once the hoard is real — count the Treasures before combat, not after, because the trigger reads the board at the moment it resolves. A typical turn four with two Treasures in play is 4 damage in the air plus 2 damage to any target, and the upkeep Treasure means the number only goes up.

Glóin the Mighty on three is the turn that makes five-drops early: {R}{R} at the beginning of your first main phase each turn pays for Smaug, Wicked Worm or Fateful Discovery a turn ahead of schedule, and his adventure Easy Pickings deals 1 damage to each creature your opponents control, which is the answer to a board of one-toughness tokens.

From the middle of the game the deck is playing with spare artifacts, and that is when the sacrifice outlets become live. Stone-Giant of High Pass makes a 3/1 artifact Wall on entry and on every attack, so it always has food. Sacrificing a Treasure, a spent Getaway Barrel or a Stone Boulder to the Giant is 4 damage, and if The Sackville-Bagginses are in play that same sacrifice is also 1 life off an opponent.

The combo turn

The engine turn is: a Treasure producer, Fateful Discovery or The Arkenstone on the board, and either Smaug the Magnificent or a saga with three counters of lore.

  1. Start the turn with the producers already in play, so the upkeep Treasure from Smaug and the chapter Treasure from The Misty Mountains Cold have both resolved.
  2. Cast Long-Bodied Grey Dog or Dori, Bearer of Friends for another Treasure; Fateful Discovery draws a card for each of those Treasures as they enter.
  3. Attack with Smaug the Magnificent. His trigger deals damage equal to the number of Treasures you control — with four Treasures that is 4 damage to a creature or to the opponent, on top of 4 in the air.
  4. If the hoard has four or more Treasures and a saga is at any chapter, the saga sacrifices itself for a 6/6 red Dragon token with flying. Doing this on the same turn as the Smaug attack is usually the turn the game ends.
  5. Spend the leftover mana on The Lonely Mountain for a 2/2 Dwarf, or on Stone-Giant of High Pass for 4 damage, whichever the board needs.

A realistic turn five: three Treasures plus a Treasure made by Dori, Fateful Discovery drawing twice, Smaug landing for 4 damage plus 4 in the air, and the saga flipping into a 6/6 Dragon on the following upkeep.

Win condition 2

The Dragons do not need Smaug the Magnificent. The Misty Mountains Cold is a four-chapter saga that turns itself into a 6/6 red flying Dragon token, and multiple copies are cumulative: a second copy starts a new Treasure every chapter and can flip as soon as the hoard is at four. Smaug, Wicked Worm is a 5/5 flying Dragon that pays you a card every time you cast a spell with Treasure mana and arrives with tapped Treasures equal to the artifacts your opponents control, which makes it a strong follow-up against artifact decks. Smaug, the Great Calamity is the top end: a 5/5 flying Dragon with five damage of removal stapled to its adventure.

The third, quiet win condition is burn. Stone-Giant of High Pass converts a spare artifact into 4 damage to any target, Getaway Barrel makes that sacrifice a free creature off the top thirteen cards, and the Sackville-Bagginses add 1 life per token sacrificed. Two Giants, a few Stone Boulders and a stocked hoard is 12 to 16 damage that never has to attack.

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
The Misty Mountains Cold counts the Treasure it just made The chapter creates a Treasure token and then checks whether you control four or more Treasures. The token from the same chapter counts, so with three Treasures in play the saga flips into a 6/6 Dragon on that chapter, and it can flip on chapter one if the hoard is already large
The saga is sacrificed whether or not it flips The four-or-more check sacrifices the saga, and only if it sacrifices do you get the Dragon. If the hoard is at four and the saga has already sacrificed itself, a second copy is the way to get a second Dragon
Smaug the Magnificent’s trigger reads the board when it resolves “Damage equal to the number of Treasures you control” is checked at resolution, not at attack declaration, so sacrificing Treasures for Stone-Giant damage before the trigger resolves shrinks it. Order the sacrifices after combat if the damage is going at the opponent
Smaug, Wicked Worm counts the opponent’s artifacts Its entry trigger creates X tapped Treasures where X is the number of artifacts your opponents control, not yours. Against an artifact-light opponent it is a 5/5 flying Dragon for {3}{B}{R} and nothing more, which is still a fine body in a deck with this much reach
The Wicked Worm’s draw needs Treasure mana actually spent The card draw only triggers if mana from a Treasure was spent on the spell. Casting a spell with lands alone is fine, it just does not draw
Desolation of Smaug kills your own non-Dragons It deals 3 damage to each non-Dragon creature, including Dori, the Sackville-Bagginses, the Dog, Bilbo and Glóin. Cast it when your board is Dragons, Treasures and Stone Boulders, or when the opponent’s board is worth more than yours; the four extra mana can only be spent on Dragon spells
Orcrist, Goblin-cleaver chooses one creature type per hit The trigger creates one Treasure for each creature you control of the chosen type. With Dragons in play, choosing Dragon is usually the biggest number; with a token board, choose whatever type is most numerous
The Arkenstone is played as an artifact in this list The front face is a legendary artifact for {5} that gives your creatures +1/+1 and draws a card at every end step. Its adventure, Seek the Heart, costs {2}{W}, and this manabase produces no white, so treat the card as an artifact only
Fateful Discovery draws on every Treasure A Treasure token is an artifact, so it triggers Fateful Discovery. Two Treasure producers in one turn with a Discovery out is three cards, which is how a deck this mana-hungry keeps hitting its land drops
Long-Bodied Grey Dog’s Treasure enters tapped The token comes in tapped, so it cannot pay for a spell the turn it is made, but it still triggers Fateful Discovery and still counts for Smaug the Magnificent the same turn
Bilbo, Thief in the Night recasts artifacts from the graveyard When he attacks you may cast an artifact, instant or sorcery from your graveyard. Getaway Barrel and Desolation of Smaug are the two best targets, and Bilbo’s cost reduction also applies to spells cast this way
Stone-Giant of High Pass eats artifacts only The {2}{R} sacrifice requires an artifact: a Treasure token, a Stone Boulder, a Getaway Barrel or The Arkenstone. It cannot eat a creature, which is why the Sackville-Bagginses matter as the other outlet
The Lonely Mountain enters tapped without an Equipment The land enters untapped only while you control an Equipment, and Orcrist is the only Equipment in the list. On the first three turns it is a tapped Mountain; from the turn Orcrist lands it is a normal Mountain that also makes 2/2 Dwarves with spare mana
Rogue’s Passage has no timing restriction {4}, {T} can be activated in the declare-blockers step, after blockers are chosen. It pays for itself in any colour and is how a 6/6 Dragon token or a charged Smaug gets through a stalled board

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain Race with Smaug the Magnificent and the Dragon tokens, and use Long-Bodied Grey Dog’s flash plus reach to ambush a flier. Desolation of Smaug is a one-sided sweeper for this deck — it kills their board and leaves your Dragons and Treasures untouched. The Sackville-Bagginses’ drain matters in games that stay close
Midrange The strong matchup. The hoard grows without spending cards, Fateful Discovery keeps the hand full, and a 6/6 flying Dragon outclasses most midrange bodies. Save Stone-Giant of High Pass sacrifices for the turn the opponent taps out, and use The Lonely Mountain to turn flood into attackers
Control Play one threat at a time and keep Fateful Discovery and The Arkenstone as the post-sweeper engine. Mistrise Village’s {U}, {T} makes the next spell uncounterable, which is best spent on Smaug, Wicked Worm or Smaug the Magnificent. Bilbo, Thief in the Night rebuying Desolation of Smaug from the graveyard is the deck’s best answer to a planeswalker-heavy control draw
Combo The clock is turn five or six, so Smaug the Magnificent’s attack trigger and Spew Flame are the interaction. Glóin the Mighty’s Easy Pickings sweeps small enabler creatures, Desolation of Smaug clears everything that is not a Dragon, and the Dragons themselves fly over most blockers
