Reality Fracture is Standard-legal but still carries no arena_id in the card data, so the import list below becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there. Several supporting cards in this list (Matoya, Archon Elder; Restless Reef) are already on Arena and playable today.

Overview

This deck is built on the set’s surveil cluster: a small number of repeatable surveil sources feed a payoff suite that all triggers off the words “scry or surveil”. The whole deck is really one engine with several outlets, plus a graveyard that grows every turn as a side effect.

The engine is Chandra, Chill of Compliance and Eye of Jace. Chandra’s first loyalty ability surveils one and, crucially, returns a noncreature, nonland card from that surveil to your hand, so she is card-neutral or better every turn while stocking the graveyard. Eye of Jace surveils on every upkeep for one mana, and once seven cards sit in the graveyard it sacrifices itself for two damage to each opponent. Matoya, Archon Elder converts each of those triggers into a card; Saheeli, Consul of Oversight converts them into Thopters; Diviner of Victory and Prudent Fateseer convert them into power.

The kill is a flying swarm that grows faster than most Standard boards can block. Saheeli is a 4/4 flier that makes a 1/1 Thopter on the first surveil of each turn, and Prudent Fateseer pumps the whole team on the same trigger. Peer Review, the back face of three prepared creatures in the deck, adds 2/2 Cadets while surveilling, which loops back into the same payoffs.

The second act is the graveyard itself. By the time six or seven lands are in play the yard is usually ten or more cards deep, and Return to the Light Realms returns every nonland permanent card from it — creatures, Saheeli, Chandra, Matoya — for one sorcery. That is not a value play, it is usually a lethal rebuild, and it is why the deck can afford to trade bodies early.

Approach

Plan by phases:

  1. Turns 1 and 2: deploy surveil bodies, not payoffs. Diviner of Victory on one, Dark Matter Manipulator or Eye of Jace follows. Diviner is the best one-drop in the deck because it grows on every surveil and its prepared back face, Unwind History, is a bounce spell with surveil attached.
  2. Turns 2 and 3: land the engine. Chandra on three is the priority play; Proft, Consulting Detective and Matoya, Archon Elder convert the first surveil triggers into cards. Proft costs two mana per trigger, so only pay when you have spare mana or need the card.
  3. Turn 4 and later: close with the swarm. Saheeli, Prudent Fateseer and Desperate Futurescribe all want the same thing — a surveil every turn — and once two of them are down, each turn is a Thopter, a Cadet, and two or three points of extra power on the attack.
  4. Long game: Return to the Light Realms. Cast it only when the graveyard holds at least two planeswalkers or four creatures, and ideally after an opponent has spent their sweeper.

Curve and mana: nine one-drops, eight two-drops, eight three-drops, seven four-drops, two five-drops and one nine-drop. Twenty-five lands is deliberate — the deck wants four lands on turn four every game and eight or nine for the reanimation turn, and the surveil engine supplies the card selection that a higher land count normally costs you.

Gameplay strategy

Early game. Lead on Diviner of Victory or Dark Matter Manipulator. Both attack the same resource — the graveyard — from different angles: Diviner grows off surveil triggers, Manipulator mills three and then scales with the yard. Trade freely with Theorix Charm and Your Fate Ends Here; every card you put in the graveyard is fuel.

Mid game. Chandra is the pivot. Her first ability surveils and returns a noncreature card, so she draws a card every turn as long as the surveil finds an instant or sorcery; her second ability pays for Theorix Charm and Rewrite Regrets; her minus ability taps a blocker and puts stun counters on it, which is how the deck survives a resolved large creature. Matoya plus Eye of Jace is a two-card draw engine: upkeep surveil, draw a card, every single turn.

The combo turn. With Saheeli and Prudent Fateseer on the battlefield, sequence the surveil last: cast whatever sets up the trigger (a Peer Review from a prepared creature, or activate Chandra), then attack. Saheeli makes a Thopter on the first surveil of the turn, Prudent Fateseer gives the team +1/+0 on the same trigger, and Desperate Futurescribe converts its combat trigger into a +1/+1 counter because you have surveilled. A typical turn four is two 1/1 Thopters, a 2/2 Cadet, and six or seven points of flying damage.

Win condition 2. Return to the Light Realms. The deck surveils and mills itself constantly, so the graveyard holds the engine by turn six. Nine mana is real, but Chandra pays part of it and the deck plays 25 lands; the usual line is to cast it on the turn after a sweeper, returning Saheeli, Chandra, Matoya and every creature, then attack for lethal with a fresh board and an empty-handed opponent.

Mulligan guide. Keep hands with two or three lands and at least one one-drop plus one engine card (Chandra, Eye of Jace, Proft). Six-card hands are fine if they contain Chandra. Ship hands with no surveil source at all — the deck’s payoffs are blank without one. Do not keep hands that need double white early; the only white cards you want on curve are Enlightened Confidant and Desperate Futurescribe, and both can wait.

Key interactions and edge cases.

  • Diviner of Victory enters prepared, so Unwind History is available immediately. Casting it unprepares Diviner, and the surveil it causes pumps Diviner — the two halves feed each other.
  • Chandra’s surveil-return only works on noncreature, nonland cards. Creature cards stay in the yard for Return to the Light Realms; that is the correct outcome, not a loss.
  • Eye of Jace triggers on upkeep, before you draw. Order it first so you know what you are drawing into before you choose your land drop.
  • Saheeli and Prudent Fateseer both say “triggers only once each turn”. Multiple surveils in one turn do not multiply them; spread the triggers across turns instead of dumping them.
  • Rewrite Regrets returns a creature or planeswalker with mana value six or less — Saheeli, Desperate Futurescribe, Semester Foreseer and Chandra are all valid, and it empowers Jace on the way out.
  • Hall of Echoes copies until end of turn only, and the copy is a land, not a creature. Copy Saheeli for a second 4/4 flier and a second Thopter trigger, or copy a large Dark Matter Manipulator to attack.

Matchups. Against aggro, lean on Enlightened Confidant’s lifelink, Theorix Charm and Chandra’s stun counters, and win with a single big Return to the Light Realms. Against control, do not over-commit: keep one engine piece in hand, force the sweeper, then reanimate. Against midrange, the flying swarm is usually faster than their ground board — attack in the air and use Unwind History to clear the one blocker that matters.
