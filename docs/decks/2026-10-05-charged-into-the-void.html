Overview

Two mechanics, one loop. Station is an activated ability that reads “Tap another creature you control: Put charge counters equal to its power on this Spacecraft or Planet. Station only as a sorcery.” Every Spacecraft has a threshold — at 4+, 6+, or 12+ charge counters it stops being a dormant artifact and becomes a real artifact creature, usually with flying. Void is a condition: “if a nonland permanent left the battlefield this turn or a spell was warped this turn.” Void cards pay you off at end of turn for things leaving the battlefield.

The fusion is direct: the sacrifice engine is what turns Void on, and the creatures that survive the sacrifice are what charge Station. Every permanent you sacrifice — a Robot token, a Treasure, a spent Greedy Freebooter, a creature eaten by Corrupted Conviction — satisfies Void for the whole turn, so every Void trigger on the board fires at end of turn. Meanwhile Wurmwall Sweeper, Lumen-Class Frigate, and Warmaker Gunship sit there collecting charge counters from the creatures you did not sacrifice, and once charged they fly over for the win.

Two sacrifice outlets make the loop repeatable at instant speed, which is the important detail: Bartolomé del Presidio (“Sacrifice another creature or artifact: Put a +1/+1 counter on Bartolomé del Presidio”) and Umbral Collar Zealot (“Sacrifice another creature or artifact: Surveil 1”) can each eat one permanent per turn for free. Susur Secundi, Void Altar — the black Station Planet land — adds a third outlet that converts a creature into cards.

The deck is black-heavy with a red burn/removal package and a white support shell (Bartolomé, Lumen-Class Frigate, and Adagia).

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-3, build the fodder. Deploy cheap permanents: Greedy Freebooter and Deep-Cavern Bat on turn one, then Umbral Collar Zealot, Bartolomé del Presidio, and Wurmwall Sweeper. Umbral Collar Zealot is a 3/2 for two mana — the best rate in the deck and a free outlet. Deep-Cavern Bat strips a card from the opponent’s hand while it is on the battlefield, so it is pure tempo.
  2. Turns 3-5, start the cycle. Sacrifice something every turn (a Robot token, a Treasure, a spent creature) so Void stays online, then station your Spacecraft with whatever power is left over. Corrupted Conviction sacrifices a creature and draws two cards — it is the cheapest way to switch Void on. Plasma Bolt and Tragic Trajectory are Void spells: play them in a turn where a permanent already left and they upgrade to three damage or minus ten.
  3. Turns 5+, convert. Charged Spacecraft attack. Wurmwall Sweeper becomes a 2/2 flier at four counters, Warmaker Gunship a 4/3 flier at six, Lumen-Class Frigate starts giving every other creature +1/+1 at just two counters, and Galvanizing Sawship is a 6/5 with flying and haste at three. Once a board is established, Alpharael’s half-life drain and Adagia’s artifact copy close it out.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a one-drop and a two-drop. Deep-Cavern Bat is the strongest turn-two play because it both attacks and removes an answer from the opponent’s hand. Umbral Collar Zealot is the best blocker — three power for two mana means it also puts three charge counters on a Spacecraft when you station with it. Do not be precious about sacrificing: Greedy Freebooter wants to die, and every death keeps Void switched on.

Mid game

This is where the deck separates from a normal aggro deck. Each turn you want to do two things: leave at least one permanent on the battlefield and then remove or sacrifice a different one. The default line is to station during your precombat main phase (Station is sorcery-speed), then attack, then sacrifice a token or a spent creature in the second main phase so Void is satisfied before the end step.

Key sequencing rule: do your Void-dependent spells in the second main phase, not the first. Plasma Bolt and Tragic Trajectory upgrade only if a nonland permanent has already left the battlefield this turn, so cast your sacrifice effect first and the burn second.

The combo turn

  1. Untap with one or more dormant Spacecraft and at least two creatures, one of which you do not need.
  2. Precombat main: choose a Spacecraft and station it repeatedly — each activation taps another creature and adds charge counters equal to its power. Tapping multiple creatures in one turn is legal, because Station has no once-per-turn restriction, only a sorcery-timing restriction.
  3. Check thresholds: 4+ for Wurmwall Sweeper, 6+ for Warmaker Gunship, 3+ for Galvanizing Sawship, 12+ for Lumen-Class Frigate. The moment a Spacecraft hits its threshold it is an artifact creature and can attack.
  4. Attack with the newly created fliers. Sweeper and Gunship fly, Galvanizing Sawship has haste.
  5. Second main: sacrifice a leftover permanent — a Robot token from last turn’s Elegy Acolyte, a Treasure, or a creature to Corrupted Conviction — to keep Void online, and hold Tragic Trajectory or Plasma Bolt to punish any crack-back.

Win condition 2: Void attrition

The Robot tokens are the second clock. Elegy Acolyte makes a 2/2 colorless Robot artifact creature token at every end step in which a permanent left the battlefield — and it draws a card whenever your creatures deal combat damage to a player, so it refills your hand while it builds a board. Those Robots then do double duty: they attack, they block, they charge Station (two power each), and they are free sacrifice fodder for Bartolomé or Corrupted Conviction.

Alpharael, Stonechosen is the finisher: when it attacks with Void online, the defending player loses half their life, rounded up. Two attacks — or one attack after burn — ends a game that was never close. Plasma Bolt and Tragic Trajectory also count as reach, and Susur Secundi converts a large creature into cards.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: two or three lands that cover black plus either white or red, one one-drop, one two-drop, and one sacrifice outlet or Corrupted Conviction.
  • Keep a one-lander only if it is a Swamp and the hand has Greedy Freebooter plus Umbral Collar Zealot — the curve is low enough to recover.
  • Ship: hands with no black source, hands with only five- and six-drops, and hands with three or more lands that enter tapped (Adagia and Susur Secundi both enter tapped, so two of them plus no basic is a real cost).
  • The deck is more resilient than most midrange decks because sacrificing is a feature, not a cost — a hand of interchangeable small creatures plus one outlet is playable.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Station is sorcery-speed only. You cannot charge a Spacecraft in response to removal or during combat. Do it in your main phase before you attack.
  • Station has no once-per-turn limit. Multiple creatures can be tapped in the same turn to charge one Spacecraft; a single Lumen-Class Frigate tap adds three counters, an Umbral Collar Zealot tap adds three more, and a Warmaker Gunship tap adds four.
  • Charge counters do not add up across cards. Each Spacecraft tracks its own counters; Adagia and Susur Secundi are Planets with their own 12+ abilities, they are not Spacecraft and never become creatures.
  • Void checks the whole turn, not the end step. A permanent that left the battlefield during combat still satisfies Void at the end step, so trading creatures in combat is a legitimate way to switch Void on.
  • Void counts any player’s permanents. When removal or an opponent’s own sacrifice effect makes them lose a permanent, that also turns your Void triggers on — your end-step payoffs only care that something left the battlefield, not whose it was.
  • Lumen-Class Frigate’s anthem is live at two counters, not twelve. The twelve-counter mode (flying lifelink) is a bonus; the +1/+1 pump is the reason to play it, and it also makes your stationing creatures hit harder next turn.
  • Sacrificing to Corrupted Conviction is an additional cost. If the spell is countered the creature is still gone, so treat it as a sacrifice outlet with a draw-two rider rather than a protection spell.
  • Adagia copies artifacts you control, including charged Spacecraft. The copy is legendary, so copying a legendary Spacecraft means you keep only one; copy a non-legendary charged Warmaker Gunship or Galvanizing Sawship instead.

Matchups

  • Aggro: the deck has cheap blockers (Umbral Collar Zealot, Deep-Cavern Bat), lifelink, and a very low curve. Trade early, keep Void switched on so Elegy Acolyte keeps producing Robots, and win the ground fight with Bartolomé growing into a real threat.
  • Control: play into Void deliberately. Sacrifice tokens to force the payoffs, keep a Wurmwall Sweeper or Galvanizing Sawship back as a threat that only needs three or four counters, and hold Alpharael for a turn where the opponent has tapped out. The deck’s threats are artifacts, so artifact removal is the main concern.
  • Midrange: the mirror-ish grind — Interceptor Mechan returns your best artifact or creature from the graveyard, so the deck recovers from sweepers better than it looks. Susur Secundi is the long-game engine: sacrifice a big creature to draw four or five cards and refill.
  • Combo: no counterspells, so the plan is speed and disruption. Deep-Cavern Bat’s exile is the best tool; Tragic Trajectory with Void online kills a combo creature through most protection.
