Overview

Fang and Fur is the Beornings deck of The Hobbit set: a bear-and-warg engine that turns lands and creature spells into a wall of counters and tokens. The engine is double-barreled — Dancing from Dark to Dawn puts X +1/+1 counters on a creature for every creature spell you cast (where X is that spell’s mana value) and makes a 2/2 Bear on every landfall; Beorn’s Hospitality puts a counter on a creature on every landfall and can itself awaken into a Bear whose size equals your land count. The payoff is Beorn the Fierce: other Bears get +2/+2, and at the start of combat he grants trample and Bear status to any creature, drawing two cards once you control three or more Bears.

The wolf half of the pack grinds and ramps: Bejeweled Warg makes Treasures, Chief Warg’s Company makes a free Wolf every upkeep, The Chief Warg draws on every attack with a 4-power creature, and Head of the Hunt exiles anything that dies and refunds a Wolf token. Radagast of Rhosgobel makes the first creature spell each turn cost {2} less and gives it flash, so the counter engine fires at instant speed. Part in Friendship recurs a creature whenever one dies, and Tom, Bert, and William trade bodies for cards.

The lands are not just mana — they are part of the kill. Mirkwood sacrifices itself to put two +1/+1 counters on any Bear, Spider, or Wolf; Restless Vinestalk becomes a 5/5 trample attacker; Restless Cottage a 4/4 that makes Food; Rogue’s Passage opens the road for the biggest bear. The set is scheduled for Arena on 2026-08-14 (arena_id is still empty in the database, so the import list works from that date).

Curve and mana

24 lands: 3 Mirkwood, 2 Restless Vinestalk, 1 Restless Cottage, 1 Rogue’s Passage, 1 Cavern of Souls, 1 Deathcap Glade, 8 Forest, 4 Swamp, 3 Island. Green sources 15, black 9, blue 5 — green is the workhorse, blue a light splash for the landfall control and draw.

Nonland curve: 2-drop x11, 3-drop x9, 4-drop x4, 5-drop x10, 7-drop x2. Average CMC 3.58. The deck wants a two-drop Warg or enchantment on turn 2, a landfall engine plus Little Bear on turn 3, Radagast or The Chief Warg on turn 4, and Beorn the Fierce on turn 5 with the pack already growing.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turn 1 is a tapped Mirkwood or Cottage. Turn 2: Bejeweled Warg, Beorn’s Hospitality, or a Riddles in the Dark. Sequence tapped lands first — Mirkwood, Cottage, Vinestalk, and Deathcap Glade (before turn 2) enter tapped, so keep basics and Cavern of Souls for the turns you need untapped green. Every land drop from here on is a spell: it triggers Hospitality’s counter and Dancing’s Bear token.

Mid game

Resolve Radagast of Rhosgobel and start chaining creature spells — each cast puts counters on a bear (Dancing from Dark to Dawn), and Radagast’s flash lets you do it on the opponent’s turn. Chief Warg’s Company builds free Wolf tokens for sacrifice fodder and Bejeweled Warg’s Treasures accelerate into the 5-drops. Head of the Hunt discourages attacks: every blocker that dies becomes your Wolf.

The combo turn

  1. Stack the pack: three or more Bears on board (Dancing’s landfall tokens, Little Bear, Large Bear) and Dancing or Hospitality online.
  2. Cast Beorn the Fierce — other Bears get +2/+2 and the combat trigger draws two cards.
  3. Attack with the swarm: Wargling or Beorn’s trample counters push damage through; The Chief Warg draws for every 4-power attacker.
  4. Finish with Gigantic Big Bear: a 10/7 hexproof haste that can’t be countered, or Large Bear’s haste 5/5, both doubled by Beorn’s anthem.

A typical kill: two 2/2 Bear tokens and a Little Bear under Beorn become 6/6, 4/4, and 4/4 with trample; Gigantic Big Bear comes down as a 12/9. Mirkwood activations put +2/+2 on whichever bear needs to get through.

Win condition 2

The lands finish the game. Restless Vinestalk animates as a 5/5 trample and its attack trigger resets blockers; Restless Cottage is a 4/4 that makes Food; Mirkwood pumps any Bear, Spider, or Wolf with two counters per activation (three activations turn a 2/2 Bear token into an 8/8). Rogue’s Passage spends {4} to make the biggest attacker unblockable — against a stalled board, the 12/9 Gigantic Big Bear or a Mirkwood-fed Wolf closes. Part in Friendship keeps the pack coming back, and Supper for Spiders converts the opponent’s dead creatures into Food.

Mulligan guide

Keep: two to three lands with a two-drop Warg or enchantment — Bejeweled Warg, Chief Warg’s Company, or Beorn’s Hospitality openers are strong; any hand with Beorn the Fierce, Dancing from Dark to Dawn, or Radagast plus a creature is excellent. Mulligan one-land hands and hands with five or more lands and no engine. Blue is a splash: a hand with only green and black is keepable, but a hand that cannot cast a two-drop by turn 2 is not.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Beorn the Fierce’s combat trigger targets any creature, not just Bears: a Bejeweled Warg or The Chief Warg gains trample and Bear status, joining the +2/+2 anthem.
  • Dancing from Dark to Dawn counts the spell’s mana value, not the mana paid — Radagast’s {2} discount still dumps full-value counters.
  • Beorn’s Hospitality’s awakening is permanent: once activated ({5}{G}{G}) it stays a Bear and grows with your land count, and Beorn the Fierce’s anthem applies to it.
  • Mirkwood’s pump targets Bears, Spiders, and Wolves — it cannot target the Army token or Beorn’s Hospitality before it awakens, but it can pump the Bear tokens from Dancing.
  • Part in Friendship triggers only once per turn and puts the creature into play only if its mana value fits your lands — sequence your land drops before the sacrifice.
  • Tom, Bert, and William return as an artifact when they die, so Part in Friendship will not recur them; they are best used as a repeatable draw engine while alive.
  • Head of the Hunt exiles dying creatures instead of letting them die — it stops death triggers like Supper for Spiders targets and fuels its own Wolf tokens.
