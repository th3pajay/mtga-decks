Naya prepared-artifact combo — Reality Fracture

Overview

Every creature in the engine is a spell in disguise. The Reality Fracture prepared creatures — Heartwood Crafter, Konstrari Improviser, Woodwork Prodigy and Blossom-Blessed Angel — enter the battlefield prepared, which means the sorcery printed on their back face can be cast once for free. This deck’s back face is Soul Tether, a {2}{R/G} sorcery that makes a Heartwood token: a red and green artifact with {T}: Add {R} or {G}.

That single loop supplies everything the deck wants. Heartwood tokens are mana, so the deck reaches five, six and seven mana without playing a ramp spell. They are also artifacts, which is the number Craterclaw Colossus reads, and the number Aerid Konstrari reads. Twelve creatures produce tokens, so the artifact count climbs on its own: Woodwork Prodigy re-prepares during your upkeep, which means one free Heartwood every turn from a single three-drop.

The white half is small and load-bearing. Blossom-Blessed Angel is the second prepared body — a 2/4 flier with vigilance that hands out a free +1/+1 counter and a life — and the equipment in the list is both a real pump and artifact padding for the two payoff creatures.

Nothing here is a fragile two-card combo: every piece advances the artifact count itself, so answering one creature slows the engine rather than stopping it.

Curve and mana

Mana value 1 2 3 4 7
Cards 9 12 4 8 3

Twenty-four lands, nine one-drops and twelve two-drops: the deck almost always has a play on turn one and two, which is what keeps the goldfish consistency at 0.638 and P(four lands by turn four) at 0.623. Craterclaw Colossus is listed at seven, but Heartwood tokens mean it usually lands on turn five. The functional average is under three.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a prepared body, not with equipment. Heartwood Crafter on one is the ideal curve: it enters prepared, so turn one is a 1/1 plus a Heartwood token, and from then on every Soul Tether copy is a free mana rock. Its own {T}: Add {C} cannot pay for spells cast from your hand, so treat that ability as a bonus rather than a plan.

Konstrari Improviser on two is the stronger clock — a 2/2 plus a token. Hold Woodwork Prodigy until you can protect it; it is the only repeatable token source and opponents will point removal at it. Do not trade Heartwood tokens for damage early: they are the mana that casts Craterclaw, and they are the number Craterclaw and Aerid read.

The combo turn

The kill is a mass pump, not an infinite loop:

  1. Untap with five or more artifacts on the battlefield — in practice three Heartwoods plus an equipment and a body.
  2. Cast Craterclaw Colossus for {4}{R}{R}{R}. Its trigger counts artifacts you control as it resolves, itself included.
  3. The whole team gains trample and gets +X/+0. With six artifacts that is a 5/5 haste trampler plus every other creature swinging for six more than printed.
  4. Konstrari Charm’s second mode adds two counters and trample to whichever creature is being chump-blocked.

Against a board stall, add Hall of Echoes: for {5} the land becomes a second Craterclaw, which fires the pump trigger again and stacks a second trample bonus on the same attack.

Win condition 2 — Aerid Konstrari and the manland

Aerid Konstrari is a 4/4 flier for four that makes a Heartwood when it enters and another when it dies, and its {6} ability converts the artifact count into power. With six artifacts it attacks as a 10/4 flier, which races any ground deck. Hall of Echoes copying Aerid is the cleanest finisher in the deck: the copy is a land until end of turn, so sorcery-speed creature removal does not answer it.

Restless Ridgeline is the third route. Three mana turns it into a 3/4, and each attack gives another attacker +2/+0 and untaps it — a loop of small advantages that closes games where the big creatures were answered.

Five lands, one payoff, no engine No The payoffs read a count that nothing is building

Woodwork Prodigy, three lands, Konstrari Charm Yes Prodigy alone produces a token every turn from turn four

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Prepared is one-shot per preparation. Casting the Soul Tether copy unprepares the creature. Only Woodwork Prodigy re-arms itself, and only during your upkeep, so a Prodigy killed at sorcery speed costs you the future tokens, not just the body.
  • Craterclaw counts artifacts as it resolves. Sacrificing a Heartwood to Marwyn in response lowers X, so make the draw decision before combat, not during it.
  • Aerid Konstrari’s pump is a one-shot. The +X/+0 lasts until end of turn; the Heartwood it makes does not.
  • Traxos, Scourge Eternal does not untap on its own. It untaps when you cast an artifact or creature spell, so a turn with only Konstrari Charm leaves it tapped on defence.
  • Solitary Cell is an artifact first. It exiles a permanent with mana value three or less until it leaves, so destroying your own Cell returns their permanent — and it counts for Craterclaw while it sits there.
  • Restless Ridgeline enters tapped. Do not count it as a turn-one red source; lead on Stomping Ground when you need red immediately.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Trade early with Konstrari Charm and Solitary Cell, then land Blossom-Blessed Angel as a blocker that also grows. The deck wants the game to reach turn five
Control Hold Woodwork Prodigy until they tap out, and keep a second prepared body in hand. Hall of Echoes and Restless Ridgeline dodge sorcery-speed sweepers
Midrange Race with Aerid plus tokens; their removal is one-for-one against a deck that rebuilds the count every turn
Combo No countermagic, so the plan is speed: tokens on turns one and two, Craterclaw on five, and Konstrari Charm’s six damage for a key flier
