Overview

Today’s roll came up three mechanics deep and five colors wide: the +1/+1 counter theme, Surveil, and Waterbending, in White, Blue, Black, Red and Green. That combination reads strange until you notice what waterbend actually does. The official ruling is one sentence: “Pay the cost. For each generic mana in that cost, you may tap an untapped artifact or creature you control rather than pay that mana.” Board presence is literally mana. Counters are how this deck grows board presence, surveil is how it finds the right cards and where it puts the mana base to work, and every waterbend ability in the list is therefore free once the board is wide enough.

The thesis is: go wide with cheap bodies and artifacts, grow them with counters, and spend them as currency to cast effects nobody can pay for in a five-color deck. Water Tribe Rallier at two mana digs for a creature for waterbend five. Katara, Bending Prodigy draws a card for waterbend six and rewards being tapped with a +1/+1 counter every end step. Flexible Waterbender becomes a 5/2 or stays a 2/5 blocker for waterbend three. Foggy Swamp Vinebender adds a counter for waterbend five. Waterbender Ascension grants unblockable for waterbend four. Katara, Water Tribe’s Hope sets the entire team’s base power and toughness for waterbend X.

Surveil is the second layer and it lives in the mana base. Nine of the twenty-four lands are two-color surveil lands, so the fixing and the mechanic are the same cards: every tapland you play smooths the next draw and, with Matoya, Archon Elder in play, replaces itself. Starfighter Pilot surveils whenever it becomes tapped, which is exactly what paying a waterbend cost does to it, so the deck’s waterbend currency is also its card selection.

The counters layer is the payoff density. Terrasymbiosis draws cards for counters. Earth King’s Lieutenant puts a counter on every other Ally each time an Ally arrives, and most of the waterbend creatures are Allies. United Front makes a token per mana and counters on everything. Loading Zone doubles the counters. Araña, Heart of the Spider adds a counter on every attack and cashes modified creatures into extra cards. The deck wins by attacking with a wide, growing team that can be made lethal by a land.

Curve and mana

24 lands: four University Campus, two each Forum of Amity, Suburban Sanctuary, Paradox Gardens, Titan’s Grave and Sinister Hideout, one each Savage Mansion, Spectacle Summit, Ominous Asylum, Frontier Bivouac, Oscorp Industries and Secluded Starforge, plus two Plains, one Island and one Forest. Color sources: 10 white, 11 blue, 8 black, 5 red, 9 green, with Secluded Starforge as a colorless land that pays for anything. Red is the splash, which is why it carries only four cards (two Araña, two Rocketeer Boostbuggy) and still has five sources.

Nonland curve: 19 two-drops, 11 three-drops, 5 four-drops, 1 five-drop, average nonland CMC 2.67. The deck never wants for turn two plays, and the two-drops are interchangeable by role: a waterbend outlet, a surveil body, or an Ally that grows the Lieutenants.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two should establish two of the three mechanics at once. Starfighter Pilot is the best opener against removal-light decks: a 2/2 that surveils every time it pays a waterbend cost, and with Matoya, Archon Elder that becomes a repeatable draw. Water Tribe Rallier is the waterbend outlet you can cast on turn two and then never pay for again: five untapped creatures and artifacts cover waterbend five entirely, so look at four cards and take the creature for free. Earth King’s Lieutenant is the other turn two play and the reason to run Ally bodies: it hands a counter to every other Ally, and every subsequent Ally that enters hands one back to it.

Rocketeer Boostbuggy is the quiet setup card. It is a two-mana artifact, so it is waterbend fuel from turn two, it makes a Treasure on every attack, and Treasures are more waterbend fuel. When the board is ready, its exhaust turns it into a 3/2 artifact creature with a +1/+1 counter for three mana, and it keeps making Treasures afterwards.

Do not tap out on turn two for the sake of it. Waterbend costs are paid by tapping, so the useful turns are the ones where you can hold up creatures and still spend them on an ability in the same turn.

Mid game

Turns three through five are about stacking counters while the board stays wide enough to pay for everything.

Katara, Bending Prodigy is the counter clock. She taps for any waterbend cost, including her own waterbend six draw, and the end step checks whether she is tapped, so the deck is rewarded for spending her as currency every single turn. Terrasymbiosis converts the first counter event of each turn into cards: Earth King’s Lieutenant handing out counters, Araña’s attack trigger, United Front, or the counter Katara puts on herself all draw.

Waterbender Ascension is the engine that scales fastest. Any creature connecting adds a quest counter, and at four or more it draws a card on every subsequent trigger, so a wide board of small attackers turns it into a steady stream of cards. Its second ability is the deck’s removal-proof answer: waterbend four makes a creature unblockable, and if the board is wide enough, four tapped creatures is no cost at all.

Surveil keeps the draws clean. Twilight Diviner surveils two on entry, Broodspinner surveils two and later sacrifices into a swarm of flying Insects equal to the card types in your graveyard, and every surveil land is a free look. Matoya, Archon Elder turns each of them into a card, so playing lands off the top of the library is how this deck keeps hitting its fourth and fifth land without flooding.

The combo turn

  1. Count your currency. Untapped artifacts and creatures, no matter how small, how recently played or how badly they attack, each pay one generic mana of a waterbend cost.
  2. Choose the payoff for the board you have. A wide board of small creatures plus United Front is the swarm line: X tokens enter, every creature gets a counter, and the tokens are more waterbend fuel next turn.
  3. Or set the team. Katara, Water Tribe’s Hope’s waterbend X makes every creature you control base X/X until end of turn, and X cannot be zero. Pay X with Treasures from Rocketeer Boostbuggy, the Invasion Submersible, Robot tokens from Secluded Starforge, or creatures you are not attacking with, then attack with everything that is still untapped.
  4. Finish with the land. Secluded Starforge, two mana and X untapped artifacts gives one creature plus X/plus 0. With Waterbender Ascension’s unblockable on the same creature, that is lethal without blockers ever mattering.

Win condition 2

The second win does not need Katara at all. Waterbender Ascension plus one grown body is a self-contained kill: Foggy Swamp Vinebender starts as a 4/3 that ignores small blockers and adds a counter every turn for waterbend five, Flexible Waterbender becomes a 5/2 for waterbend three, and Araña, Heart of the Spider adds a counter to an attacker every combat while turning modified creatures’ damage into extra cards. Make the biggest one unblockable for waterbend four, pump it with Secluded Starforge, and the deck’s wide cheap board is the payment. Broodspinner’s sacrifice is the grindy backup: flying Insect tokens equal to the card types in your graveyard is a real clock in a deck that surveils this much.

Mulligan guide

Keep two to three lands and at least one two-drop that touches two mechanics: Starfighter Pilot or Water Tribe Rallier first, Rocketeer Boostbuggy or Earth King’s Lieutenant second. The best hands are two lands, a two-drop and a three-drop with blue and white or blue and green among the lands, because Katara, Bending Prodigy and Matoya, Archon Elder are the two engines worth curving into. Ship one-land hands and hands with five or more lands and no engine. A hand of only surveil lands is slow but keepable if it has a two-drop and a three-drop, since every one of them fixes two colors.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Waterbend is not a tap ability. “For each generic mana in that cost, you may tap an untapped artifact or creature you control rather than pay that mana,” and the ruling is explicit that you may tap a creature you have not controlled since the start of your turn. Summoning-sick creatures, spare Vehicles, Treasures and Robot tokens all pay, so board width matters more than size.
  • If an artifact or creature has a mana ability with tap in the cost, activating that ability while paying a waterbend cost taps it first, and you cannot tap it again for waterbend. Do not plan a land-fetch or mana creature as your waterbend payment in the same window.
  • Katara, Water Tribe’s Hope sets base power and toughness, which overwrites counters and pumps for the turn rather than adding to them, and it can only be activated during your turn with X of at least one. Pay it with artifacts and creatures you are not attacking with, because paying for it taps the payment.
  • Waterbender Ascension draws on every trigger once it has four or more quest counters, not just the fourth one, so a wide attack with several connecting creatures can draw multiple cards in a single combat.
  • Crashing Wave’s X is the amount of generic mana you actually waterbend, and you tap up to X target creatures before distributing three stun counters among any tapped creatures your opponents control, including creatures that were already tapped. If every target becomes illegal before it resolves, the spell does not resolve and no stun counters are distributed.
  • Loading Zone doubles counters for every creature you control. With Terrasymbiosis, the doubled number is what the draw effect sees on that turn’s first counter event, so a single counter from Earth King’s Lieutenant becomes two counters and two cards.
  • Terrasymbiosis triggers only once each turn, on the first event that puts counters on a creature. United Front’s mass counter placement counts as several events but only the first one draws.
  • Earth King’s Lieutenant counts Allies: Water Tribe Rallier, Katara, Bending Prodigy, Flexible Waterbender, Foggy Swamp Vinebender, Katara, Water Tribe’s Hope, the Ally token she makes and every Ally token from United Front all trigger it.
  • Broodspinner’s Insects count card types among cards in your graveyard, and surveil lands put cards there for free, so the swarm is usually three to five tokens by the time it is sacrificed.
  • Twilight Diviner’s copy clause needs a creature that entered or was cast from a graveyard, which this build does not produce. Treat it as a 3/3 with surveil two and ignore the rest of the text.
  • Secluded Starforge’s pump is sorcery speed only and it taps the artifacts as part of the cost, so use it before combat and use artifacts, not creatures you want to attack with.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro Flexible Waterbender blocks as a 2/5, Earth King’s Lieutenant and the token makers trade up, and Crashing Wave taps their attackers and stuns two or three of them for a full turn cycle
Midrange Go over the top with counters: Terrasymbiosis and Matoya keep your hand full, Loading Zone doubles every counter, and an unblockable grown creature plus Secluded Starforge closes faster than they can block
Control Do not overcommit into sweepers; keep waterbend fuel in artifacts and Treasures rather than only creatures, and hold Waterbender Ascension for the turn you can use waterbend four on the same turn you attack
Combo Crashing Wave buys the turn you need, Starfighter Pilot and the surveil lands dig toward Crashing Wave and United Front, and the swarm from United Front or Broodspinner ends games quickly
