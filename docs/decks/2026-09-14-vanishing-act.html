Archetype Tempo combo — evasive Ninja pressure plus a Clue and Disappear value engine
Size 60 cards: 28 creatures, 8 non-creature spells, 24 lands

Secondary win Ten poison counters from Persuasive Interrogators, earned by sacrificing Clue tokens

Overview

Vanishing Act is built on three Standard mechanics that feed each other: Investigate, Sneak and Disappear.

Investigate makes Clue tokens, and every Clue is an artifact that can be cashed in with two mana to draw a card. Sneak lets you cast a spell for its reduced sneak cost if you also return an unblocked attacker to your hand during the declare blockers step — the card comes back to hand, the Sneak creature enters tapped and attacking, and you effectively got a free extra attacker. Disappear text reads “if a permanent left the battlefield under your control this turn”, so it is switched on by exactly the two things the deck does constantly: sacrificing Clues and bouncing attackers back to hand.

The result is a deck that never takes a turn off. Clues convert mana into cards, bounced attackers convert into discounted reinforcement, and every sacrifice turns on the end-step Disappear payoffs (Michelangelo, Game Master growing; Lord Dregg, Insect Invader making fliers; Putrid Pals arriving with counters). The damage plan is evasion-first: Turtle Lair and Rogue’s Passage both hand out “can’t be blocked”, which is a damage engine and a Sneak enabler at the same time.

Four colors would normally be a liability, so the mana base is deliberately built out of colorless fixing rather than coloured duals: eight lands that fetch basics, plus utility lands that happen to be combo pieces.

Approach

Game plan phases

Turns 1 to 2 — set the table. Land, then either Deduce or Toxin Analysis. Both Investigate, so you trade a card for a card and leave a Clue behind. A first-turn Scene of the Crime is also fine: it enters tapped but it is a Clue that later cashes in for a card, a poison counter pair, and a Disappear trigger all at once.

Turns 3 to 4 — establish pressure. Turncoat Kunoichi, Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising and Michelangelo, Game Master are the three-mana core. Turncoat Kunoichi removes a blocker while it attacks; Oroku Saki refills your hand every time it connects; Michelangelo banks a +1/+1 counter at every end step in which something left your battlefield, which is most of them.

Turns 4 to 6 — start the Sneak loop. Every attack step is now a decision: if a Ninja goes unblocked, return it to hand during declare blockers and pay a sneak cost. That is a rebate, a Disappear trigger and often lethal damage in the same moment. Lord Dregg and Putrid Pals convert that online Disappear into tokens and counters once the turn ends.

Turns 6 and later — close. Foot Ninjas and Shredder, Unrelenting are the big finishers, and Persuasive Interrogators gives the deck a way to win that ignores the opponent’s creatures entirely.

The deck’s average non-land mana value is about 3.5, weighted to three drops so that the Sneak rebate matters. Black is the deepest colour (Elektra, Shark Shredder and Persuasive Interrogators each want two black), so lead on Swamp or a fetch land, and treat the first Forest or Plains as a splash you fetch when it is needed.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Spend the first two turns on cards that do not cost you anything: Deduce and Toxin Analysis both draw a card and leave a Clue, so the deck starts ahead on material. Do not attack into a bad block just to look busy. Against faster decks, hold Toxin Analysis to give a blocker deathtouch and lifelink; a Putrid Pals or Foot Mystic with deathtouch and lifelink blanks most ground assaults.

Mid game and the combo turn

The pattern that wins is always the same, and it happens inside one combat step:

  1. Before combat, cast your draw spells and leave Clue tokens on the battlefield.
  2. Attack with everything that can get through. Turtle Lair or Rogue’s Passage guarantees at least one unblocked attacker.
  3. During declare blockers, return an unblocked attacker to your hand to pay a sneak cost. Cast Elektra for one and two black to kill a blocker and add a tapped, attacking 3/3; cast Oroku Saki for one and one black to keep the pressure and draw; cast Michelangelo, Improviser for two and two green to add a 4/4 that rewards connecting.
  4. After combat, sacrifice each Clue you do not need (two mana each). Every sacrifice draws a card, turns Disappear on and, if a Persuasive Interrogators is on the battlefield, hands an opponent two poison counters.
  5. At the end step, collect: Michelangelo, Game Master gets a counter, Lord Dregg makes a 1/1 flying Insect Warrior, and Putrid Pals arrives with two +1/+1 counters on any turn where something already left your battlefield.

Note that Turncoat Kunoichi exiles a creature only until it leaves the battlefield, so bouncing Turncoat to hand to pay a sneak cost returns that creature to its owner — pay the sneak cost instead when you want the exile to be permanent.

Win condition two: poison

Persuasive Interrogators turns every sacrificed Clue into two poison counters, and a player with ten or more poison counters loses regardless of life total. Clues come from Deduce, Toxin Analysis, the Interrogators’ own enters-the-battlefield trigger and Scene of the Crime, so five Clue sacrifices finish the game. This is the route to take when the opponent clogs the ground with bigger creatures: keep two mana open, crack a Clue every turn, and let the poison accumulate while your Ninjas hold the board.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands, at least one black source, and a play on turns two and three. A hand of six or seven lands is a mulligan. Since the mana base fetches basics, a one-land hand with an Evolving Wilds and a two-drop is playable; a one-land hand with only utility lands is not. Do not keep a hand that has no creature at all — this deck wins by attacking.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Sleight of hand and Sneak: you pay a sneak cost only “if you also return an unblocked attacker you control to hand during the declare blockers step”. The attacker must actually be unblocked, so unblockable effects (Turtle Lair, Rogue’s Passage, Escape-style effects) are what make the mechanic reliable.
  • The bounced creature is not lost: casting it again later re-triggers its enters-the-battlefield ability, so returning Turncoat Kunoichi or Oroku Saki is rarely a real cost.
  • Shark Shredder, Killer Clone has first strike and steals a creature card from the defending player’s graveyard whenever it connects, and that creature enters tapped and attacking that player. Trade early, then take their best dead creature.
  • Shredder, Unrelenting gives another creature deathtouch when it enters and whenever it attacks. Pair it with first strike damage (Shark Shredder) or trample from a pumped Michelangelo, Game Master to make blocks hopeless.
  • Michelangelo, Improviser puts a creature card and a land card from hand onto the battlefield when it connects — free acceleration, and the land entering is not a sacrifice, so Disappear still needs a Clue or a bounce.
  • Fetch lands are Disappear triggers: cracking an Evolving Wilds in your second main phase switches Disappear on for the end step even if you never sacrificed a Clue that turn.
  • Clue sacrifices cost two mana each. Sequence them into spare mana rather than tapping out for a big creature and leaving three Clues unused.

Matchups

Against aggro. You have more interaction than it looks: Elektra kills a small attacker on entry, Turncoat Kunoichi exiles one, Shredder’s Technique kills anything, and Toxin Analysis turns a blocker into a deathtouch lifelink wall. Foot Ninjas gains three life. Stabilise, then race with Lord Dregg’s fliers.

Against control. Sneak costs make your threats cheap enough to double-spell, Oroku Saki and Deduce keep your hand full, and the Disappear tokens rebuild a board after a sweeper. Persuasive Interrogators is the trump: poison ignores their life total.

Against midrange. Shark Shredder and Shredder, Unrelenting outclass most four- and five-drop blockers, and their graveyards become your resources. Use Rogue’s Passage to make the biggest deathtouch threat unblockable rather than fighting on the ground.
