Overview

Every connive is a loot: draw a card, then discard a card, and if the discarded card was not a land the conniving creature keeps a +1/+1 counter. That is a fair card on its own. In this deck it is a graveyard engine, because the discard is chosen: every connive puts a permanent card straight into the bin, and the deck is paid by the number of permanent cards sitting there.

Souls of the Lost is the payoff. For {1}{B} plus a discarded card it enters as a Spirit whose power is exactly the number of permanent cards in your graveyard and whose toughness is that number plus one. Four connives and a Squirming Emergence later it is a 7/8 for two mana. Squirming Emergence and Raise the Past then turn the stocked yard into a second and third wave, and Rogue's Passage removes the only real answer to a huge ground creature: a blocker.

The second engine is tribal. Leader, Super-Genius upgrades every connive into “draw two, discard one”, so each Villain that connives replaces itself and then some, while Baron Strucker, HYDRA Overlord makes the Villain half of the deck cheaper and hands out a free connive every turn. With five or six Villains on the table, Mysterio, Master of Illusion arrives with a 3/3 Illusion Villain for each of them.

The deck deliberately plays no one-drops. It opens on two, loots on two, and spends turns three and four converting a full graveyard into bodies. The trade-off is honest: the first turn is a land, and the deck wins on card quality and recursion rather than speed.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Stock the yard (turns 1–3). Land, then two-drop. Mob Lookout and Red Room Recruit both connive on entry; Unstable Experiment connives at instant speed for {1}{U}. Discard permanent cards, never lands, unless you are short on lands. Every nonland discarded is a point of power on every future Souls.
  2. Convert (turns 3–5). Leader, Super-Genius doubles each connive. Baron Strucker, HYDRA Overlord discounts the Villain team and connives a fresh Villain each turn. Cast Souls of the Lost once the yard holds four or more permanents, then keep conniving to grow it — the power is a static count, not a trigger.
  3. Close. Attack with Souls through Rogue's Passage, or cast Raise the Past to return every two-drop in the yard (Mob Lookout, Red Room Recruit, Souls of the Lost, Bloom Tender) and follow with Mysterio, Master of Illusion for a token board. Squirming Emergence re-buys the best nonland permanent in the yard — usually Leader or Mysterio — when the board has been swept.

Curve and mana

Twenty of the thirty-six nonland cards cost two, which is why the deck can afford twenty-four lands and a four-colour manabase. Sixteen basics carry the coloured requirements, with five fetch lands to find the missing one: five Islands and five Swamps make blue and black the main colours, three Forests and three Plains are the splash for Bloom Tender, Sheltered by Ghosts and Raise the Past. Bloom Tender is the safety valve — with a Villain in play it taps for black and blue, and with a second colour on board it pays for Raise the Past or Squirming Emergence on its own.

White is the lightest splash: two Sheltered by Ghosts and two Raise the Past. Do not keep a hand that needs double white before turn five.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with a land that produces black or blue. Red Room Recruit is the cleanest turn two: it connives immediately, and the counter it keeps makes it a 2/3 that survives most one-for-one removal. Mob Lookout is the defensive option — a 0/3 that connives whichever creature you want, including a Souls of the Lost you cast afterwards only if the Lookout is still around.

Discard discipline is the whole deck. Discard a permanent you can recur (a two-drop Villain, or a Squirming Emergence target) rather than the land you will need on turn four.

Mid game and the combo turn

The “combo turn” is a chain of loots rather than a single loop:

  1. Leader, Super-Genius on board makes every connive draw two and discard one.
  2. Mob Lookout or Unstable Experiment triggers a connive, which draws two, keeps the best, and puts a permanent in the yard.
  3. Baron Strucker, HYDRA Overlord adds one free connive per turn, and discounts each Villain cast by {1}.
  4. Cast Souls of the Lost for {1}{B} discarding a spare permanent. With six permanents in the yard it is a 6/7; two more connives make it an 8/9.
  5. Activate Rogue's Passage and attack. Unblockable, and nothing in the deck needs to tap for it beyond the four generic mana.

Squirming Emergence on the same turn re-buys whatever the chain discarded — Mysterio for a token burst, or Leader if the engine was removed.

Win condition two

Mysterio, Master of Illusion counts nontoken Villains on entry: Mob Lookout, Red Room Recruit, Doc Ock’s Henchmen, Leader, Strucker and Mysterio himself. Five Villains means five 3/3 Illusion Villain tokens, which is usually a lethal alpha strike the turn after a sweeper. Because the tokens are exiled when Mysterio leaves, cast him second main phase or with Sheltered by Ghosts backup. Raise the Past returning four two-drop creatures is the follow-up wave — it rebuys the Villain count and the Souls of the Lost in one card.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: two lands (one black or blue), a two-drop connive creature, and one payoff (Souls, Leader, or Mysterio).
  • Keep: any hand with Bloom Tender plus two lands and a Villain, since Bloom Tender fixes four colours.
  • Ship: hands with fewer than two lands, and hands with no connive enabler at all. The deck has sixteen enablers; a hand without one is a hand that does nothing until turn four.
  • Ship: hands that need double white early. Raise the Past is a late card here.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Leader, Super-Genius is a replacement effect: the creature still gets its +1/+1 counter, and you draw the extra card before the connive resolves. Two Leaders do not stack — the second is a 1/3 body.
  • Baron Strucker grants one connive per turn, no matter how many Villains enter. Order the enters so the biggest body gets the counter.
  • Souls of the Lost’s power is a static ability. Killing it does not shrink the yard, and reanimating it with Squirming Emergence (mana value 2) or Raise the Past brings it back at full size.
  • Squirming Emergence needs a nonland permanent card in the yard and enough permanents there to cover its mana value. Count before you cast; connive in response is not possible, but an instant-speed Unstable Experiment beforehand is.
  • Nowhere to Run also strips hexproof and ward from the target, which matters against Ward creatures that would otherwise blank Sheltered by Ghosts.
  • Villainous Hideout’s coloured mana only pays for Villain spells — do not count it as a source for Squirming Emergence or Raise the Past.

Matchups

  • Aggro: the deck’s 0/3 and 1/2 bodies trade badly. Lean on Nowhere to Run, Sheltered by Ghosts and flash Doc Ock's Henchmen; race with a big Souls only once the yard is deep.
  • Control: Cavern of Souls naming Human protects the Villain half, and Leader, Super-Genius out-draws one-for-one removal. Hold Squirming Emergence for the turn after their sweeper.
  • Graveyard hate: the deck loses the Souls sizing but not the plan. Mysterio, Master of Illusion and Raise the Past both work from the board, and Bloom Tender keeps the mana clean.

Iterations

Three battery rounds were run; the shipped list is the v1 configuration, which produced the highest simulated win rate while every other gate passed.
