Overview

Void asks a single question every turn: did a nonland permanent leave the battlefield this turn? Every Void card in the deck reads the same condition, and the deck is built so the answer is always yes.

The cheapest yes in Standard is Evoke. Catharsis, Vibrance and Emptiness are Elemental Incarnations with hybrid evoke costs of two mana. Cast one for its evoke cost, collect the enters trigger, and the creature is sacrificed on entry. That one two-mana play pays for every Void card in the deck for the rest of the turn, and it pays at a profit: Catharsis evoked with two red gives the whole team +1/+1 and haste, so the same two mana that turns Void on also turns the board sideways.

The payoffs split into two halves that feed each other. The Void half converts the condition into permanent advantage at end of step: Elegy Acolyte grows a 2/2 Robot, Insatiable Skittermaw grows a counter, Alpharael, Stonechosen halves the defending player’s life when he attacks. The attack half converts a wide board into damage: Ingris Stingerquill makes every single attacker ping, Marshal of the Lost turns attacker count into power, War Effort adds a free attacker to every combat, Suki, Kyoshi Warrior is as large as the board and brings a friend. Nothing in the deck is a dead card on its own: the engine is a condition, not a two-card combo, so thirteen enablers and twenty payoffs all read the same line of text.

Approach

Game plan phases

Phase Turns What you are doing
Set up 1-2 Land, Hylderblade or a one-mana interaction spell, hold an evoke creature
Ignition 2-3 Evoke for two mana, Void live, attack with haste or add a Void body
Snowball 4-6 Void payoffs accumulate robots, counters and cards while the board widens
Close 6+ Alpharael attack, Ingris ping turn, or Chorale reanimation swing

The deck wants to be the one asking the questions. It does not need to combo off in a single turn; it needs the Void condition true on every one of your turns, which the evoke creatures guarantee as long as you have one in hand or one in the graveyard for Emptiness.

Dalkovan Encampment is the land that wins games. Two mana and a tap after combat is declared gives you two attacking Warriors, and those tokens are exactly what Ingris Stingerquill, Marshal of the Lost and War Effort are counting. It enters untapped as long as you have a Swamp or a Mountain, which the four Blood Crypt and three Godless Shrine supply.

Curve and mana

The printed curve looks top-heavy because the three evoke creatures cost six, five and six. Counted at the price you actually pay, it is a two-drop deck with a real top end:

Cost 1 2 3 4 5
Cards 6 10 7 12 2

The one-drops are Hylderblade and Plasma Bolt; the twos are the three evoke creatures plus Finneas; the fives are two Alpharael. Two colours carry the engine, black and red, and both are on turn one through eleven untapped duals. White is the token and deathtouch colour, green is the Suki and Finneas colour, and neither is needed before turn three. Twenty-three lands with four shocks and five fastlands puts four lands on the battlefield by turn four about 62 percent of the time.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two decide the shape of the game. Hylderblade on one is the best opening in the deck: one mana for three power, and every later turn with Void on lets you move it onto whichever creature is actually connecting. Plasma Bolt on one kills a mana creature or clears a blocker.

Turn two is usually the ignition turn. Evoke Catharsis paying two red for a team-wide +1/+1 and haste, then attack with everything. Paying two white instead gives two 1/1 Kithkin, which is the right choice when you are holding Suki, Finneas or a Marshal rather than an empty board. Either mode is a two-mana play that leaves Void switched on for the rest of the turn.

Mid game

From turn three the deck plays one threat and one piece of interaction per turn while the Void triggers stack up. Elegy Acolyte is the best four-drop in the deck: a 4/4 lifelink that draws a card whenever the team connects and grows a Robot every turn Void is live. Those Robots are the fuel for the wide swing later, and they never cost a card.

Chorale of the Void is the quiet engine. Enchant the creature that attacks most, and each combat returns a creature from the defending player’s graveyard tapped and attacking. It is also self-preserving: it is only sacrificed at your end step if nothing left the battlefield, which the evoke creature already paid for.

The combo turn

The signature turn costs five mana and wins from a losing board.

  1. Evoke Catharsis for two red: team gets +1/+1 and haste, the Elemental is sacrificed, Void is on.
  2. Attack with everything, including any creature that entered this turn.
  3. With Void live, Alpharael, Stonechosen attacking makes the defending player lose half their life, rounded up. Any Void creature still in hand is also live: Temporal Intervention costs a single black to strip their answer, Plasma Bolt deals three.
  4. At end of step, Elegy Acolyte makes a Robot, Insatiable Skittermaw takes a counter, Hylderblade reattaches for free.

Two Alpharael attacks take a full life total to a quarter, and the second one usually arrives with four or five other attackers behind it.

Win condition two

Ingris Stingerquill is the deck’s second, separate clock. She is a 1/4 flyer for three, and every creature that attacks deals 1 damage to each opponent, so a six-creature swing is six extra damage on top of combat. She turns War Effort, Dalkovan Encampment, Suki and the Elegy Acolyte Robots into direct damage, and she blocks well while you build.

The same wide board also makes Suki, Kyoshi Warrior enormous: her power equals your creature count. Sequence her after the tokens, not before.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands, one evoke creature and one Void payoff. A one-land hand is keepable on the play only with Hylderblade or Plasma Bolt and a second land in the top three. Ship hands with four or more lands and no evoke creature, or hands that curve Alpharael into Chorale with no cheap plays: those are payoffs, not plans.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Evoke is the switch, and it is one-sided. The sacrificed Elemental counts as a nonland permanent that left the battlefield, so the condition is true for the rest of that turn, including your end step. Cast the evoke creature in your first main phase and every Void card in hand is upgraded for the turn. The enters trigger and the sacrifice trigger both go on the stack on entry; order it so the enters trigger resolves first when you need the tokens or the pump before combat.
  • Hylderblade never pays its equip cost. With Void live, the end-step trigger attaches it to any creature for free, so one blade moves every turn. It also makes the equipped creature modified, which is what Araña, Heart of the Spider is looking for.
  • Chorale’s sacrifice clause is a bonus, not a cost. The aura is only lost if nothing left the battlefield that turn. A single evoke creature keeps it for the whole game.
  • Temporal Intervention at one mana. Void reduces its cost by two, so the discard spell costs a single black. It is the cleanest answer to a board wipe or a counterspell.
  • Emptiness evokes into a reanimation. Paying two white returns a creature with mana value three or less from the graveyard, which includes a previously evoked Catharsis or a Skittermaw, and the returned creature stays.
  • Catharsis modes are a real choice. Two red is the alpha-strike turn; two white is the go-wide turn. Read which payoff is in hand before you tap the mana.

Matchups

Aggro. Elegy Acolyte is the whole matchup: a 4/4 lifelink that draws cards stabilises faster than anything else in the deck. Three Plasma Bolt are the only direct removal, so block early, trade tokens freely and let lifelink do the work.

Control. Do not commit more than two threats at once. Hold an evoke creature so you can rebuild after a wipe, and use Temporal Intervention at one mana to strip their sweeper. Chorale of the Void is the card they struggle with most, because it turns their own removal into your board.

Graveyard decks. Chorale of the Void steals from the defending graveyard every combat. Against decks that fill their own yard, the aura is a one-card army.

Ramp and midrange. Race with Ingris and the token swarm. Their creatures are bigger, but every attacker pings, Marshal of the Lost turns a wide board lethal, and Alpharael halves life totals that no amount of ramp protects.
