Sphinx Quillstorm

Jund (black / red / green) Elder Sphinx legends + Heartwood-to-Dragon combo — Reality Fracture

Reality Fracture is Standard-legal, but every card in the set still carries no arena_id in the card data. The import list at the end becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there.

Overview

The Elder Sphinx cycle is the spine of this list, and two of the five are the win conditions. Ingris Stingerquill is a three-mana 1/4 flier that turns every attacker into a one-point drain on each opponent; Aerid Konstrari is a four-mana 5/4 flier that pays a Heartwood token on entry and again on death. Everything else either makes attackers, makes Heartwoods, or converts Heartwoods into something lethal.

The conversion card is Draconic Visitor. Its text is a replacement effect, not a trigger: “if one or more artifact tokens would be created under your control, that many 5/5 red Dragon creature tokens with flying are created instead.” Every Heartwood the deck would make becomes a Dragon instead. Aerid Konstrari enters and dies for Dragons. Tenured Tethermage sacrifices one spare land and gets two Dragons. Each prepared Soul Tether copy turns three mana into a Dragon. That is the combo: the deck’s mana-rock engine becomes a Dragon engine the moment a Visitor lands.

The Heartwood supply is the prepared cycle. Heartwood Crafter, Konstrari Improviser and Woodwork Prodigy all enter prepared, and while prepared you may cast a copy of Soul Tether ({2}{R/G}) to create a Heartwood and unprepare the creature. Woodwork Prodigy re-prepares at every upkeep, so it is a Dragon every turn once the Visitor is out. Before the Visitor arrives those same tokens are red-green mana rocks, which is how a five-drop flier gets cast on turn four.

The second axis is drain rather than damage. Ingris Stingerquill triggers on each creature you attack with, so four Dragons deal four combat and four noncombat damage per opponent per turn, through blockers. Master of Barbs reads that noncombat damage as a team-wide +1/+0. Hall of Echoes copies Ingris for a turn — the legend rule is waived — so every attacker pings twice. Combat and drain finish together; the red zone does not need to be clear.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-3, build the Heartwood economy. Heartwood Crafter on one is the ideal opener: it enters prepared, so turn two’s three mana buys a Soul Tether copy and leaves a Heartwood behind. Konstrari Improviser does the same job a turn later with a better body. Tenured Tethermage on three trades one land for two Heartwoods — a mana profit on the spot, and two Dragons later.
  2. Turns 3-4, land the sphinxes. Ingris Stingerquill turns the early bodies into drain immediately, and Aerid Konstrari is a 5/4 flier that leaves a Dragon behind whatever happens to it. Woodwork Prodigy holds the engine together by re-arming every upkeep, so the deck keeps making tokens without spending cards.
  3. Turns 4-6, convert. Draconic Visitor is the turn the deck stops playing mana rocks. From then on every Soul Tether copy, every Tethermage entry and every Aerid death is a 5/5 flying Dragon. Attack with everything: Ingris drains on declaration, Master of Barbs pumps the team off that drain, and Hall of Echoes copies Ingris for a second round of pings when the board stalls.

Curve and mana

The curve is low and double-peaked: eleven one- and two-mana plays, eleven three-drops, three four-drops, four five-drops. Twenty-four lands is deliberate — the deck wants five mana for Draconic Visitor on time, and Heartwood tokens are the ramp that gets there. Red and green are the primary colours (twelve sources each), black the splash (ten) for Ingris Stingerquill and Terminal Criticism. Konstrari Charm’s {C}{C}{C} mode pays generic costs only: use it on Soul Tether, Hall of Echoes, or a Tethermage-plus-charm turn, never on a coloured spell.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with Heartwood Crafter whenever it is in the opener: a one-mana 1/1 that taps for colourless and casts a free Soul Tether copy is the best turn one in the deck. Turn two is either the copy (three mana total, one Heartwood) or Konstrari Improviser into the same play on three. Hold Stingerquill Charm for a real target — the hasty Cadet mode is often the better half when you are the beatdown.

Mid game and the combo turn

The combo turn is the turn Draconic Visitor resolves. Before that, dump every Soul Tether copy you can afford, because the tokens stop being mana the moment the Visitor is on the battlefield. After it, sequence for maximum token entries: Tenured Tethermage first (two Dragons for a land), then Aerid Konstrari (a Dragon and a 5/4 flier), then any prepared body. Five mana for the Visitor plus three for a Tethermage is a ten-power flying board from two cards.

Win condition 2

When the ground stalls or the opponent has more life than the air force can chew, switch to drain. Attack with every creature you have — even a 1/1 Cadet is a point to each opponent — and let Ingris Stingerquill convert the attack step into noncombat damage. Master of Barbs converts that same damage into power, so the second attack is bigger than the first. Hall of Echoes with five spare mana doubles the Ingris trigger for a turn; that is usually eight to twelve points of drain across two combats and it does not care about blockers.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with a one-drop prepared creature plus two lands, and keep one-land hands only with Heartwood Crafter and a second one-mana play. Four- and five-mana hands without a Heartwood producer are mulligans: the deck needs the token engine online before the Visitor, otherwise the Visitor is just a 5/5 flier. A hand of Ingris Stingerquill plus cheap attackers is a legitimate keep on the play — the drain plan does not need the Dragon engine to win.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Draconic Visitor is a replacement effect. While it is on the battlefield, Soul Tether no longer ramps you — no Heartwood, no mana. Cast the ramp copies before the Visitor, not after. Two Visitors do not stack; the second is only a 5/5 flier.
  • Aerid Konstrari pays a Heartwood on death as well as entry, so trading it in combat is often two Dragons’ worth of value across the game.
  • Ingris Stingerquill triggers on declaration of attackers, before blockers, so the drain happens even when the attack is unfavourable. Its {4} activation makes a Cadet and then grants haste to the whole team — use it in the first main phase if you want the Cadet to attack.
  • Hall of Echoes copies a creature, so the land stops being a land for the turn. Do not activate it with your only black source and an Ingris in hand.
  • Terminal Criticism only answers blue or red permanents; against green and white threats the deck leans on Stingerquill Charm and Konstrari Charm.
  • Konstrari Charm’s mana mode produces colourless mana: it pays generic costs and Hall of Echoes, never a coloured pip.

Matchups

Against aggro, block with Tethermages, trade Aerid for value and race with Dragons. Against control, overload the board with token entries in one turn and force a sweeper before the drain plan comes online; Master of Barbs punishes them for tapping out. Against other midrange decks the Dragon conversion is the trump, and Hall of Echoes copying Ingris ends the long games. Against ramp, be the beatdown: curve Heartwood Crafter into Improviser into Ingris and drain before their payoffs land.
