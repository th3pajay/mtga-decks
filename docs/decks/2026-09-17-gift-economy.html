Gift Economy

Black / Red / White (three colors) — a Gift build-around that turns every promised gift into a drain trigger

combo
gift
Published

September 17, 2026

Colors Black / Red / White (three colors)
Archetype Midrange combo — Gift spells plus a draw-punishment drain engine
Size 60 cards (36 spells and permanents, 24 lands, average mana value 2.61 across nonlands)

Measured performance Sim 52.3 percent over three 500-game runs (50, 58, 49), turn-4 consistency 0.623, four-land probability by turn 4 of 0.602, synergy 72.04, cohesion 72

Overview

Gift Economy is built on the one thing the Gift mechanic always does: it pays a cost. Promising a gift means an opponent draws a card or creates a token before the spell’s other effects resolve, and every single Gift card in the deck is a card that would be played anyway — removal, an edict, recursion, a two-card draw spell. The deck simply refuses to pay that cost in a vacuum.

Every promised gift of a card is a draw for the opponent, and that draw is the engine. Razorkin Needlehead deals 1 damage to the opponent each time they draw a card. Scrawling Crawler makes them lose 1 life for the same event and keeps both players’ hands full at every upkeep. Gleaming Splendor turns the opponent’s second draw of a turn into a Treasure token for us. Cast Consumed by Greed with the gift promised and the opponent draws a card first — so the spell is an edict plus 1 or 2 points of drain, and on their own draw step it also pays a Treasure. Seven copies across three Gift spells promise a card, and every one of them is a spell the deck wants to cast anyway.

The other four Gift spells promise a token instead — a tapped Fish, a Food, a Treasure. Those promises cost the opponent nothing in cards, so the drain engine ignores them, but they cost the deck nothing either: Nocturnal Hunger destroys any creature for {2}{B} once the Food is promised and only costs us 2 life when it is not, Parting Gust exiles a creature permanently for {W}{W}, and Blooming Blast adds 3 damage to the controller’s face. In this deck the gift is never the downside it looks like on the card.

The second half of the deck is the life ledger. Sanguine Syphoner drains 1 on every attack. Melancholic Poet drains 1 every time an instant or sorcery targets a creature, which in this list is most of the deck. Marauding Blight-Priest turns every point of life we gain into a point each opponent loses. Bloodthirsty Conqueror refunds us every point of life an opponent loses. Put Blight-Priest and Conqueror together and the ledger never closes: as soon as one life is lost anywhere, the loop runs until the table is dead.

Note on the mechanic: a promised gift resolves before the rest of the spell, and the word “gift” in the reminder text means exactly the object named — a card (the opponent draws), a tapped 1/1 blue Fish token, a Food token, or a Treasure token. Nothing else changes about the spell, and the promise is always optional, so a Gift spell cast without a promise is just a worse card.

Approach

Game plan phases

Phase Turns What the deck is doing
Open 1 to 2 Nothing costs one. Turn two is the engine turn: Razorkin Needlehead as a 2/2 for {R}{R}, Gleaming Splendor as a two-mana enchantment, Sanguine Syphoner as a blocker that drains, or Melancholic Poet in front of a hand full of removal
Build 3 to 4 Scrawling Crawler, Marauding Blight-Priest and the first Gift spells. Every removal spell is now a drain trigger, and the opponent is drawing into a board they cannot keep up with
Drain 4 to 6 Parting Gust and Consumed by Greed clear the way while the opponent loses 2 to 3 life a turn to their own draw steps. Sanguine Syphoner and Marauding Blight-Priest convert attacks and life gain into more damage
Close 5 plus Bloodthirsty Conqueror, an active Marauding Blight-Priest, or the attacking Restless Fortress end it. Coiling Rebirth and Dewdrop Cure rebuild the engine after a wipe

Curve and mana

Mana value Count Cards

Two 22 Razorkin Needlehead, Melancholic Poet, Sanguine Syphoner, Gleaming Splendor, Parting Gust, Blooming Blast, Sazacap’s Brew

Five 3 Bloodthirsty Conqueror, Coiling Rebirth

There is no one-drop and no four-drop, which is the shape of a deck whose turn two is the engine and whose turn three is interaction. Two mana holds twenty-two of the thirty-six nonlands, and the average nonland mana value is 2.61, well under the four-mana ceiling the build is held to. Twenty-four lands is deliberate: four-land probability by turn four is 0.602 and turn-four consistency 0.623, and the deck genuinely wants the fourth land so that Coiling Rebirth or Consumed by Greed plus a promise can be cast on curve.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are for the engine, not for damage. Razorkin Needlehead is the best turn-two play in the deck because it turns every later Gift spell into damage, but Gleaming Splendor is the more important one against anything slow: it starts paying Treasures the moment the opponent’s draw step plus any gift draw makes two cards, and the Treasures are what pay for the three- and five-mana halves of the deck.

Do not cast removal without the gift. Parting Gust without a promise is a temporary exile that gives the creature back with a +1/+1 counter; with the promise it exiles the creature forever, and if Needlehead or Crawler is out the card the opponent draws costs them 1 or 2 life. The same logic applies to Nocturnal Hunger, which only makes us lose 2 life when the Food is not promised, and to Blooming Blast, which loses 3 points of reach without its Treasure.

Sanguine Syphoner is the safest turn-two body: a 1/3 that drains on every attack and survives most one- and two-mana removal. Melancholic Poet, on the other hand, is a 2/2 that should be played on a turn when a Parting Gust, Nocturnal Hunger or Blooming Blast is already in hand, because otherwise it does nothing.

Mid game

Turn three is when the drain engine starts compounding. Scrawling Crawler draws both players a card in the upkeep, taking 1 life off the opponent, and then any Gift-a-card spell that turn takes another 1 or 2 and hands us a Treasure from Gleaming Splendor. A turn of Crawler, Consumed by Greed with the gift promised and a Melancholic Poet trigger is 4 points of life loss and a creature sacrificed, from three cards.

The board is meant to be small. Marauding Blight-Priest is a 3/2 that exists only for its trigger; Sanguine Syphoner attacks every turn whether or not it can be blocked. What matters is that the opponent keeps drawing: their draw step, our Gift spells, Scrawling Crawler’s upkeep trigger and Gleaming Splendor’s {2}{W} ability all feed the same three payoffs, so the deck is never short of triggers, only of cards — which is why the Splendor ability targeting us and the opponent at once is almost always correct in the mid game.

Dewdrop Cure is the engine’s reset button. With the gift promised it returns up to three creatures with mana value 2 or less, which is Razorkin Needlehead, Melancholic Poet and Sanguine Syphoner in one card. Against a wipe, that is the play that decides whether the deck still has a game.

The combo turn

The kill turn is either a drain turn or the loop.

  1. Get Marauding Blight-Priest and Bloodthirsty Conqueror onto the board at the same time. The loop is mandatory on both sides: an opponent losing life makes Conqueror gain us that much life, and every life gain makes Blight-Priest drain each opponent for 1. One point of life loss anywhere — a Needlehead ping, a Syphoner attack, a Restless Fortress swing, one damage from Blooming Blast — and the drain repeats without end.
  2. If the loop pieces are not both available, drain instead: play two Gift-a-card spells in one turn with Needlehead and Crawler on the board. Each promised gift is a draw, and each draw is 2 life — 1 from Needlehead and 1 from Crawler — so two gifts plus the opponent’s own draw step is 6 life, and Scrawling Crawler’s upkeep trigger makes it 8. Blooming Blast adds 3 more to a creature controller’s face.
  3. Attack with Restless Fortress activated for {2}{W}{B}. The attacking Nightmare drains 2 from the defending player and gains us 2, which triggers Blight-Priest for 1 more to each opponent.
  4. Hold Parting Gust for the turn the opponent commits their best creature. With the Fish promised it exiles it permanently for two mana, and the Fish token is the cheapest gift in the deck because it costs the opponent no cards.

Win condition 2

The deck also wins with creatures, and that plan needs no loop: Sanguine Syphoner drains on every attack, Melancholic Poet drains on every removal spell, Restless Fortress attacks as a 1/4 that drains 2, and Soulstone Sanctuary becomes a 3/3 vigilant attacker out of spare mana. Against a deck that removes the engine, keep attacking with whatever survives and let the split damage from attacks, drain triggers and the Restless Fortress activation add up.

Coiling Rebirth is the bridge between the two plans. Reanimating a Bloodthirsty Conqueror with the gift promised returns a 5/5 flying deathtouch body plus a 1/1 copy of it, which is two Conqueror triggers on the stack and two attack steps from one card. Reanimating a Scrawling Crawler after a wipe restarts the draw engine immediately.

Mulligan guide

Hand shape Verdict
Two lands, Razorkin Needlehead and any Gift spell Keep. This is the deck’s ideal opening: the engine online on turn two with a way to fire it
Two lands, Gleaming Splendor and two black removal spells Keep. The Splendor pays for the rest and the removal is drain once anything is on the board
One land, any hand Mulligan. The deck has no one-drop and needs three mana before the drain plan is worth anything
Four lands, Dewdrop Cure, Coiling Rebirth, Scrawling Crawler Keep. Crawler restarts the engine and the recursion spells mean the first wipe is survivable
Five lands, Bloodthirsty Conqueror, Coiling Rebirth Mulligan. Two five-drops in the opener without an engine is a hand that loses to its own curve
Two lands, but only one colour of source in hand Mulligan. Black is the deepest colour but Razorkin Needlehead wants two red, so an opener with no black source is the one to send back

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
The gift resolves before the spell A promised gift happens first, so the opponent draws (or makes the token) before the spell’s main effect. Parting Gust’s exile and Consumed by Greed’s edict still happen after the gift draw, and the draw can never take the target away because the gift is not a spell the opponent controls
Gift is optional and the word means exactly what it says Gift a card means the opponent draws a card. Gift a tapped Fish, Gift a Food and Gift a Treasure create those tokens for them. Of the seven Gift spells in the deck only Consumed by Greed, Dewdrop Cure and Coiling Rebirth promise a card, so only those three feed Razorkin Needlehead and Scrawling Crawler
Needlehead triggers on any draw Razorkin Needlehead does not care whose turn it is or why the card was drawn — the opponent’s own draw step counts, so the engine deals damage every turn even when the deck casts nothing
Gleaming Splendor counts draws, not turns It triggers on the opponent’s second card drawn in a single turn. A Scrawling Crawler upkeep draw plus a promised Consumed by Greed on the same turn is exactly two, which is a Treasure. Two Splendors make two Treasures
Melancholic Poet’s repartee It needs an instant or sorcery that targets a creature, and it triggers on the cast, not the resolution, so a Gift spell cast into a counterspell still drains 1 and gains us 1
Marauding Blight-Priest plus Bloodthirsty Conqueror Both triggers are mandatory and neither is a may, so once one point of life is lost by an opponent the loop cannot be stopped. The loop needs both permanents in play; it does not need a Gift spell or an attack to start, and Scrawling Crawler’s upkeep trigger is enough to begin it
Nocturnal Hunger without the gift Destroying a creature without promising the Food costs us 2 life. In races, that is sometimes correct — but the Food costs nothing and only ever gains the opponent 3 life, so promise it
Parting Gust on our own creatures With no gift promised, the exiled creature returns at the beginning of the next end step with a +1/+1 counter, which is a way to reset a Razorkin Needlehead that has been hit by a -3/-3 effect or to re-trigger nothing at all. Its real value against our own board is protection from a sorcery-speed wipe
Coiling Rebirth’s copy The 1/1 token copy only appears if the gift was promised and the returned creature is not legendary, which is why the deck runs no legends. Copying a Scrawling Crawler doubles the upkeep draw and both drain triggers
Restless Fortress activation timing It is an activated ability, so it can be used after blockers are declared. The 2 life loss is the defending player’s, and the 2 life gained is ours, which is one Blight-Priest trigger whether it connects or not
Soulstone Sanctuary’s mana It taps for colorless. Treat it as a spell land, not a source for a colored spell, and count it only when the deck has four lands and spare mana

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain counters Race the drain rather than the board. Nocturnal Hunger and Parting Gust with the promised gift kill the biggest threat outright, Sanguine Syphoner blocks and drains, and the deck’s damage comes from their own draw steps, so it wins with almost no attackers. Bloodthirsty Conqueror is the card that ends these games: every point they take is a point we gain
Midrange The strong matchup. Every removal spell two-for-ones against a curve of expensive threats because the gift’s only drawback is a card the opponent draws into a Needlehead ping, and Consumed by Greed forces the sacrifice of their largest creature. Board the drain plan first, keep the loop as the closer
Control The weak matchup. Wipes do remove the engine, so keep Dewdrop Cure for after the first wipe and do not over-commit the enchantments: one Gleaming Splendor plus one Needlehead is enough pressure to force a wipe, and Scrawling Crawler refills the hand afterwards. The loop is the way through countermagic, because it needs only two permanents already in play
Combo Faster combo decks are the real problem, because this deck’s clock is turn five or six. The interaction is Parting Gust and Nocturnal Hunger, both of which should be held for the turn the opponent commits, plus Blooming Blast’s 3 damage to a controller’s face as reach. Melancholic Poet drains while the removal is spent, which is what closes these games
