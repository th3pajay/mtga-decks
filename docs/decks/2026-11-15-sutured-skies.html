Reality Fracture is Standard-legal, but every card in the set still carries no arena_id in the card data. The import list at the end becomes playable on Arena once the set is enabled there.

Overview

Reality Fracture’s prepared mechanic gives a creature a spell stapled to it: while the creature is prepared you may cast a copy of that spell, and casting it unprepares the creature. Six of this deck’s creatures enter prepared, and all six carry one of two cheap spells. Emergency Phytomedic, Vigorbloom Vanguard and Blossom-Blessed Angel carry Seed Suture ({G/W}, sorcery: put a +1/+1 counter on target creature, gain 1 life). Fatehold Chronologist and Prudent Fateseer carry Peer Review ({2}{W/U}, sorcery: create a 2/2 Cadet, surveil 1). Variable Chaser carries Arc of Fortune.

The doubling card is Codie, Ravenous Codex. Its first line is “whenever you cast a prepared spell, copy it” — so every one of those cheap back-face spells resolves twice for one card and one payment. Two Seed Sutures off a single prepared body means two counters and two life; two Peer Reviews mean two Cadets and two surveils.

The counter payoff is Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion: any +1/+1 counter put on your creature becomes one more. A single copied Seed Suture therefore places four counters across two creatures, and Vigorbloom Vanguard gives every creature that has a counter vigilance, which is what makes attacking and re-preparing compatible — a vigilant attacker is still untapped in the postcombat main phase, which is when Hexhaven Dueling Arena wants to re-arm it.

The lifegain is a second engine rather than a rounding error. Bloombrute draws a card the first time you gain life each turn, Kwia Vigorbloom makes a Lotus token (three mana of one colour) the first time you gain life each turn, and Germinate Recruits makes X 2/2 Cadets where X is the life you gained that turn. A turn of two copied Seed Sutures is four life: a card, a Lotus, and four Cadets for three mana.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-3, establish prepared bodies. Emergency Phytomedic on one is the ideal opener — a 1/1 that holds a Seed Suture. Turn two is Vigorbloom Vanguard, Fatehold Chronologist or the Phytomedic’s spell; turn three is Prudent Fateseer or Codie.
  2. Turns 3-5, turn the crank. With Codie down, every prepared spell is doubled, so the order is: cast the cheapest prepared spell first to bank value, then attack, then spend {2} on Hexhaven Dueling Arena in the postcombat main to re-prepare the attacker. Yoshimaru should be cast before the counter spells whenever possible — it does nothing on its own and everything to a Seed Suture.
  3. Turns 5-7, convert. Two paths close the game. Wide: Germinate Recruits plus Peer Review Cadets plus a Bestial Incursion Beast, all carrying counters, with Vigorbloom Vanguard keeping them on defence. Tall: Kwia Vigorbloom with counters and vigilance, backed by Bloombrute drawing a card a turn and Lotus mana paying for a second spell per turn.

Curve and mana

The curve is deliberately flat: four one-drops, ten two-drops, twelve three-drops, seven four-drops and two six-drops. Twenty-five lands is high for a deck this cheap, and it is the correct number — the engine consumes lands through Hexhaven Dueling Arena activations, and the deck would rather flood than miss a re-prepare. Green is the primary colour (eighteen sources), white is the secondary (twelve), blue is the splash (six sources for two Variable Chaser). Fatehold Chronologist, Prudent Fateseer and Seed Suture are hybrid, so blue is only truly needed for Variable Chaser; if the blue source is missing, the deck still functions.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with Emergency Phytomedic or Vigorbloom Vanguard. Casting a Seed Suture copy on turn one or two is usually worse than developing a second prepared body, because an unused prepared creature is a spell you still own. Fatehold Chronologist is the best turn-two play when you expect to be attacking — a 1/2 flier plus two Cadets from a copied Peer Review is four power across three bodies for two mana and then three more on the following turn.

Mid game and the combo turn

The combo turn is any turn where three things line up: Codie on the battlefield, two prepared creatures, and {2} spare for Hexhaven Dueling Arena. Cast the first prepared spell, let Codie copy it, cast the second, let Codie copy that, then attack with the vigilant bodies and re-prepare the best one in the postcombat main. With Yoshimaru out, each copied Seed Suture is four counters; with Prudent Fateseer out, the second Peer Review’s surveil pumps the whole team.

Win condition 2

The lifegain line is the second, independent win. Bloombrute turns the first lifegain of each turn into a card, which is how the deck keeps casting spells after it has emptied its hand. Kwia Vigorbloom turns the same trigger into three mana of one colour, which pays a Peer Review or a Hexhaven activation outright. Germinate Recruits is the finisher of that line: two Seed Suture casts (four life once Codie doubles them) plus Vigorbloom Charm’s draw mode (three life) is seven life, and the three-mana instant then makes seven 2/2s.

Mulligan guide

Keep two or three lands with at least two prepared creatures and one spell you intend to cast. A hand with Codie and three prepared bodies is a keep on the play even with two lands, because the deck’s whole plan costs two to four mana. Mulligan hands with no prepared creature, hands with Hexhaven Dueling Arena as the only land, and hands holding two six-drops. Yoshimaru and Kwia Vigorbloom are the two cards worth keeping a slower hand for; Vigorbloom Charm alone is not.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Prepared is a state, not a trigger. Casting the spell unprepares the creature, and nothing re-prepares it except Hexhaven Dueling Arena and its own rules text — so sequence Hexhaven after combat, never before.
  • Codie copies the prepared spell as it is cast, so the copy resolves first. That matters for Peer Review: the copy’s surveil resolves before the original’s, so Prudent Fateseer’s pump applies to the same combat only if you are in a main phase.
  • Yoshimaru adds one counter per counter-putting event, not per counter. Two Seed Sutures are two events of one counter, so each becomes two counters — four total, not six.
  • Germinate Recruits counts all life gained that turn, including Vigorbloom Charm’s draw mode (3 life) and Kwia Vigorbloom’s lifelink. It does not count life gained on the opponent’s turn.
  • Hexhaven Dueling Arena’s {2} mode requires the creature to have attacked this turn and can only be activated as a sorcery; the {4} mode has no such restriction. A vigilant attacker from Vigorbloom Vanguard is the cleanest way to use both halves.
  • Kwia Vigorbloom’s Lotus token adds three mana of a single colour, which does not pay Codie’s five-colour activation on its own — it pays the generic side of Bestial Incursion’s flashback or a Peer Review copy instead.
  • The deck has no unconditional removal. Vigorbloom Charm’s third mode (counter, then fight) is the answer to a large creature, and Blossom-Blessed Angel plus Prudent Fateseer are the blockers that buy the time to find it.

Matchups

Against aggro, the deck wants Vigorbloom Vanguard and Blossom-Blessed Angel on defence and Vigorbloom Charm held for the biggest attacker; the lifegain from Seed Suture is real life total, not just a trigger. Against control, do not over-commit prepared bodies into a sweeper — hold two creatures and cast the spells after the wipe, then rebuild with Bestial Incursion’s flashback and Bloombrute. Against midrange, the counter engine outclasses most boards once Yoshimaru is down. Against ramp, the deck is the beatdown: curve prepared bodies into Kwia Vigorbloom and use Hexhaven every turn to keep the pressure on, because a long game favours their payoffs.
