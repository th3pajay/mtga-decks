Overview

Eavesong is the Elf-legends build of The Hobbit set, and its engine is the one thing Elves and lands share in this set: landfall. Ten of the deck’s thirty-six nonland cards either trigger on a land entering or put extra lands onto the battlefield, and the two payoffs are both legendary Elves. Thranduil, Sindarin Liege gives the whole Elf team +1/+1 and makes a 1/1 green Elf token every time a land enters. Thranduil’s Company lets you play an extra land each turn and puts two +1/+1 counters on a creature on every landfall, with vigilance attached. Two creatures, one land per turn, and the board grows in three directions at once.

The second engine is mana. Wood Elves puts a Forest onto the battlefield, Woodland Weavemaster taps for one mana for each point of its power (and it grows whenever another Elf enters), and Thranduil’s Company adds a land drop. The deck reaches five and six mana early, which is where Thranduil, the Elvenking and Bard, King of Dale live.

Thranduil, the Elvenking is the top of the curve and the reason the deck is four colors. He draws two cards every time another legendary Elf enters — and there are six legendary Elves in the list. His first ability is the sharper one: he has all activated abilities of all Elf cards in your graveyard, so the unblockable activation from Elvenking’s Harper, the three counters from Guardian of the Halls and the mana ability from Woodland Weavemaster stay available to him after those creatures die. Elrond, Moon-Reader converts each of those activations into a card.

Bard, King of Dale closes the loop from the other side. Every draw after the first in a turn becomes two draws, and every token the deck creates is doubled. Down in the Valley chapter two turns every land into two 1/1 Elf tokens instead of one, Bard’s Company is a lord that recruits a Soldier on every attack, and Galion, Elvenking’s Butler turns whichever token you point it at into a 4/4.

Note on the set: The Hobbit releases on Arena on 2026-08-14. Arena IDs are still empty for most of the set’s printings in the card database, so the import list below takes effect from that release date.

Close 5 plus Thranduil, the Elvenking refills the hand, Bard, King of Dale doubles draws and tokens, and the deck attacks wide with a doubled Elf army or tall with one unblockable land-sized Elf

Six of the fourteen three-cost cards and both five-drops are castable earlier than the table suggests: Cantankerous Keepers has affinity for Elves and routinely costs four or five, Woodland Weavemaster taps for two or three mana in the mid game, and Wood Elves plus Thranduil’s Company mean the deck often has six or seven lands by turn five. Four-land probability by turn four is 0.632 and turn-four consistency is 0.625, both above the 0.55 threshold the build is held to.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about lands and one cheap body. Woodland Weavemaster is the priority two-drop: it blocks, it grows whenever another Elf enters, and once its power is two or three it taps for that much mana, which is what pays for the four-drop half of the deck without slowing the land count. Guardian of the Halls is the aggressive option at the same cost, and Elvenking’s Harper is the one that matters later, because its unblockable activation is an ability Thranduil, the Elvenking inherits from the graveyard.

Never trade a land drop for a spell in the first three turns. Every land is a Thranduil, Sindarin Liege token, a Thranduil’s Company counter, a Mirkwood Pathmaker point of power, and a Mirkwood Meditator trigger, so the deck’s mana base is also its threat base.

Mid game

Thranduil, Sindarin Liege on turn four is the pivot. From that point you land tokens on every land, and the whole Elf team is one point bigger, which changes every combat. Thranduil’s Company on top of it means two lands per turn: one land makes a token, and the Company’s landfall trigger adds two +1/+1 counters with vigilance.

Mirkwood Pathmaker is the card opponents consistently misjudge. It is a three-drop whose power is your land count — usually 5/5 or 6/6 by turn five in this deck — and Galion, Elvenking’s Butler can point at any other attacker and set its base power and toughness to 4/4, which turns a 1/1 Elf token into a real threat. Down in the Valley should be played before the big attack turn: chapter two turns each land into a token, and chapters three and four add +1/+0 and vigilance across the team.

The combo turn

The deck’s engine turn needs Thranduil’s Company or Thranduil, Sindarin Liege, a handful of lands, and one evasion source.

  1. Land for the turn. Thranduil, Sindarin Liege makes a 1/1 Elf token, and Thranduil’s Company’s landfall puts two +1/+1 counters on Mirkwood Pathmaker and grants it vigilance.
  2. Play a second land if Thranduil’s Company or another Elf is out. Same two triggers again: another token, another two counters.
  3. Cast Down in the Valley if it is not already on the board. Chapter two turns your next land into two Elf tokens, and with Bard, King of Dale those tokens are doubled again.
  4. Activate Elvenking’s Halls for {2}{G}{U}, sacrificing it, to put two more +1/+1 counters on the attacking Elf.
  5. Activate Elvenking’s Harper for {4}{U} to make that creature unblockable. Elrond, Moon-Reader draws you a card for the activation, and Bard the Bowman puts a counter and lifelink on a creature for that second draw.
  6. Attack with everything. Galion, Elvenking’s Butler can upgrade whichever token is smallest, and Bard’s Company gives the team +1/+1 before damage.

Two lands and one evasion activation is typically eight or more points of unblockable damage on a board that was two creatures wide one turn earlier. Order matters: make the land drops and the Halls activation before the unblockable activation, because both can change which creature should carry the attack.

Win condition 2

The second win does not need Mirkwood Pathmaker at all. Thranduil, Sindarin Liege and Down in the Valley create Elf tokens on landfall, Bard, King of Dale doubles each creation, Bard’s Company recruits a Human Soldier on every attack and attack trigger, and Galion, Elvenking’s Butler turns the largest token into a 4/4 attacker each combat. Against a deck that answers one big creature, the wide plan is the better one; against a deck with many small blockers, the tall Pathmaker plan is.

Bard, King of Dale is worth protecting with Confusticate and Bebother: while it is on the board every draw spell in the deck is worth double and every token the deck makes arrives twice. Down in the Valley’s chapters three and four giving the team vigilance is what makes the alpha strike safe, since the Elf tokens can stay back to block on the following turn.

Mulligan guide

Hand shape Verdict
Three lands, Woodland Weavemaster and any three-drop Keep. This is the deck’s ideal opening
Two lands, Wood Elves and Thranduil, Sindarin Liege Keep. Wood Elves finds the third land and the Liege converts every later land into a token
One land with a four-drop and no two-drop Mulligan. The deck needs three lands before its payoffs function
Four lands, Thranduil’s Company and a landfall three-drop Keep. Thranduil’s Company is the best card in the deck with four lands in hand
Five lands and both Bards but no Elf Mulligan. The Bard package doubles the Elf engine; it does not replace it
Two lands with a green source and one Swamp plus Thranduil, the Elvenking Keep. Black is a one-card splash, and a single Swamp plus Opulent Palace or Underground Mortuary is enough

Key interactions and edge cases

Interaction Ruling detail
Thranduil, the Elvenking and the graveyard His ability grants him all activated abilities of all Elf cards in your graveyard, so an Elvenking’s Harper that died still makes his unblockable activation available, and a Woodland Weavemaster in the graveyard gives him its mana ability
Elrond, Moon-Reader triggers once per turn Any activation of an ability of a creature draws a card, but only the first activation each turn. Pair it with the unblockable activation, not with mana abilities you would rather activate twice
Landfall needs a land, not a token Only a land entering triggers landfall. Ramp that puts a land onto the battlefield (Wood Elves, Down in the Valley chapter one) does trigger it, and Thranduil’s Company’s extra land drop is a second trigger on the same turn
Bard, King of Dale doubles draws Every draw after the first in each of your draw steps becomes two draws, so a single recruited card from Bard’s Company becomes two cards and immediately triggers Bard the Bowman’s second-draw ability
Bard, King of Dale doubles tokens Thranduil, Sindarin Liege’s 1/1 Elf token becomes two, Down in the Valley’s tokens become two each, and Bard’s Company’s Human Soldier becomes two. This is the entire wide plan, so the Bard is a removal magnet
Recruit and the discard Recruit draws a card and then discards one; if the discarded card is a nonland, you create a 1/1 white Human Soldier token. Discarding a spare land is often correct, and it feeds Thranduil, the Elvenking’s graveyard clause
Galion sets base power and toughness The change is to base values until end of turn, so it overwrites Mirkwood Pathmaker’s land-based strength if you point Galion at the Pathmaker. Point it at a token instead
Elvenking’s Halls and Lake-town are one-shot Both lands sacrifice themselves for two +1/+1 counters, and only as a sorcery. Tap them for mana first, then sacrifice in the second main phase
Key to the Side-Door The unblockable activation costs {2} and taps the artifact, so it is the cheap evasion for the turn you need it; the second ability needs a legendary card in hand with the same name as a legendary permanent you control, which happens after a second Thranduil, the Elvenking or Bard, King of Dale is drawn

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro, including fliers and lifegain counters Guardian of the Halls blocks and grows, Mirkwood Meditator switches to a 4/2 on every landfall, and Bard the Bowman’s lifelink counter offsets their clock. Race with the wide token plan and keep Confusticate and Bebother for their pump spell
Midrange The strong matchup. Thranduil, the Elvenking outdraws them, Thranduil’s Company’s landfall counters outgrow their blockers, and Galion, Elvenking’s Butler makes any token a 4/4 that trades up
Control The weak matchup. Board wipes take the tokens with them, so commit Thranduil’s Company plus one token-maker and hold the second. Cantankerous Keepers returns the Elf cards that a wipe puts into the graveyard, and Thranduil, the Elvenking’s ability-stealing clause means the Elves that died still contribute
Combo The deck’s clock is turn five or six, so interaction is minimal: Confusticate and Bebother is the only counter and Key to the Side-Door’s unblockable is the only way to push damage through a stalled board. Land Thranduil, Sindarin Liege early and attack every turn to force them to block instead of assembling

Iterations

The list was run through the viability battery twice before publishing. Round one missed the cohesion gate by half a point, so round two replaced the cards that were not feeding the engine with the highest-affinity Elf and Bard slots.
