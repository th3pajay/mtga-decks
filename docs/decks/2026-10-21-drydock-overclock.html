Overview

Station is a cost, not a bonus: tap another creature you control, put charge counters equal to its power on this Spacecraft, sorcery speed only, and it is an artifact creature at N+. Every Spacecraft and every Planet in the deck is a machine that sits there as a non-creature artifact until somebody is fed into it. That is the whole deck. The question this build answers is how to turn two or three cheap bodies into twenty counters in a single turn.

The answer is Loading Zone. For four mana it replaces every counter placement on a creature, Spacecraft or Planet you control with twice that many, and it has warp {G}, so it can come down for one green on turn two, sit in exile, and land on the turn you actually want to go off. With it in play the arithmetic stops being about tapping a big creature and starts being about cards that place counters outright.

Drill Too Deep is the cheapest of those: two mana, instant, put five charge counters on a Spacecraft or Planet. Under Loading Zone that is ten. Two of them are twenty, which is exactly Dawnsire, Sunstar Dreadnought, and Systems Override is the third copy of the effect at three mana — it untaps the hull, gives it haste, and its ten charge counters become twenty that only shrink back by ten at the end step, so ten stay for good.

The fuel is deliberately brutal. Kalakscion, Hunger Tyrant is seven power for three mana; Pugnacious Hammerskull is a 6/6 for three; Rot-Curse Rakshasa is a 5/5 trampler for two. Any one of them tapping into a Spacecraft with Loading Zone out is twelve to fourteen counters, which turns on a Planet’s 12+ ability or a Lumen-Class Frigate’s 12+ body in one activation. Without the Zone they still do the honest work of a midrange deck: attack, block, and station on the turn the ground stalls.

Sunstar Chaplain and Oreplate Pangolin are the low end of that plan — the Chaplain grows a creature on every end step with two or more tapped creatures, the Pangolin grows whenever another artifact enters, and under Loading Zone each of those counters is doubled too.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Deploy bodies and the doubler (turns two to four). Two mana threats first — Rot-Curse Rakshasa, Oreplate Pangolin, Sunstar Chaplain — and Loading Zone at the first opportunity, ideally warped in for one green so the four mana never has to be held up. Lumen-Class Frigate on two is the best bridge: it is a 3/5 wall that gives your other creatures +1/+1 from two charge counters, which makes every later station tap count for more.
  2. Station the mid-size hulls (turns four to six). Sledge-Class Seedship at three mana is the workhorse: seven counters is one 7-power creature tap or a single Drill Too Deep, after which it is a 4/5 flier that converts every attack into a free creature from hand. Galvanizing Sawship needs only three counters to become a 6/5 flying haste, so it attacks the turn it comes online.
  3. Overclock the dreadnought (turn six onwards). Dawnsire, Sunstar Dreadnought is the kill. Land it as a dead artifact, then on the following turn stack counters: two Drill Too Deep (twenty), or one Drill plus a station activation with any five-power body (twenty), or Systems Override under Loading Zone (twenty, ten of which persist). At twenty it is a 20/20 flying and its 10+ trigger deals 100 damage to a blocker on attack.

Curve and mana

Twelve cards cost two, seven cost three, four cost four, two cost five and two cost six, on twenty-four lands that include three fetch-style fixers and two Planets. Green is the deepest colour (nine cards), red is the most pip-hungry (Drill, Sawship, Kavaron), and the two double-black and double-red costs are avoided entirely so that a five-colour manabase of basics plus colourless fixing can actually cast the deck. Average mana value is 3.03; the deck wants four lands by turn four and five by turn five, and never wants more than seven.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with bodies, not with the engine. A turn-two Rot-Curse Rakshasa or Oreplate Pangolin forces the opponent to spend removal on creatures that are also your charge counter supply, and a turn-two Lumen-Class Frigate makes every subsequent station tap worth one more. Loading Zone is at its best when it is warped for one green on turn two: it costs a card but no tempo, and it comes back from exile on the turn you are ready to place twenty counters.

Do not station early unless the counters buy something. A Lumen-Class Frigate with two counters is already doing its job (+1/+1 to the team); putting a 5/5 into it for ten counters that do nothing is a waste of a body.

Mid game

This is where Sledge-Class Seedship takes over. Seven counters is a single Drill Too Deep or one 7-power tap, and the payoff is a 4/5 flier that puts a creature from hand onto the battlefield tapped and attacking every time it attacks. That is how the deck keeps a board after a sweeper: the hull survives as an artifact and the creatures arrive from hand.

Galvanizing Sawship is the tempo play. Three counters is nothing — Drill Too Deep alone is ten — and a 6/5 flying haste that comes online the turn it lands ends games that stalled on the ground.

The combo turn

The pattern to memorise: Loading Zone in play, Dawnsire on the battlefield as an artifact, and one of these:

  • Two Drill Too Deep (four mana, instant speed): ten plus ten, twenty counters, dreadnought online.
  • One Drill Too Deep plus one station activation from any creature with five or more power: ten plus ten, twenty counters.
  • One Systems Override (three mana): twenty counters placed, ten removed at the end step, ten that remain — enough to re-trigger it the following turn with a single Drill.

At twenty the Dreadnought is a 20/20 flying, and its 10+ clause deals 100 damage to up to one target creature or planeswalker whenever you attack, so a single blocker is not an answer. Rogue's Passage answers the other kind of blocker.

Win condition two

When the Dreadnought is answered (it is an artifact until it is not, so artifact removal and Gravkill-style exile effects both hit it), the deck still has a flying clock: Galvanizing Sawship at 6/5, Sledge-Class Seedship at 4/5 that keeps replacing itself, Lumen-Class Frigate at 3/5 flying lifelink, and Kavaron, Memorial World paying one red a turn to make Robots and give the team haste. Ten to fourteen points of evasive damage a turn is normal once two hulls are online.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with two lands, a two-drop and Loading Zone, or any hand with three lands and two Spacecraft. Ship hands with only one coloured source and no Evolving Wilds; five colours is real and the deck cannot afford to be colour-screwed. A hand of Dawnsire plus Drill Too Deep plus Loading Zone is a snap keep even with four lands, because the finish only needs one turn.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Station is sorcery speed only. You cannot station in response to removal, so sequence it in your first main phase before combat, and remember the tapped creature cannot block that turn.
  • Loading Zone doubles the counters from Systems Override to twenty, but the delayed trigger only removes ten — that is where the permanent ten counters come from.
  • Loading Zone also doubles +1/+1 counters on your creatures, so Sunstar Chaplain and Oreplate Pangolin grow twice as fast while it is out.
  • Station can tap a creature that is already a creature-hull: a stationed Sawship at 6/5 can feed six counters (twelve doubled) into the Dreadnought.
  • Rot-Curse Rakshasa is decayed, so it dies at end of combat if it attacks. Stationing it never triggers decayed, which is usually the better use of a 5/5.
  • Pugnacious Hammerskull gets a stun counter if it attacks without another Dinosaur, so attack with it once and then park it in a hull.
  • Kavaron, Memorial World enters tapped; it is a Station land, not a fixing land.

Matchups

  • Fast red aggro: the worst matchup. Lumen-Class Frigate and Rot-Curse Rakshasa are the early blockers; trade, then race in the air with Sawship.
  • Midrange and control: good. Hulls dodge sorcery-speed creature removal while they are artifacts, Sledge-Class Seedship rebuilds after sweepers, and the Dreadnought’s 100-damage trigger beats a single blocker.
  • Other combo decks: the deck’s clock is turn six to seven, so it usually wants to be the aggressor; Systems Override can also steal and untap an opposing artifact in a pinch.
