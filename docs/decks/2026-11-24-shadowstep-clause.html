Overview

Sneak is a cost, not a keyword ability: Sneak {cost} reads “you may cast this spell for its sneak cost if you also return an unblocked attacker you control to hand during the declare blockers step.” That single sentence is the whole deck. The list is built to guarantee three things on every attack step: an attacker that can be blocked by nobody, a hand holding a Sneak spell, and a board that gets paid when the attacker bounces back.

Prehistoric Pet is the ideal turn-one enabler. It is a 1/2 for {W} that cannot be blocked by creatures with greater power, so anything bigger than a 1/1 simply cannot stand in front of it. Splinter, Hamato Yoshi is the second: a 1/3 with menace for {1}{B} that also pumps every other Ninja by +1/+1. Splinter, Radical Rat is the card that turns the deck from cute into lethal, because it doubles every triggered ability of every Ninja you control.

The payoffs are all Ninjas, so bouncing the attacker is never a cost paid to nobody. Dark Leo & Shredder gives attacking Ninjas deathtouch, makes a 1/1 Ninja token on combat damage, and then checks the board: with five or more Ninjas the defending player loses half their life, rounded up. Splinter, Radical Rat doubles that trigger, so a single connection takes three quarters of the opponent’s life. Karai, Future of the Foot rebuys creatures from the graveyard, Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising draws cards, and The Last Ronin's Technique puts three attacking bodies on the board for two mana when its sneak cost is paid.

Every creature in the list is a Ninja, so every creature is a viable sneak attacker, a deathtouch carrier under Dark Leo, a +1/+1 recipient under Splinter, and a Turtle Lair target. That overlap is what makes a 24-land list with a three-drop curve function like a combo deck.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Turns 1-2, establish the unblockable attacker. Prehistoric Pet on one is the best opening in the deck. Splinter, Hamato Yoshi on two gives menace and a lord, and Leonardo, Cutting Edge on two is a lifelink body that starts growing the moment anything gains you life. Secluded Courtyard naming Ninja pays for essentially the entire creature base untapped, so there is no reason to fall behind on tempo here.
  2. Turns 3-4, start the sneak chain. Attack with the evasive body. In the declare blockers step, return it to hand and cast the cheap half of a Sneak spell: Shredder's Technique for {B} kills a blocker or an enchantment, Splinter's Technique for {1}{B} finds the missing combo piece, The Last Ronin's Technique for {1}{W} makes three attackers. Turtle Lair for {3} makes any Ninja unblockable, which means the sneak is available even when the opponent has a board.
  3. Turns 4-6, convert. Dark Leo & Shredder plus Splinter, Radical Rat and five Ninjas is a two-trigger kill. Without the doubler, the squad of pumped Ninjas and 1/1 tokens attacking past a Turtle Lair activation is the backup plan. Karai, Future of the Foot returning a creature directly to the battlefield when her sneak cost was paid keeps the chain going after a sweeper.

Curve and mana

The deck averages roughly 2.6 mana. Twenty-one of the thirty-six non-land cards cost three or less, and the two four-mana sorceries both have a two-mana sneak mode, so the effective curve is lower than the printed one. Twenty-four lands look generous for a tempo list, but four of them are Turtle Lair, which is a spell as much as a land: it makes the unblockable attacker that the entire engine keys on. Twenty-four lands also keeps the four-land turn-four rate at 0.64, which is the number that lets Dark Leo & Shredder plus Splinter, Radical Rat actually resolve on curve.

The colour split is deliberately shallow: white only covers Prehistoric Pet, Leonardo, The Last Ronin's Technique and the white half of the two Orzhov legends, while black carries the removal, the tutor and the card draw. Secluded Courtyard covers the creatures, so the basics lean black for the spells.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead with Prehistoric Pet. A turn-one 1/2 that only 1/1s can block is the cleanest Sneak setup in the format, and it survives because nobody wants to trade a real creature for it. Turn two is Splinter, Hamato Yoshi if you have a Sneak spell to follow, otherwise Leonardo, Cutting Edge to start the lifelink counters. Do not crack Turtle Lair for colour if you can avoid it; its {3} activation is worth more than the mana.

Mid game and the combo turn

The combo turn is any turn where you have an unblocked attacker, two to four mana, and a Ninja in hand. Sequence it like this:

  1. Move to combat and attack with the evasive body, holding priority on the declare blockers step.
  2. If the opponent has a blocker that could eat it, activate Turtle Lair for {3} before blockers are declared.
  3. In the declare blockers step, return the unblocked attacker to hand and cast the Sneak spell. Creatures cast this way enter tapped and attacking, so the attack is not lost: you trade one attacker for a better one plus the spell’s rider.
  4. Land Dark Leo & Shredder and count Ninjas. Tokens from The Last Ronin's Technique and from Dark Leo itself count, and so does every other Ninja on the board. Five is the magic number.

With Splinter, Radical Rat on the battlefield, Dark Leo & Shredder’s combat-damage trigger happens twice: create a 1/1 Ninja, check for five Ninjas and take half the opponent’s life, then do it all again. That is three quarters of their life from one connection, before any combat damage.

Win condition two

When the half-life trigger is not available, the deck wins the ordinary way. Splinter, Hamato Yoshi pumps every other Ninja, Leonardo, Cutting Edge accumulates counters off lifelink, Ninja's Blades adds +1/+1 and a loot trigger, and Turtle Lair makes the biggest carrier unblockable. The Last Ronin's Technique cast for {1}{W} off a bounced attacker adds three more bodies to the same swing, which is often twelve or more points of evasive damage across two turns.

Mulligan guide

Keep any hand with Prehistoric Pet or Splinter, Hamato Yoshi plus two lands and one Sneak spell. Keep Splinter's Technique if the rest of the hand is lands and cheap Ninjas, because the tutor finds the payoff. Ship hands with no one- or two-drop attacker: the deck has no way to start the chain from an empty board, and a hand of three-drops and lands does nothing until turn four.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Sneak is paid during the declare blockers step, so the returned creature was already declared as an attacker. Bouncing it does not undo the attack; the Sneak creature enters tapped and attacking.
  • Dark Leo & Shredder counts Ninjas, not attacking Ninjas, so tokens held back on defence still count towards five.
  • Splinter, Radical Rat doubles the trigger, not the damage: a 20-life opponent goes to 10 and then to 5.
  • Karai, Future of the Foot returns the creature to the battlefield only if her sneak cost was paid this turn; cast her at full price and she is a 3/3 that buys back a card.
  • Ninja's Blades makes the carrier a Ninja, so any stray body becomes a Turtle Lair target and a Splinter recipient.
  • Turtle Lair’s coloured mana pays only for Ninja or Turtle spells; it is not a source for Shredder's Technique.
  • Prehistoric Pet’s second ability is a backup bounce that re-triggers an enters-the-battlefield effect without attacking.

Matchups

Against midrange the deck is faster and Dark Leo’s deathtouch blanks their big blockers. Against aggro, Leonardo, Cutting Edge, lifelink and Shredder's Technique for {B} stabilise, and the half-life trigger does not care about their life total. Against control, hold Splinter's Technique for a turn where you can commit two threats; a bounced attacker is a card back in hand, not a loss. Against cheap flying blockers, activate Turtle Lair every turn.
