Overview

Grave Team Six fuses two mechanics into one engine: Fathomless descent (your graveyard’s permanent count X makes creatures huge and spells brutal) and Teamwork (tap creatures with total power N to unlock the second mode of a spell). The bridge between the two is Souls of the Lost — its power literally equals the number of permanent cards in your graveyard, which makes it the perfect creature to tap for Teamwork costs, over and over again.

Every fetchland, surveil land, looting artifact, and mill spell in the deck doubles as fuel: each permanent in the graveyard makes Souls bigger, makes Terror Tide and Chupacabra Echo wipe harder, lets Squirming Emergence reanimate bigger things, and turns Matzalantli’s back face — the land The Core — into a mana engine. Teamwork spells then convert that giant board presence into reanimation, removal, counterspells, and a 6/5 hexproof Leviathan.

Approach

Game plan phases

  1. Dig (turns 1-3). Play fetchlands and crack them, surveil with Undercity Sewers / Hedge Maze / Underground Mortuary, cast Cache Grab or Song of Stupefaction to mill, and loot with Matzalantli, the Great Door. Every permanent that hits the graveyard counts.
  2. Grow (turns 3-5). Souls of the Lost becomes a 5/6 or 6/7. Tap it (and Solemn Simulacrum or Chupacabra Echo) to pay Teamwork costs: Too Evil to Stay Dead reanimates anything, Cruel Alliance exiles any creature and gains life, We Say Thee Nay! hard-counters, Go Nuts! fights and pumps.
  3. Win (turn 5+). Reanimate with Squirming Emergence and Earth’s Mightiest Heroes, clear the board with Terror Tide (Souls survives as X+1 toughness), then kill with Rogue’s Passage plus a giant Souls, Season of Loss draining for your creature count, or the hexproof Leviathan.

Curve and mana

Average CMC is about 3.1. The deck is black-heavy with green support and a light blue touch, matching the mana base: five UB lands, five UG lands, three BG surveil lands, seven fetchlands, and five basics. Fetchlands thin the deck and always count as a permanent in the graveyard after cracking.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Prioritize a fetchland or surveil land on turn one. Turn two, either Souls of the Lost (discard a redundant land) or Song of Stupefaction / Cache Grab to start the graveyard. Do not hold back graveyard fuel — X grows fast, and every point matters for Souls, Terror Tide, and Squirming Emergence.

Mid game

With four or five permanents in the graveyard, Souls is a real threat and Teamwork costs are trivial to pay. Reanimate a Solemn Simulacrum to ramp and draw, or loop Matzalantli for card selection. Use Cruel Alliance and Chupacabra Echo to keep the opponent’s board small while your graveyard grows.

The combo turn

  1. Graveyard has 10+ permanents: Souls of the Lost is an 11/12.
  2. Tap Souls plus one other creature to cast Too Evil to Stay Dead with Teamwork — return the best creature card in your graveyard (often another Souls or Solemn).
  3. Cast Squirming Emergence (X = 10+): return Matzalantli or a creature.
  4. Attack: activate Rogue’s Passage on Souls. 11+ unblockable damage, often lethal. If they survive, Terror Tide next turn clears everything while Souls survives as X+1 toughness.

Win condition 2: Season of Loss and the Leviathan

Season of Loss with {P}{P}{P} makes each opponent lose life equal to the number of creature cards in your graveyard — commonly 8 to 12. The {P} mode forces sacrifices to clear blockers, and {P}{P} refills your hand if creatures died this turn. Atlantis Attacks (cast with Teamwork) creates a 6/5 hexproof Leviathan and bounces two permanents — a resilient finisher that dodges spot removal. Restless Reef and Restless Vinestalk close out games when the opponent is low.

Mulligan guide

Keep: two to three lands, plus any of Souls of the Lost, Cache Grab, Song of Stupefaction, Matzalantli, or a reanimation spell. A hand with no graveyard fuel and no two-drop is a mulligan. Ship hands full of six- and seven-mana spells. Lands that surveil or fetch count as fuel, so land-heavy hands are better here than in most decks.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Souls of the Lost survives your own Terror Tide: toughness is X+1, so a board wipe leaves your biggest creature standing.
  • Matzalantli transforms with four or more permanent types (artifact, creature, enchantment, land, planeswalker, battle) in the graveyard — the deck naturally has artifact (Matzalantli, fetchlands), creature, land, and enchantment types.
  • Teamwork taps as an additional cost: tap creatures before the spell resolves, so removal in response does not stop the second mode.
  • Cache Grab mills four and returns a permanent card to hand — instant speed, and it is both fuel and recursion in one card.
  • Insidious Roots: every reanimation makes a Plant, then pumps all Plants; Plants also tap for mana, creating a late-game army that pays Teamwork costs.
  • Graveyard hate (exile effects) is the deck’s main weakness: fetchlands and mill still work, but keep Cache Grab and draw spells for resilience.

Matchups

  • Aggro: Terror Tide, Chupacabra Echo, and Cruel Alliance keep the board clear; Souls blocks well. Prioritize graveyard count over speed.
  • Midrange: win the value war — reanimation loops out-grind most midrange decks; Solemn Simulacrum and Squirming Emergence provide card advantage.
  • Control: We Say Thee Nay! protects key spells; Atlantis Attacks and recursion from the graveyard are hard for control to answer permanently. Rogue’s Passage turns any surviving creature into a clock.
