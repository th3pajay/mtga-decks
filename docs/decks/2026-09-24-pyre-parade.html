Overview

Pyre Parade fuses the two mechanics rolled for today, Celebration and Firebending, into a single engine rather than running them side by side. Celebration rewards a turn in which two or more nonland permanents entered under your control; Firebending pays you red mana in combat, based on the size of the board, and that mana has to be spent before combat ends. The deck is built so each half pays the other’s bill.

Celebration is the enabler side. Dragon Fodder, Frontline Rush, Tinker’s Tote and Heroic Reinforcements each put two bodies onto the battlefield from a single card, which switches the Celebration text on by themselves: Goddric, Cloaked Reveler stops being a 3/3 haste Human and becomes a 4/4 flying Dragon, Gallant Pie-Wielder gains double strike, Grand Ball Guest becomes a 3/3 trampler, and Ash, Party Crasher grows a +1/+1 counter every attack.

Firebending is the payoff side. Sun Warriors adds one red mana per creature you control when it attacks, Firebending Student adds mana equal to its power including prowess, Azula, On the Hunt adds two, and Vindictive Warden adds one. On a wide board that is four to eight red mana per combat, spent at instant speed while the attack is still happening. Vindictive Warden turns that mana into direct damage ({3}: one damage to each opponent), Sun Warriors turns it into more Ally tokens at {5} each, Frontline Rush turns it into two Goblins mid-combat, and Silverquill Charm or Lightning Helix turns it into reach.

The fusion is what makes the deck tick. The permanents that Firebending mana buys in the middle of combat also refresh Celebration for the rest of the turn, and every artifact token among them (Treasure, Clue, Gnome, Tote) drains the opponent through Pactdoll Terror. Nothing in the list is filler: thirty-seven nonland cards split between token producers, Celebration payoffs, Firebending bodies and the sinks that spend the burst.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Deploy two-drops and pick the axis you drew into. If the opener has a two-permanent spell (Dragon Fodder, Tinker’s Tote, Heroic Reinforcements) alongside a Celebration creature, play the spell first, then attack with the pump already active. If it has Sun Warriors plus a couple of bodies, play the board first and hold the token spells for the combat step, where the Firebending mana pays for them.

Do not trade away Vindictive Warden or a pre-Celebration Goddric just to add a point of damage. The deck wins by attacking on turns four through six with a board that is two to four power larger than it looks.

Mid game and the combo turn

The signature turn looks like this:

Step Action
Main phase 1 Cast Dragon Fodder or Tinker’s Tote, then Heroic Reinforcements if you have it. Two or more nonland permanents have now entered this turn
Attackers Declare Sun Warriors, Firebending Student, Vindictive Warden, Goddric, Party Crasher. Celebration is on, so Goddric swings as a 4/4 flyer, Grand Ball Guest as a 3/3 trampler, Gallant Pie-Wielder with double strike
Firebending triggers Sun Warriors adds red equal to your creature count, each other firebender adds its own, all lasting until end of combat
Combat, instant speed Spend it: Vindictive Warden for 1 damage each at {3}, Sun Warriors for an Ally token at {5}, Frontline Rush for two more Goblins at {R}{W}, Silverquill Charm for drain 3, Lightning Helix for 3 damage
Damage and end of turn Combat damage resolves on an inflated board; every token or artifact that arrived in combat still counts, so Celebration stays live for the rest of the turn

The order matters: Firebending mana empties at end of combat, so every Vindictive Warden activation and every instant has to be spent before the damage step ends. Float first, then commit.

Win condition 2: the drain line

When the ground stalls, the deck does not need to attack to win. Pactdoll Terror drains 1 for every artifact that enters, and the deck produces artifacts constantly: Treasure from Fountainport, Clues from Azula, Gnome tokens from Tinker’s Tote, and the Tote itself. Acolyte of Aclazotz taps to convert any spare token into another drain and life. Restless Fortress animates and drains 2 per attack without costing a card. Vindictive Warden’s {3} ability converts the entire Firebending burst into direct damage, and Silverquill Charm’s third mode deals 3 on its own. Against a deck that can block all day, this is the plan: never attack into a wall, just bleed and hold Restless Fortress back as a blocker.

Mulligan guide

Keep Ship
Two or three lands plus a two-drop and a two-permanent spell (Dragon Fodder, Tinker’s Tote, Frontline Rush) Zero or one land, or five lands
Any hand with Sun Warriors and three lands: the mana burst carries the mid game All four-drops with no early bodies, unless a shock or fastland makes turn three Heroic Reinforcements certain
One-land hands with Frontline Rush and a two-drop only if the single land is a shock or fastland Hands with only payoffs and no enabler: Celebration creatures with nothing to turn them on

The deck needs 23 lands to hit its four four-drops, so a two-land hand with a realistic turn three play is fine, but a one-land hand is not.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Firebending mana expires at end of combat. It is not end-of-turn mana: any unspent red is gone before your second main phase. Sequence the drains and instants inside combat.
  • Celebration checks the whole turn. A permanent that enters during the combat step still counts, so a Clue from Azula or an Ally from Sun Warriors keeps Goddric a Dragon after the attack, and the static parts of Grand Ball Guest and Gallant Pie-Wielder stay on through the end step.
  • Celebration payoffs differ in timing. Goddric, Grand Ball Guest and Gallant Pie-Wielder are continuous, so they are live as soon as the second permanent lands. Ash, Party Crasher checks on attack, which means a Clue made by another attacker during combat does not retroactively grow him: sequence that permanent in the first main phase if you want the counter.
  • Sun Warriors counts creatures, not power. Token spells cast before combat inflate the mana burst directly, which is why Dragon Fodder and Tinker’s Tote are the best turn-three plays before a Sun Warriors attack.
  • Firebending Student pumps with prowess. Frontline Rush, Lightning Helix and Silverquill Charm all grow it, and its Firebending X equals its power at the moment it attacks, so casting an instant in the first main phase adds both damage and mana.
  • Fountainport’s Fish is a token, not an artifact. It counts for Celebration but not for Pactdoll Terror; its Treasure mode is the one that drains.
  • Acolyte of Aclazotz needs another permanent. It cannot sacrifice itself, so keep a Goblin, Fish, Clue or Tote around as fuel.
  • Restless Fortress enters tapped. Animate it only when you can also afford to keep the drain attacker profitable; a 1/4 that dies to a removal spell is worse than a land.

Matchups

Opponent Plan
Aggro (red or white weenies) Race with Grand Ball Guest and Gallant Pie-Wielder; Lightning Helix and Silverquill Charm’s exile mode trade up, and Acolyte of Aclazotz’s lifegain buys the extra turn the drain line needs
Midrange and ramp Do not overcommit into sweepers: keep a token spell in hand, rebuild two permanents in one turn, and lean on Pactdoll Terror plus Vindictive Warden drains so the clock does not depend on the board
Control Play one threat at a time until they tap out, hold Frontline Rush as a combat trick, and use Azula’s Clues and Fountainport to out-card them; Heroic Reinforcements plus Firebending mana is the burst that ends a tapped-out control turn
Combo Attack first, remove the combo piece with Lightning Helix or Silverquill Charm, and use Vindictive Warden’s menace body to keep blockers honest while the drain finishes
