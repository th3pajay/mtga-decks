Blue-Red-Green — Behold a Dragon and Treasure

Overview

Hoardmouth is built on one blunt rules interaction: Draconic Visitor replaces every artifact token you create with a 5/5 red Dragon token with flying. The deck is therefore a pile of cheap Treasure production, and each Treasure is really a Dragon once the Visitor is on the battlefield. Gold Rush, Sarkhan, Dragon Ascendant, Charming Scoundrel, Rapacious Dragon, Bonehoard Dracosaur and even The Gold Saucer all read “make a Treasure” until the Visitor rewrites them.

Behold is the connective tissue. Sarkhan wants a Dragon revealed to pay for his Treasure. Molten Exhale gets flash, Piercing Exhale adds surveil 2 and Dispelling Exhale demands {4} instead of {2} when a Dragon is beheld. Because half the deck is Dragons, the additional costs are free, and revealing a Dragon is never a cost you actually pay.

The payoff layer is Lathliss, Dragon Queen (a 5/5 token for every nontoken Dragon that enters) and Dragonstorm Globe (every Dragon enters with an extra +1/+1 counter). A single Rapacious Dragon then produces three extra bodies: its two hatching Treasures and Lathliss’s token. That is 18 power across four fliers for five mana.

Deck average mana value is 3.25 across 36 nonland cards, with 24 lands. The plan wants four lands on turn four and a Visitor by turn five, then a burst turn.

Curve and mana

The curve peaks at five: thirteen cards cost five or more, but only four lands short of half the deck produce colored mana and every spell is single-pip except Draconic Visitor and Lathliss. Mana is 11 Mountain, 6 Forest, 4 Island, plus two Maelstrom and one Gold Saucer. Red is the primary color for Visitors, Lathliss, Kiora and Molten Exhale; green covers Gold Rush and Piercing Exhale; blue is a splash for Roiling Dragonstorm and Dispelling Exhale. Sequence dual-colored needs by holding Maelstrom for the Dragon half of the deck, and never keep a hand that needs double red on turn five from a single Mountain.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one through three are about lands and a two-drop. Sarkhan is the best opening: he beholds a Dragon from hand, makes a Treasure, and turns into a 2/2 flier with a +1/+1 counter the moment your first Dragon lands. Charming Scoundrel is the flexible option, filtering on a land-heavy hand or making the Treasure outright. Hold Molten Exhale rather than firing it at the first creature; four damage at instant speed is your answer to the format’s two- and three-drops and it costs the same either way.

Mid game and the combo turn

Resolve Draconic Visitor before anything else. Everything after it is a burst turn: Gold Rush for two mana, Sarkhan’s Treasure, Rapacious Dragon’s two Treasures, Bonehoard Dracosaur’s upkeep Treasure. The correct sequence on a burst turn is Visitor, then Dragonstorm Globe if you have it, then as many Treasure sources as mana allows, because the Globe’s counter applies to the tokens as they are created. Five lands with a Visitor and two Gold Rush is 10 power of flying on turn five from two cards already on the table.

Roiling Dragonstorm is the draw engine that keeps this going. Cast it, draw two and discard the worst card, then play any Dragon and it returns to your hand. Each replay is two fresh cards for two mana, and the deck’s Dragon count means the rebuy happens almost every turn.

The combo turn in detail

Brass’s Bounty is the ceiling. With seven lands and a Visitor, it makes seven Dragons for seven mana, and with a Globe those are 8/8 fliers. That is the turn where a stalled board becomes lethal; do not fire it early for value, because without the Visitor it is only a pile of Treasures you cannot use the same turn.

Win condition two

Firespitter Whelp plus Lathliss is the grind plan. Every noncreature spell and every Dragon spell drains each opponent for one, and Lathliss makes the Dragon count climb on its own. Against decks that answer individual fliers, the drain finishes what the swarm starts. Kiora of Fire and Ashes is the long-game reset: a 5/5 on entry, then eight mana for another every turn once the board is stable.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with three or four lands, at least two mana sources of the same color as your cheapest spell, and either a two-drop or a Visitor. Ship hands that have no Dragon to behold and no way to make Treasure, because both Sarkhan and the Exhale cycle are dead without one. A hand of five lands, Draconic Visitor and Lathliss is a keep on the play; the same hand with no red source is not.

Key interactions

  • Beholding is not a sacrifice. Revealing a Dragon card from hand satisfies the cost, so Sarkhan and the Exhales never cost you a permanent.
  • Lathliss triggers only on nontoken Dragons, which here means Draconic Visitor, Rapacious Dragon and Bonehoard Dracosaur. Visitor-made 5/5s are tokens and do not multiply themselves, and Kiora’s Dragon is also a token, so it does not trigger her.
  • Dragonstorm Globe’s counter applies to Dragon tokens created after it resolves, including Visitor’s 5/5s.
  • Draconic Visitor is a replacement effect, not a trigger. It applies to the Treasure from The Gold Saucer and from any source, and it also stops Treasure from being sacrificed for mana because the Treasure is never created.
  • Piercing Exhale uses the creature’s power at resolution, so fire it after the Globe counters or a Lathliss pump for the biggest fight.
  • Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon can only pay for Dragon spells, which means the Visitor, Lathliss, Rapacious Dragon, Bonehoard Dracosaur, Kiora and Sarkhan, not for Gold Rush or the Exhales.
