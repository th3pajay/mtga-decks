Overview

Empower is the strangest mechanic in Reality Fracture: “Empower Jace N” puts N loyalty counters on a planeswalker you control, and if you do not control one it first hands you a blue Jace planeswalker token with “[-1]: Surveil 1” and “[-3]: Draw a card”. The whole cluster is built to make that token, grow it, and then keep it forever. Sanctum Lurker ({2}{B}, 3/2) is the piece that breaks the mechanic open in two directions at once: planeswalkers you control are not put into their owners’ graveyards for having 0 loyalty, and every planeswalker you control gains “[+2]: This planeswalker deals 1 damage to each opponent and you gain 1 life.”

That second line is the deck. A Jace token is normally a bad cantrip engine. Under Lurker it is a free, indestructible-by-loyalty repeating drain: it never dies to its own loyalty count, and every turn it can pay +2 to hit each opponent for 1 and gain you 1. Two tokens is 2 damage a turn, four is 4, and the tokens keep climbing in loyalty, so the drain never turns off.

The rest of the list is the machinery that keeps producing loyalty. Repurposed Enforcer ({1}{W}, 3/2) empowers Jace X on every attack, where X is the number of creatures you control — on turn four with three other bodies that is four loyalty a combat, every combat. Mindseeker Oculus ({2}{U}, 2/1) and Keeper of the Quiet Hour ({3}, 3/2 artifact) empower 4 and 2 as they enter, which means the tokens arrive already tall. Academic Ascent ({1}{W}) is a combat trick and two loyalty for two mana. Theorist’s Sanctum makes more of them from a land slot, and Hexhaven Battalion makes three bodies and two loyalty at once.

The deck never needs a printed planeswalker card. Eighteen empower sources across eighteen slots make the same blue token, and Sanctum Lurker makes that token a win condition.

Approach

Game plan phases

Turns one to three — establish the token. Land, then the cheapest empower body: Repurposed Enforcer, Keeper of the Quiet Hour, Teyo, Lightshield Expert to protect what you have. There is no turn-one play, so the opening is a land and a two drop. The first empower resolves on turn three or four and puts a Jace token on the battlefield with two to four loyalty.

Turns four to six — resolve Lurker and turn the engine on. Sanctum Lurker is the only card that must resolve. Once it does, every Jace token you already own stops being expendable and becomes a drain piece. From that point the plan is arithmetic: add a token a turn, activate +2 on each of them, and keep the board from being overrun with Vindictive Triumph and Silence the Echo.

Turns six and later — close with bodies. The drain alone is slow against a deck that gains life, so the ground game matters. Captain America and Winter Soldier attack through most boards, Thor blinks the empower creatures to re-trigger them, and Cadets under Gideon’s Memorial are 2/1 vigilance blockers that also swing.

Curve and mana

Twenty-three lands support a curve that is almost entirely two and three drops: sixteen cards at two mana, seventeen at three, two at four and two at six. The average mana value of the non-land cards is 2.8. White is the primary colour with eighteen non-land cards, black contributes twelve, and blue is a light splash at nine cards carried by four Islands and three Theorist’s Sanctum. Thin blue is deliberate: everything blue in the deck is either colourless-friendly to cast late or worth waiting for.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead on a land and hold the two drop. Repurposed Enforcer is the best turn two because it starts the loyalty clock a turn early; Keeper of the Quiet Hour is the more resilient choice against red removal because the body is colourless. Do not cast Academic Ascent on an empty board — the card is worth four damage and two loyalty when it protects a creature that is already attacking, and worth almost nothing as a pump on nothing.

The first empower resolves on turn three: a Jace token appears with two to four loyalty. Treat it as a resource, not a threat. It surveils with [-1] when you have nothing better to do, draws cards with [-3] when you are flooded, and exists to soak an attack that would otherwise hit you.

The combo turn

The turn the deck takes over is the turn Sanctum Lurker lands while a token is already out.

  1. Attack with Repurposed Enforcer and everything else. The attack trigger is one empower of X, where X is your creature count.
  2. Put those loyalty counters on the Jace token. With four creatures that is a token sitting on four or more loyalty.
  3. Activate +2 on the token: one damage to each opponent, one life to you, and the token climbs instead of shrinking.
  4. Cast Academic Ascent in the second main phase for two more loyalty, or use Theorist’s Sanctum on the following turn.

From then on every token on the board drains every turn, and the tokens no longer die at zero loyalty. Two tokens plus a Thor attack that blinks Mindseeker Oculus is four damage a turn to each opponent from a board that looks like a mediocre creature deck.

Win condition two

The drain is not fast enough alone, so the deck also attacks. Captain America is a 3/4 vigilance body that never taps out, Winter Soldier attacks with menace and vigilance, and Thor is a 3/3 flier. Hexhaven Battalion adds three 2/2 Cadets and Gideon’s Memorial makes them 2/1 vigilance attackers, so a stalled board turns into eight or ten power of evasion-free pressure. Restless Fortress is the last two points: attack with it for {2}{W}{B} and the defending player loses 2 while you gain 2.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two to four lands, one two-drop empower creature and one three-drop. Repurposed Enforcer plus any three-drop is a keep; two creature-light hands with only answers are not. Sanctum Lurker is worth keeping in an opening hand, but it is not worth keeping a two-land hand for. A one-land hand with Academic Ascent and two three-drops is a mulligan: the deck has no turn-one play and cannot function on one land.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Zero loyalty is not death. With Lurker out, a Jace token at 0 loyalty stays on the battlefield and can still activate +2 to climb back to 2. Do not trade tokens away just because they are small.
  • Sacrifice is not destruction. Silence the Echo’s additional cost can be paid by sacrificing a Jace token; a 0-loyalty token that Lurker is protecting is a legal sacrifice and often the cheapest removal spell in the deck.
  • Thor re-triggers the engine. The Mighty Thor exiles a creature on attack and returns it tapped. Target Mindseeker Oculus for four fresh loyalty every combat, or Keeper of the Quiet Hour for two. Never blink the only creature you have if the board is racing.
  • Vindictive Triumph is a re-trigger too. If its target has mana value 3 or less it comes back under your control tapped, which re-triggers an enters-the-battlefield empower. Casting it on your own Keeper of the Quiet Hour is a legal, if inefficient, line.
  • Hexhaven Battalion cycles. Basic landcycling {2} for a basic land is the correct play when stuck on two lands, and the Cadets are only worth six mana when you are already ahead on board.
  • Theorist’s Sanctum timing. Play it after a Jace token exists so it enters untapped; playing it first means a land that arrives tapped in a deck that cannot afford to fall behind on tempo.
  • Gideon’s Memorial is an anthem first. The {T} mana is restricted to planeswalker spells and this deck has none, so use the artifact for the +1/+0 and for the {1}{W} discard ability that kills an attacker.

Matchups

Against red aggro the deck wants Keeper of the Quiet Hour and Teyo, Lightshield Expert early, and Vindictive Triumph on their best two drop. Silence the Echo is a two-mana answer as long as a token is available to sacrifice. The matchup is close; the drain races only once Lurker is down and two planeswalkers exist.

Against control the plan is to run out one token at a time and force them to spend answers on 2/1s. Sanctum Lurker is the card that must be protected, so hold Teyo, Lightshield Expert for it. Hexhaven Battalion is the post-wrath reload.

Against midrange the deck is faster to a stable board and the drain punishes stalls. Vindictive Triumph on their three drop is usually the tempo swing that decides the game.

Against combo there is no interaction for the stack beyond two sorcery-speed answers, so the race is on: attack with everything, keep the drain running, and use Silence the Echo on the piece that matters.
