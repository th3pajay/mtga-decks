Sultai descend aggro — Akawalli, the Seething Tower and Souls of the Lost

Overview

Understory is built on descend: the deck wants permanent cards in the graveyard, and it wants them there early. Descend appears on the cards in two shapes, and the deck uses both.

  • Descend thresholds — a static “if there are four or more permanent cards in your graveyard” clause sits on Basking Capybara, Echo of Dusk, Akawalli and Join the Dead. Descend 8 upgrades Akawalli again and unlocks every mode of Wail of the Forgotten.
  • Descend events — The Mycotyrant counts the number of times you descended this turn and makes that many 1/1 Fungus tokens at end of turn. Every milled creature and every milled land is one descend.

Because the engine counts permanents rather than cards, the deck is almost entirely permanents: 30 creatures and 23 lands against only 7 spells, and three of those spells mill. A single Deathcap Marionette, Rubblebelt Maverick or Quag Feast commonly bins two to four permanents at once, which switches on descend 4 for the rest of the game.

The payoff structure is deliberately redundant. Basking Capybara becomes a 4/3 for two mana, Echo of Dusk a 3/3 lifelinker, and Souls of the Lost a Spirit whose power is literally the size of your graveyard. Akawalli is the ceiling: a 3/3 for three that becomes a 7/7 trampler which can’t be blocked by more than one creature once eight permanents are down. Rogue’s Passage then removes the last blocker from the equation.

Approach

Game plan in three phases

  1. Stock the yard (turns 1–3). Lead with one- and two-drop bodies that mill on the way in: Rubblebelt Maverick surveils 2, Deathcap Marionette mills 2, and the surveil dual lands add a card each. Do not trade creatures if a block would cost you a descend body — the yard is the resource, so a creature that dies is not a lost card.
  2. Switch on the thresholds (turns 3–5). Four permanents in the yard is the first real checkpoint: Capybara, Echo of Dusk, Akawalli and Join the Dead all flip at that point. Eight is the second: Akawalli gains another +2/+2 and the “can’t be blocked by more than one creature” clause, and Wail of the Forgotten becomes a three-mode spell. Quag Feast is live removal in this window because it counts all cards in the graveyard, not just permanents.
  3. Close (turns 5+). Attack with Akawalli and Champion of Dusan, use Rogue’s Passage on the biggest body, and keep re-triggering descend each turn so The Mycotyrant keeps producing Fungus tokens. Souls of the Lost is the flex threat: it is as large as the yard, so it doubles as both a clock and a blocker.

Curve and mana

23 lands: 4 Underground Mortuary, 4 Hedge Maze, 2 Undercity Sewers, 1 Rogue’s Passage, 4 Forest, 4 Swamp, 4 Island.

The colored requirements are cheap and mostly single-pipped. Black is the deepest color (Deathcap Marionette, Echo of Dusk, Souls of the Lost, Quag Feast, Join the Dead) and green supplies the bodies (Maverick, Capybara, Skrull, Ochu, Champion of Dusan). Blue is a splash for a single Wail of the Forgotten, so the two blue sources on the duals plus four Islands are ample, and Undercover Skrull converts any green body into fixing.

The nonland curve is 4 one-drops, 22 two-drops and 11 three-drops, with nothing above three mana. Twenty-three lands are viable precisely because every spell is castable off two or three lands.

Gameplay strategy

Early game (turns 1–3)

  • Turn 1: Rubblebelt Maverick (surveil 2, bin a land or a creature) or Gran Pulse Ochu as a deathtouch roadblock.
  • Turn 2: Deathcap Marionette, which mills two on entry, or Undercover Skrull as a body that fixes your colors. Against aggro, hold the deathtouch body back as a blocker rather than attacking into a trade.
  • Turn 3: Akawalli or The Mycotyrant. Akawalli is the better clock; Mycotyrant is the better engine once you can mill on multiple turns.
  • Play surveil duals on curve when the tapped land does not cost you a play — the surveil is a real card, and every permanent you bin counts for descend.

Mid game and the descend turn

The deck’s “combo turn” is any turn where you can put two or more permanents into the graveyard before combat:

  1. Mill with Deathcap Marionette, Rubblebelt Maverick or Quag Feast, and surveil with a dual land.
  2. Count the yard. Four permanents flips Capybara, Echo of Dusk, Akawalli and Join the Dead. Eight flips Akawalli’s second clause and Wail of the Forgotten.
  3. Cast Souls of the Lost with its additional cost (discard a card or sacrifice a permanent) — that cost is itself a descend trigger, so it both grows Souls and adds a Fungus token from Mycotyrant.
  4. Attack. With Akawalli at seven power, activate Rogue’s Passage and send it through alone if the opponent has a single large blocker.

Win condition 2: the Fungus swarm

The Mycotyrant does not care about thresholds — it counts descend events. Each permanent card that hits the graveyard in a turn is one descend, so a mill 4 plus a surveil 2 is up to six Fungus tokens at end of turn. Its power and toughness equal the number of Fungi you control, so the token engine also makes the Tyrant itself lethal. Champion of Dusan and Souls of the Lost are the trample and stats that push the swarm through; Rogue’s Passage can carry any single Fungus past a stalled board.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep: two or three lands including black or green, at least one mill or surveil permanent, and a two-drop.
  • Keep (aggressive): Maverick into Marionette into Akawalli, even with a single land type, because the curve tops out at three.
  • Ship: hands with no permanent that mills, hands of only three-drops, and one-land hands without a one-drop.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Descend counts permanents, not cards. Instants and sorceries in the yard never help a threshold. That is why the spell count is only seven.
  • The additional cost on Souls of the Lost is a feature — discarding a card or sacrificing a permanent triggers descend and feeds The Mycotyrant.
  • Quag Feast checks all cards in the graveyard, not just permanents, so it kills larger targets than the descend thresholds would suggest.
  • Wail of the Forgotten needs eight permanents at the moment you cast it — it is a late-game card, not a turn-two play.
  • Undercover Skrull needs two creature cards in the yard to be a 3/3; it is a 1/1 mana dork before that, so do not attack with it early.
  • Akawalli’s second clause is not unblockable — it can’t be blocked by more than one creature, so a single chump blocker still stops it. Rogue’s Passage is the answer to that.
  • Champion of Dusan renews from the graveyard — after it dies, {1}{G} from the yard puts a +1/+1 and a trample counter on your best attacker, which is often Akawalli.

Matchups

  • Aggro: you have the better blockers. Deathcap Marionette and Gran Pulse Ochu trade up with deathtouch, Echo of Dusk gains life at descend 4, and Join the Dead is a two-for-one tempo swing. Do not race with Souls of the Lost if a block is available.
  • Control: the curve is the weapon — 22 two-drops means you can present two threats a turn and rebuild after a sweeper. Rogue’s Passage beats a single large blocker, and The Mycotyrant’s tokens survive most one-for-one removal.
  • Graveyard hate: Rest in Peace effects turn off every threshold at once. The deck still functions as a low-curve aggro deck (Skrull 3/3, Champion 4/2 trample, Capybara 1/3) but the reach shrinks; answer the enchantment with Wail of the Forgotten if you can.
  • Ramp and midrange: you are the beatdown. Count to eight permanents as fast as possible, then use Rogue’s Passage to ignore their oversized blocker.
