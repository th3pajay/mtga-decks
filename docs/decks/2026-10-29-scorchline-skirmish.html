Scorchline Skirmish

Jeskai haste-trample pressure: pump spells that draw cards, and a land that adds two attackers — Reality Fracture

Overview

Reality Fracture’s red half is the most direct thing in the set: creatures that convert mana into damage on the turn they arrive. Frostbite Pyromental ({U}{R}{R}, 4/4, trample, haste) is the engine. It attacks the turn it lands, draws two cards whenever it connects, and dies at the beginning of the end step — so it is a spell with a body, not a permanent. Curse-Marred Demon ({2}{R}{R}, 4/4, flying, trample) is the reload: it fetches a card on entry and discards one at random, which is exactly the kind of selection a deck full of redundant pumps wants.

Everything else in the deck exists to make those two clocks connect one turn earlier or one damage larger. Blazing Crescendo ({1}{R}) is +3/+1 for two mana and exiles the top card of your library, playable until the end of your next turn: on a Pyromental it turns 4 trample damage into 7 and replaces itself twice. Charge the Sanctum ({2}{R/W}) is the wide version — creatures you control get +2/+0 — and it is the card that cashes in Dalkovan Encampment, a land that creates two attacking 1/1 Warriors for {2}{W} whenever you attack.

The white and blue cards are the reason the deck does not fold to a single blocker. Surgical Precision answers anything with four or more toughness or draws a card for two mana; Memory Trap exiles a nonland permanent for as long as it stays; Thalia, the Survivor trades profitably with lifelink and taxes opposing noncreature spells; Lyra, Archangel of Dawn is a flying body that grows every time the deck gains life.

Approach

Game plan phases

Turns 1–2: set the table. Land, then a two-drop. Skilled Battlecarver ({1}{R}, 2/1, first strike on your turn) or Samut, Tyrant of Naktamun ({1}{U}, 2/1) are the body; Surgical Precision or Memory Trap is the correct play when the opponent leads with something that will otherwise eat your attack step. Do not tap out for a two-drop if it means tapping the only red source you need for a Pyromental next turn.

Turns 3–5: first clock. Pyromental or Demon lands and attacks immediately. Hold Blazing Crescendo until the attack is declared: +3/+1 is worth more after blockers are chosen, and the Pyromental’s damage trigger draws two cards regardless of what happens to the creature afterwards.

Turns 5+: second clock and reach. Dalkovan Encampment turns a land into two attackers; Charge the Sanctum then adds +2/+0 to every attacker including the Warriors. This is the turn that converts a stalled board into a lethal one, and it is also the reason the deck plays 24 lands rather than 22.

Curve and mana

The deck curves at 2.83 average mana value: thirteen two-drops, sixteen three-drops, seven four-drops, nothing above four. That shape is deliberate — the Pyromental is a three-drop that dies at end of turn, so every turn you are not casting a threat is a turn the deck’s best card does nothing.

Coloured sources: sixteen red (Vantage, Sundown Pass, Stormcarved Coast, Mountain), eight blue (Stormcarved Coast, Island) and twelve white (Vantage, Sundown Pass, Dalkovan Encampment, Plains). The blue count is the tightest and the one that matters most: Pyromental needs one blue and two red, so hands without a blue source should not keep a Pyromental as their only threat.

In goldfish testing the list deploys a threat on turn four in 63 percent of games and reaches four lands by turn four in 62 percent, which is the consistency this curve needs rather than a perfect one: the deck wins by attacking twice, not by hitting every drop.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Lead on a two-drop and hold interaction. Skilled Battlecarver is the best turn-two play because first strike on your turn makes it a real blocker against other small creatures, and its {1}{R} activation lets it attack into a 2/2 later. Divining Duelist is the flexible one: flash means you can hold up two mana and either loot at the end of their turn or ambush a one-toughness attacker.

Mid game

The turn-three decision defines the game: Pyromental (attack now, draw two, lose it) or hold up Surgical Precision. Against decks that can block a 4/4, cast the Pyromental only when a pump spell is available; a 7/5 trampler that draws two cards is a two-for-one, while a 4/4 that gets blocked by a 5/5 is a one-for-one that also costs you the card.

Curse-Marred Demon is the follow-up. It is the only card in the deck that finds exactly what you need — the fourth land, a pump spell, or the Memory Trap — at the cost of a random discard, and its flying trample body is the deck’s most reliable way to push the last points through a ground stall.

The combo turn

The deck’s “combo” is a single combat step that stacks four effects:

  1. Dalkovan Encampment activation ({2}{W}, {T}) during the precombat main phase, before attackers are declared.
  2. Declare every attacker, including the two 1/1 Warriors that arrive tapped and attacking.
  3. Charge the Sanctum for {2}{R/W} in the team mode: every attacker gets +2/+0.
  4. Blazing Crescendo on the largest trampler, ideally the Pyromental, for +3/+1 and an extra card.

With Pyromental, Demon and two Warriors that is five attackers; +2/+0 across the board is ten extra damage in one combat, and the Crescendo adds three more on the trampler. Because Samut gives your instants split second, the opponent cannot respond to the pump with a trick of their own.

Second win condition

If the red clock is answered, the deck goes wide instead of tall. Dalkovan Encampment is a land, so it survives sorcery-speed sweepers that kill the board, and it re-arms every turn: two Warriors per combat, each of them a legal target for Charge the Sanctum. Thalia’s lifelink and Lyra’s flying body buy the turns this plan needs, and Lyra grows permanently off every lifelink attack, which turns a stalled race into a one-sided one.

Mulligan guide

  • Keep two or three lands with at least one red source and a two- or three-drop.
  • Keep hands with Pyromental only when they also have blue and two red sources; a Pyromental you cannot cast is a mulligan.
  • One-land hands with no two-drop go back, even with multiple three-drops — the deck has 24 lands and cannot function on one.
  • Dalkovan Encampment plus a Mountain is a legitimate keep: the land is a spell in this deck.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Pyromental is sacrificed at the beginning of the end step whether or not it attacked. Cast it when it can attack, block, or bait removal; never as a defensive afterthought.
  • Its damage trigger draws two cards even if the Pyromental dies in the same combat. Pump it, let it connect, then let it die.
  • Blazing Crescendo’s exiled card is playable until the end of your next turn, so a Crescendo cast on your turn can set up an instant on theirs.
  • Charge the Sanctum is modal: the team mode is +2/+0 only, while the single-target mode adds first strike and a +1/+1 counter. Use the single mode when one evasive attacker needs to survive a block.
  • Dalkovan Encampment’s Warriors are sacrificed at the beginning of the next end step and arrive tapped and attacking, so they cannot block. Activate it in the precombat main phase of the turn you intend to win.
  • Memory Trap exiles the permanent only while the enchantment stays; destroying your own Memory Trap is a legal way to give the permanent back, which is never what you want.
  • Surgical Precision is dead against creatures with three or less toughness; take the draw-two-life mode rather than boarding it out, because the deck has few other ways to dig.
  • Thalia’s tax hits only your opponents. Your own Blazing Crescendo and Charge the Sanctum cost normal price with her on the battlefield, so there is never a reason to hold a pump spell because of your own Thalia.

Matchups

Against aggro: Surgical Precision and Memory Trap are the whole game; Thalia’s 3/4 lifelink body blocks almost everything at three mana. Race only when the Pyromental is already in hand.

Against control: Samut’s split second makes the pump spells uncounterable and unrespondable, and Thalia taxes their interaction. Do not commit more than two threats at a time into a sweeper; Dalkovan Encampment keeps producing attackers after the board is wiped.

Against ramp: the deck’s clock is faster than theirs, but only if the Pyromental attacks. Blazing Crescendo on turn three is often correct even without a second threat, because the two cards it draws are the follow-up.
