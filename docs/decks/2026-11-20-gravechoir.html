At a glance

Format Standard (60 cards)
Colors Blue, Black, Red, Green
Archetype Graveyard midrange with a recursion engine
Size 60 mainboard, no sideboard
Primary win Scion of the Deep tokens and unblockable Threshold creatures, forced through with Rogue’s Passage
Secondary win Harmonize reach: Channeled Dragonfire, Glacial Dragonhunt and hasty Wild Ride cast a second time out of the graveyard

Overview

Gravechoir is built on one overlap: both Threshold and Harmonize read the graveyard, but they read it in different directions. Threshold wants seven or more cards in the bin so its creatures switch on. Harmonize wants spells in the bin so they can be cast again from there, with a creature tapped to shave the generic cost by its power.

Fill the graveyard and every permanent in the deck improves. Then tap what improved to pay for the second copy of every spell. A surveilled land, a milled spell and a discarded creature are not losses here; they are the fuel that turns a 2/2 into an unblockable evasive threat and the fuel that turns a one-mana burn spell into a repeatable finisher.

The deck’s engine, in order: surveil and loot to reach seven cards, Threshold creatures to convert that count into evasion and power, then Harmonize spells to convert that power back into cards and damage. Kiora, the Rising Tide is the hinge, because Scion of the Deep is both the biggest body in the deck and the biggest Harmonize discount in the deck.

The deck is functionally a blue-black deck with a red-green spell package: do not keep a hand that needs green mana on turn three without a Forest, a fetch or Roamer’s Routine.

Gameplay strategy

Early game

Turns one and two are about stock, not pressure. Any surveil or loot effect is worth more than a vanilla body, because the first seven cards in the graveyard are worth roughly a card of value across the whole deck. Glacial Dragonhunt on turn two is the ideal opener: it replaces itself, and if you discard a nonland it kills a two-toughness threat. Thought Shucker is the patient alternative, since it blocks and then converts into a card once Threshold is live.

Do not trade creatures early unless the trade fills the graveyard. Every creature that dies is a Threshold counter, but every creature that dies also removes a body you may want to tap for Harmonize later.

Mid game and the combo turn

The deck’s characteristic turn is five or six mana: attack with a Threshold board, then in the second main phase tap a large creature to cast a Harmonize spell out of the graveyard. The math is the point.

  • Mammoth Bellow from hand costs {2}{G}{U}{R} and leaves a 5/5. From the graveyard it costs {5}{G}{U}{R} minus the tapped creature’s power, so a 5/5 Elephant pays its own five generic.
  • Channeled Dragonfire from the graveyard is {5}{R}{R} minus power. With a Scion of the Deep on board it is {R}{R} for two damage, and the Scion is still untapped for blocks if you attack first.
  • Winternight Stories from the graveyard is {4}{U} minus power, which is often one blue mana for draw three, discard two.
  • Wild Ride from the graveyard is {4}{R} minus power: one red for three power and haste on an unblocked attacker.

A six-power creature therefore reduces any Harmonize cost to its colored pips. Scion of the Deep at eight power makes every Harmonize spell effectively free on the generic half.

Win condition 1: the passage

Kiora attacks at Threshold, makes an 8/8, and the Scion attacks the following turn. Two Scions is already sixteen power. Rogue’s Passage makes the biggest one unblockable for four mana, which converts a stalled board into a two-turn clock, and it works equally well on Cephalid Inkmage and Nightwhorl Hermit, which are unblockable on their own at Threshold but still need a way past a huge blocker when the game goes long. Mind Drill Assailant and Crypt Feaster are the budget versions of the same plan: menacing or oversized attackers that only need one lane cleared.

Win condition 2: the second cast

Every Harmonize spell is a two-for-one by construction. Channeled Dragonfire deals two, then two more from the bin. Glacial Dragonhunt kills a creature, then kills another and loots again. Mammoth Bellow makes a 5/5, then a second 5/5 that taps to pay for the next spell. Against control, the sequence is to bait a counterspell with the first cast and win with the second, since the graveyard copy is uncounterable in practice only if you resolve the tap discount first.

Mulligan guide

Keep hands with two or three lands and at least one surveil or loot effect. Keep any hand containing Cephalid Inkmage plus a second blue source. Ship hands with no graveyard filler, because the deck’s creatures are all below rate until Threshold is live. One-land hands are unkeepable even with Evolving Wilds, since the deck has no zero-cost cyclers. Five-land hands are keepable if two of the lands surveil.

Key interactions and edge cases

  • Threshold counts only your graveyard. Seven cards, not seven creatures.
  • Harmonize exiles the spell after it resolves, so each spell is castable exactly twice.
  • Tapping a creature for Harmonize removes it from combat. Attack first, then tap in the second main phase, unless the discount is what lets you cast at all.
  • Roamer’s Routine from the graveyard can find a basic land, which fixes a color mid-combo.
  • Cavern of Souls names Rat, covering five of the deck’s creatures and making the Kiora curve uncounterable.
  • Kiora is legendary: a second copy in hand is a discard outlet for Winternight Stories, not a dead card.

Matchups
